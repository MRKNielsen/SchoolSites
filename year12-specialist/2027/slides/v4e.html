Three points on one line, and points that divide a segment

Collinear points

Collinear

Three or more points are collinear if they lie on a single straight line.

Test

\(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are collinear exactly when \(\overrightarrow{AC} = k\,\overrightarrow{AB}\) for some real \(k\). The vectors are parallel and share the point \(A\).

Equivalent form

\(\overrightarrow{OC} = (1 - k)\,\overrightarrow{OA} + k\,\overrightarrow{OB}\). The coefficients add to 1.

Parallel vectors with a common point

Dividing a segment

Ratio

If \(P\) lies on \(AB\) with \(AP : PB = m : n\), then \[\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + \frac{m}{m+n}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \frac{n\mathbf{a} + m\mathbf{b}}{m + n}\]

Quick check

Midpoint: \(m = n = 1\) gives \(\tfrac12(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\).

Writing it up

Say both things: the vectors are parallel, and they have a point in common.

I do Showing points are collinear

Example 1

Show that \(A(1, 2, -1)\), \(B(3, 5, 1)\) and \(C(7, 11, 5)\) are collinear, and find \(AB : BC\).

  1. 1 \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}\)
  2. 2 \(\overrightarrow{BC} = 4\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} + 4\mathbf{k} = 2\,\overrightarrow{AB}\)
  3. 3 \(\overrightarrow{BC}\) is parallel to \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(B\) is common to both.
  4. ✓ \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) are collinear and \(AB : BC = 1 : 2\)

Your turn, with me

Show that \(A(2, -1, 4)\), \(B(4, 3, 2)\) and \(C(5, 5, 1)\) are collinear, and find \(AB : BC\).

  1. 1 \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} - 2\mathbf{k}\)
  2. 2 \(\overrightarrow{BC} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k}\), so \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\,\overrightarrow{BC}\)
  3. 3 Parallel, with \(B\) in common.
  4. ✓ Collinear, and \(AB : BC = 2 : 1\)

I do Unknown coordinates

Example 2

The points \(A(1, -2, 3)\), \(B(3, 2, -1)\) and \(C(m, 8, n)\) are collinear. Find \(m\) and \(n\).

  1. 1 \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} - 4\mathbf{k}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC} = (m - 1)\mathbf{i} + 10\mathbf{j} + (n - 3)\mathbf{k}\)
  2. 2 \(\overrightarrow{AC} = k\,\overrightarrow{AB}\). From \(\mathbf{j}\): \(10 = 4k\), so \(k = \tfrac52\)
  3. 3 \(m - 1 = 5\) and \(n - 3 = -10\)
  4. ✓ \(m = 6,\ n = -7\)

We do Unknown coordinates

Your turn, with me

The points \(A(2, 1, 0)\), \(B(5, -1, 3)\) and \(C(-4, p, q)\) are collinear. Find \(p\) and \(q\).

  1. 1 \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC} = -6\mathbf{i} + (p - 1)\mathbf{j} + q\mathbf{k}\)
  2. 2 From \(\mathbf{i}\): \(-6 = 3k\), so \(k = -2\)
  3. 3 \(p - 1 = 4\) and \(q = -6\)
  4. ✓ \(p = 5,\ q = -6\)

I do Where two lines cross Exam style

Example 3

In triangle \(OAB\), \(\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}\). \(M\) is the midpoint of \(OA\), and \(N\) lies on \(OB\) with \(ON : NB = 2 : 1\). The lines \(AN\) and \(BM\) meet at \(P\). Find \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\).

  1. 1 \(P\) on \(AN\): \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + s\left(\tfrac23\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}\right) = (1 - s)\mathbf{a} + \tfrac23 s\,\mathbf{b}\)
  2. 2 \(P\) on \(BM\): \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{b} + t\left(\tfrac12\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\right) = \tfrac12 t\,\mathbf{a} + (1 - t)\mathbf{b}\)
  3. 3 \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\) not parallel: \(1 - s = \tfrac12 t\) and \(\tfrac23 s = 1 - t\)
  4. 4 Solving: \(s = \tfrac34,\ t = \tfrac12\)
  5. ✓ \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \tfrac14\mathbf{a} + \tfrac12\mathbf{b}\)
Method

Write \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) two ways, one for each line, then equate coefficients.

We do Where two lines cross Exam style

Your turn, with me

In triangle \(OAB\), \(\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}\). \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), and \(N\) lies on \(OA\) with \(ON : NA = 1 : 2\). The lines \(OM\) and \(BN\) meet at \(P\). Find \(\overrightarrow{OP}\), and the ratio \(OP : PM\).

  1. 1 \(P\) on \(OM\): \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \tfrac12 s(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\)
  2. 2 \(P\) on \(BN\): \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{b} + t\left(\tfrac13\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\right) = \tfrac13 t\,\mathbf{a} + (1 - t)\mathbf{b}\)
  3. 3 \(\tfrac12 s = \tfrac13 t\) and \(\tfrac12 s = 1 - t\), so \(t = \tfrac34,\ s = \tfrac12\)
  4. ✓ \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \tfrac14\mathbf{a} + \tfrac14\mathbf{b}\), and \(OP : PM = 1 : 1\)

Are \((0, 1, 2)\), \((2, 0, 5)\) and \((6, -2, 11)\) collinear?

Yes: \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}\) and \(\overrightarrow{BC} = 2\,\overrightarrow{AB}\)

\(A(1, 1, 1)\) and \(B(6, -4, 11)\). Find \(P\) on \(AB\) with \(AP : PB = 2 : 3\).

\(\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac25\overrightarrow{AB}\), so \(P(3, -1, 5)\)

Find \(k\) if \((1, 2, 3)\), \((2, 4, k)\) and \((4, 8, 9)\) are collinear.

\(\overrightarrow{AC} = 3\,\overrightarrow{AB}\), so \(3(k - 3) = 6\) and \(k = 5\)
