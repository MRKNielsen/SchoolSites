Intersection of lines and skew lines

Parallel, intersecting or skew, and the angle between lines

VCE Specialist Mathematics 3&4 · Term 1, 2027

Chapter 5 · Section 5B

By the end of this section you can…

  • Decide whether two lines in 3D are parallel, intersecting or skew
  • Find the point of intersection of two lines
  • Find the acute angle between two lines

Textbook: Cambridge Specialist Mathematics 3&4, Section 5B

Two lines in three dimensions

CaseDirectionsCommon point?
Parallel (or the same line)\(\mathbf{d}_1 = k\mathbf{d}_2\)None, or every point
IntersectingNot parallelExactly one
SkewNot parallelNone
New in 3D

In a plane, lines that are not parallel must meet. In space they can miss each other: these are skew lines.

Skew lines lie in parallel planes

Method: do the lines meet?

  • Use a different parameter for each line, say \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\)
  • Equate the \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) coordinates: three equations, two unknowns
  • Solve two of the equations
  • Check the third. If it holds, the lines meet. If not, and the directions are not parallel, the lines are skew
Angle between lines

Use the direction vectors, and take the acute angle: \[\cos\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{d}_1\cdot\mathbf{d}_2|}{|\mathbf{d}_1|\,|\mathbf{d}_2|}\]

I do Lines that meet

Example 1

Show that the lines \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + \lambda(\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + \mu(\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\) intersect, and find the point of intersection.

  1. 1 \(x\): \(1 + \lambda = 2 + \mu\)   \(y\): \(2 + \lambda = 6 - 2\mu\)   \(z\): \(2\lambda = 3 + \mu\)
  2. 2 From \(x\): \(\lambda = 1 + \mu\). Into \(y\): \(3 + \mu = 6 - 2\mu\), so \(\mu = 1,\ \lambda = 2\)
  3. 3 Check \(z\): \(2(2) = 4\) and \(3 + 1 = 4\) ✓
  4. ✓ The lines intersect at \((3, 4, 4)\).
Marks

The check of the third equation is where the mark for "show that" is earned.

We do Lines that meet

Your turn, with me

Show that the lines \(\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + \lambda(\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = 3\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j} - 3\mathbf{k} + \mu(2\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k})\) intersect, and find the point of intersection.

  1. 1 \(2 + \lambda = 3 + 2\mu\),   \(-1 + 2\lambda = 7 - 2\mu\),   \(3 - \lambda = -3 + 3\mu\)
  2. 2 From \(x\): \(\lambda = 1 + 2\mu\). Into \(y\): \(1 + 4\mu = 7 - 2\mu\), so \(\mu = 1,\ \lambda = 3\)
  3. 3 Check \(z\): \(3 - 3 = 0\) and \(-3 + 3 = 0\) ✓
  4. ✓ The lines intersect at \((5, 5, 0)\).

I do Skew lines

Example 2

Show that the lines \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{k} + \lambda(2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = 3\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k} + \mu(\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k})\) are skew.

  1. 1 Directions \(2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}\) are not multiples, so the lines are not parallel.
  2. 2 \(x\): \(1 + 2\lambda = \mu\)   \(y\): \(\lambda = 3 - \mu\)   \(z\): \(2 - \lambda = 1 + 2\mu\)
  3. 3 From \(x\) and \(y\): \(\lambda = 3 - (1 + 2\lambda)\), so \(\lambda = \tfrac23,\ \mu = \tfrac73\)
  4. 4 Check \(z\): \(2 - \tfrac23 = \tfrac43\) but \(1 + \tfrac{14}{3} = \tfrac{17}{3}\) ✗
  5. ✓ No common point and not parallel, so the lines are skew.

We do Skew lines

Your turn, with me

Show that the lines \(\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \lambda(\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{k} + \mu(2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\) are skew.

  1. 1 The directions are not multiples, so the lines are not parallel.
  2. 2 \(x\): \(2 + \lambda = 1 + 2\mu\)   \(y\): \(1 + \lambda = -\mu\)   \(z\): \(\lambda = 3 + \mu\)
  3. 3 From \(y\) and \(z\): \(4 + \mu = -\mu\), so \(\mu = -2,\ \lambda = 1\)
  4. 4 Check \(x\): \(2 + 1 = 3\) but \(1 - 4 = -3\) ✗
  5. ✓ The lines are skew.

I do The angle between two lines

Example 3

Find the acute angle between the lines \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + \lambda(\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + \mu(\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\), correct to one decimal place.

  1. 1 \(\mathbf{d}_1\cdot\mathbf{d}_2 = 1 - 2 + 2 = 1\)
  2. 2 \(|\mathbf{d}_1| = \sqrt6\) and \(|\mathbf{d}_2| = \sqrt6\)
  3. 3 \(\cos\theta = \tfrac16\)
  4. ✓ \(\theta \approx 80.4^\circ\)

We do The angle between two lines

Your turn, with me

Find the acute angle between the lines \(\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + \lambda(\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = 3\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j} - 3\mathbf{k} + \mu(2\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k})\), correct to one decimal place.

  1. 1 \(\mathbf{d}_1\cdot\mathbf{d}_2 = 2 - 4 - 3 = -5\)
  2. 2 \(|\mathbf{d}_1| = \sqrt6\) and \(|\mathbf{d}_2| = \sqrt{17}\)
  3. 3 Acute angle: \(\cos\theta = \dfrac{|-5|}{\sqrt{102}}\)
  4. ✓ \(\theta \approx 60.3^\circ\)

I do Lines that meet, with an unknown Exam style

Example 4

The lines \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + \lambda(2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = a\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mu(\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k})\) intersect. Find \(a\) and the point of intersection.

  1. 1 \(y\): \(2 - \lambda = -1 + \mu\)   \(z\): \(3 + \lambda = 2\mu\)
  2. 2 Adding: \(5 = -1 + 3\mu\), so \(\mu = 2,\ \lambda = 1\)
  3. 3 \(x\): \(1 + 2\lambda = a + \mu\), so \(3 = a + 2\)
  4. ✓ \(a = 1\), and the lines meet at \((3, 1, 4)\).
Strategy

Start with the two equations that do not contain the unknown.

Your turn, with me

The lines \(\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{k} + \lambda(\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k})\) and \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + b\mathbf{j} - 4\mathbf{k} + \mu(2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\) intersect. Find \(b\) and the point of intersection.

  1. 1 \(x\): \(2 + \lambda = 1 + 2\mu\)   \(z\): \(1 - \lambda = -4 + \mu\)
  2. 2 Adding: \(3 = -3 + 3\mu\), so \(\mu = 2,\ \lambda = 3\)
  3. 3 \(y\): \(2\lambda = b + \mu\), so \(6 = b + 2\)
  4. ✓ \(b = 4\), and the lines meet at \((5, 6, -2)\).
