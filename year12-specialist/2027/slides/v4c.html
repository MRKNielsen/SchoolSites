Scalar product of vectors

The dot product, angles and perpendicular vectors

VCE Specialist Mathematics 3&4 · Term 1, 2027

Chapter 4 · Section 4C

By the end of this section you can…

  • Calculate \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}\) from components

Textbook: Cambridge Specialist Mathematics 3&4, Section 4C

The scalar product

Scalar (dot) product

\[\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = |\mathbf{a}|\,|\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta\] where \(\theta\) is the angle between the vectors placed tail to tail. The answer is a number.

In components

\[\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3\]

Angle

\[\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|\,|\mathbf{b}|}\]

\(\theta\) is measured tail to tail, \(0 \le \theta \le 180^\circ\)

Properties worth knowing

  • \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a}\)
  • \(\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}\)
  • \((k\mathbf{a})\cdot \mathbf{b} = k(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})\)
  • \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a} = |\mathbf{a}|^2\)
Perpendicular

For non-zero vectors, \(\mathbf{a} \perp \mathbf{b}\) exactly when \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0\).

Sign

\(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} > 0\) means an acute angle. \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} < 0\) means an obtuse angle.

I do Dot product and angle

Example 1

Let \(\mathbf{a} = 3\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k}\). Find \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}\) and the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\), correct to one decimal place.

  1. 1 \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = (3)(1) + (-1)(4) + (2)(-1) = -3\)
  2. 2 \(|\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{14}\) and \(|\mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt2\)
  3. 3 \(\cos\theta = \dfrac{-3}{\sqrt{14}\times 3\sqrt2} = -\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt7}\)
  4. ✓ \(\theta \approx 100.9^\circ\)

I do Perpendicular vectors

Example 2

Find the values of \(t\) for which \(t\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - 3\mathbf{k}\) is perpendicular to \(t\mathbf{i} - t\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}\).

  1. 1 Perpendicular, so the dot product is zero.
  2. 2 \(t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0\)
  3. 3 \((t - 3)(t + 1) = 0\)
  4. ✓ \(t = 3\) or \(t = -1\)

We do Perpendicular vectors

Your turn, with me

Find the values of \(t\) for which \(2t\mathbf{i} + t\mathbf{j} - 4\mathbf{k}\) is perpendicular to \(t\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}\).

  1. 1 \(2t^2 - 2t - 4 = 0\)
  2. 2 \(t^2 - t - 2 = 0\), so \((t - 2)(t + 1) = 0\)
  3. ✓ \(t = 2\) or \(t = -1\)

I do Dot product algebra

Example 3

Given \(|\mathbf{a}| = 3\), \(|\mathbf{b}| = 4\) and \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = -5\), find \(|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}|\) and \((\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b})\).

  1. 1 \(|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}|^2 = (\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}) = |\mathbf{a}|^2 + 2\,\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} + |\mathbf{b}|^2\)
  2. 2 \(= 9 - 10 + 16 = 15\), so \(|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{15}\)
  3. 3 \((\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) = |\mathbf{a}|^2 + \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} - 2|\mathbf{b}|^2\)
  4. ✓ \(|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{15}\) and \((\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) = 9 - 5 - 32 = -28\)
Key move

To find a length, square it: \(|\mathbf{v}|^2 = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{v}\).

We do Dot product algebra

Your turn, with me

Given \(|\mathbf{a}| = 2\), \(|\mathbf{b}| = 5\) and the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) is \(60^\circ\), find \(|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}|\) and \((2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b})\).

  1. 1 \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 2 \times 5 \times \cos 60^\circ = 5\)
  2. 2 \(|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}|^2 = 4 - 10 + 25 = 19\)
  3. 3 \((2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) = 2|\mathbf{a}|^2 - \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} - |\mathbf{b}|^2 = 8 - 5 - 25\)
  4. ✓ \(|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{19}\) and \((2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) = -22\)

I do Angle and area of a triangle Exam style

Example 4

The points \(A(1, 2, -1)\), \(B(3, 0, 2)\) and \(C(0, 1, 1)\) form a triangle. Find \(\cos(\angle BAC)\) and the exact area of the triangle.

  1. 1 \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC} = -\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}\)
  2. 2 \(\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC} = -2 + 2 + 6 = 6\), \(|\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{17}\), \(|\overrightarrow{AC}| = \sqrt6\)
  3. 3 \(\cos(\angle BAC) = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{102}}\)
  4. 4 \(\sin(\angle BAC) = \sqrt{1 - \tfrac{36}{102}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{66}{102}}\)
  5. 5 Area \(= \tfrac12\,|\overrightarrow{AB}|\,|\overrightarrow{AC}|\sin(\angle BAC) = \tfrac12\sqrt{102}\times\sqrt{\tfrac{66}{102}}\)
  6. ✓ \(\cos(\angle BAC) = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{102}}\) and area \(= \dfrac{\sqrt{66}}{2}\)
Angle at A

Both vectors must start at \(A\).

Your turn, with me

The points \(A(2, 1, 0)\), \(B(4, 2, 2)\) and \(C(1, 3, 1)\) form a triangle. Find \(\cos(\angle BAC)\) and the exact area of the triangle.

  1. 1 \(\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC} = -\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}\)
  2. 2 \(\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC} = -2 + 2 + 2 = 2\), \(|\overrightarrow{AB}| = 3\), \(|\overrightarrow{AC}| = \sqrt6\)
  3. 3 \(\cos(\angle BAC) = \dfrac{2}{3\sqrt6} = \dfrac{\sqrt6}{9}\)
  4. 4 Area \(= \tfrac12\sqrt{|\overrightarrow{AB}|^2|\overrightarrow{AC}|^2 - (\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC})^2} = \tfrac12\sqrt{54 - 4}\)
  5. ✓ \(\cos(\angle BAC) = \dfrac{\sqrt6}{9}\) and area \(= \dfrac{5\sqrt2}{2}\)
