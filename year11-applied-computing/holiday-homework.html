Reading — Somebody has to decide what the numbers mean

A weather station on a runway at Mildura records a temperature every day. So does one on a lighthouse at Cape Otway, and four more around Victoria. Nobody reads them. There are 2,190 readings in a year from those six stations alone, and a row of numbers answers no question on its own.

That gap is what this subject is about. Data is raw, unorganised facts and figures. Information is data that has been organised, given a context, and made to answer a question. “24.4” is data. “Mildura's average maximum in 2025 was 24.4 °C” is information. The number did not change; what changed is that it now answers something.

Closing that gap is a job, and it has a method. You work out what the question actually is, you design something that would answer it, you build it, and afterwards you judge whether it worked. In Unit 1 you will do that twice: once with a database — data split into tables that relate to each other, so each fact is stored once — which you will question using SQL, a language for asking a database things; and once with Python, writing programs of your own.

The part people find surprising is how much of it is judgement rather than typing. A chart can be drawn from entirely true numbers and still leave a reader believing something false. A survey can be run honestly and still miss the people whose answers would have changed it. An average can be correct and describe nobody. Most of what goes wrong in data analysis is not wrong numbers — it is a true number attached to a claim it cannot support.

So the subject is half technical and half argument. You will learn to build the thing, and to say honestly what it does and does not show. Both halves are assessed, and the second is the one that takes longer to get good at.

Three things every program does

Sequence — one step after another. Selection — choosing between steps, if this, then that, otherwise the other. Iteration — repeating steps until something is true. Everything you write this year is those three, combined.
