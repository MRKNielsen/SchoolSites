Problem-solving methodology · Overview

How to read this page

The problem-solving methodology comprises the four stages of analysis, design, development and evaluation. For each of these stages there is a typical set of activities. When creating solutions, this methodology can be applied as a single stage-by-stage problem-solving process or applied to each iteration of an agile problem-solving process.

The Unit 1 and Unit 2 columns

Not every activity applies in every unit. The tables below carry the study design's own applicability marks: yes means the activity is specified for that unit, — means it is not. Unit 1 concentrates on design and development; Unit 2 Area of Study 1 runs all four stages.

A note on scope

This page reproduces the problem-solving methodology specification. It is not the same list as the key knowledge for each area of study — an activity can be absent here but still named in an outcome's key knowledge. Ideation is the example to watch: unticked in the specification for both Applied Computing units, but explicitly listed in Unit 2 Area of Study 1 key knowledge. Read this page alongside the Unit 1 and Unit 2 outlines, not instead of them.

Problem-solving methodology · Stage 1 of 4

Analysis

Analysis involves determining what is required to solve a problem. This involves acquiring and analysing data, and then identifying the solution requirements, constraints and scope.

Solution requirements

Solution requirements can be described as functional and non-functional.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Determine the functional requirements of the solution. These describe what the software solution should do. This involves specific details such as input required, output developed and functions of the solution, including data manipulation and validation.yesyes
Determine the non-functional requirements. These describe the quality attributes of the solution, including usability, reliability, portability, robustness and maintainability.yesyes
Use tools to assist in determining solution requirements, including context diagrams, data flow diagrams and use case diagrams.——
Solution constraints

Solution constraints can be described as the conditions or limitations that must be considered when designing a solution.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Determine the constraints of the solution. These include economic (cost and time), technical (speed of processing, capacity, availability of equipment, compatibility, security), social (level of expertise of users), legal (intellectual property, ownership of data, privacy of data), and usability (accessibility, usefulness, ease of use).yesyes
Solution scope

Solution scope describes the boundaries or parameters of the solution.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Identify what will be and/or what will not be addressed by the solution.yesyes

Problem-solving methodology · Stage 2 of 4

Design

Design involves determining how the solution requirements will function and appear. It involves designing the functionality, appearance and user interface of the solution and developing evaluation criteria to determine the efficiency and effectiveness of the software solutions.

Solution design

Solution design focuses on the development of design ideas into preferred designs using design principles and a range of relevant design tools.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Design how the solution will function and appear by identifying the specific data required and how it will be named, structured, validated and manipulated.yesyes
Use ideation techniques and tools for generating design ideas, including mood boards, brainstorming, mind maps, sketches and annotations.——
Use design tools to demonstrate structure and functionality, including data dictionaries, input–process–output (IPO) charts, object descriptions, flowcharts and pseudocode.yesyes
Use design tools to show the relationships between the components of a solution, including annotated diagrams, mock-ups, storyboards, sitemaps, queries, context diagrams, data flow diagrams and use case diagrams.yesyes
Use design tools to design the visual components of a solution (user interface, graphic representations or data visualisations), including layout diagrams, annotated diagrams and mock-ups. This includes identifying the position and size of text, images and graphics, font types and styles, colours and text enhancements.yesyes
Skills underpinning the Design stage
ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Investigate existing and possible solutions.—yes
Generate design ideas and develop connections between ideas using text and diagrams.—yes
Produce detailed designs with annotations, including appearance and functionality of the solution, based on design ideas.—yes
Determine possible contingencies when developing solution designs to mitigate issues.—yes
Evaluation criteria

Evaluation criteria measure the efficiency and effectiveness of designs and the entire solution.

Note that the same evaluation criteria are used in the Evaluation stage to determine the degree to which the solution meets requirements. In the Evaluation stage, the evaluation criteria measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the solution.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Develop evaluation criteria to determine the degree to which solution designs meet requirements.—yes
Modify and refine the evaluation criteria as the solution develops.——
Problem-solving methodology · Stage 3 of 4

Development

Development involves transforming the requirements and designs into a working software solution. It involves the manipulation and validation of data, testing to ensure the software solution meets requirements and generating documentation to support the use of the solution.

Manipulation

Manipulation involves applying a range of functions and techniques using software tools.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Develop the solution to meet specifications using appropriate algorithms, features of programming languages or software functions and techniques.yesyes
Apply appropriate formats and conventions to enhance the appearance of solutions.yes—
Perform calculations using descriptive statistics and identify trends, patterns and relationships.yes—
Validation

Validation checks the reasonableness of data being input. Validation can be both manual and electronic.

Note that the effectiveness of validation is determined through the testing activity below.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Use validation techniques to check data entry for reasonableness and completeness of data, including existence check, range check and type check.yes—
Use verification techniques after data entry to ensure that data entered matches the source data, including proofreading, confirming the data against other sources and checking that the data visualised is consistent with the data acquired/collected.——
Testing

Testing checks whether solutions meet all the requirements, function as expected and are usable by intended users.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Conduct debugging techniques to ensure solutions meet requirements.yes—
Develop a testing strategy to ensure that the solution works as intended, including features to be tested, test data and expected results.yesyes
Conduct tests, record the actual results and compare the actual results of the tests against the expected results.yesyes
Correct identified errors.yesyes
Documentation

Documentation is used to explain the structure and behaviour of a solution.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Write internal documentation to support the functioning, maintenance and upgrading of the solution.yes—
Write documentation to support the development of solutions.—yes

Problem-solving methodology · Stage 4 of 4

Evaluation

Evaluation involves determining the degree to which the software solution has met requirements. It involves developing a strategy to evaluate the software solution after implementation and evaluating the software solution against the evaluation criteria to see how well it meets requirements.

Evaluation strategy

Evaluation strategies focus on how the solution will be evaluated, including evaluation criteria, time frame and responsibility.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Propose a strategy to evaluate the extent to which the solution meets identified problems, needs or opportunities, or solves the identified problem after the solution has been developed and implemented.——
Specify a timeline for evaluation, the data to be collected, methods and techniques for data collection, who is responsible for collection and how the data relates to the evaluation criteria.——
Solution evaluation

Solution evaluation determines whether a solution has met requirements.

ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Use the evaluation criteria to determine how the solution has met requirements, needs or opportunities. Discuss in terms of efficiency and effectiveness of the solution.—yes
Skills underpinning the Evaluation stage
ActivityUnit 1Unit 2
Critically evaluate the analysis process and discuss how it assisted in meeting the requirements, problems, needs or opportunities.——
Critically evaluate the design process and discuss how it assisted in meeting the requirements, needs or opportunities.——
Identify and discuss any improvements that could be made to the solution by approaching the problem-solving methodology process differently.——
