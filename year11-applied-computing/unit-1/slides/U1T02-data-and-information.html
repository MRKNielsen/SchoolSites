Part 1

Two types of data

Qualitative · Quantitative

Vocabulary

Qualitative and quantitative

TermSay it like thisLooks like
QualitativeDescribes a quality. Words, categories, opinions.“Students said the canteen queue felt too slow”
QuantitativeDescribes a quantity. Numbers you can count or measure.Mean wait time 4 minutes 20 seconds
Careful

A number is not automatically quantitative. A station ID of 86038 is a label, not a measurement — averaging station IDs is meaningless. Ask what the number measures, not whether it is a digit.

Key knowledge

Five sources, five purposes

The study design names these. Each pairs a kind of data with the job it does.

  1. 1 Interviews and surveys — to gather insights and perspectives on a topical issue
  2. 2 Sensor data — to monitor a person's health
  3. 3 Census and demographic data — for statistical analysis
  4. 4 Data collected over a period of time — to show change and spot trends
  5. 5 Data generated by artificial intelligence — and this one needs care, see below

Worked example

I do One dataset, both types

Our weather dataset holds 2190 daily observations from six Victorian stations.

FieldType
station_nameQualitative
region_nameQualitative
max_temp_cQuantitative
rainfall_mmQuantitative
station_idNeither — an identifier
Why it matters

You can take a mean of max_temp_c. You cannot take a mean of region_name — you can only count it.

The type decides which statistics are available to you, which decides which chart you can honestly draw.

The trap

Emerging

Data generated by artificial intelligence

AI systems now produce data as well as consume it: synthetic records, generated images, model predictions presented as measurements.

It is in the study design because you will meet it, not because it is safe to use uncritically.

Discuss

If a model predicts tomorrow's maximum temperature and you store 24.6 in the same column as yesterday's measured 24.6 — what have you lost?

This dataset is itself synthetic, generated to mirror real Bureau of Meteorology structure. Its data dictionary says so in the first paragraph. That disclosure is the point.

Together

We do Three that argue back

4. Heart rate logged every minute by a watch

Quantitative, and sensor data collected over time

5. A star rating out of five

Quantitative, but ordinal — the gap from 1 to 2 need not equal 4 to 5, so a mean is arguable

6. Suburb name in a census record

Qualitative — a category you can count, not measure

Number 5 is the one to remember

Being a number is not enough. Ask whether the gaps between the numbers mean the same thing.

Activity

We do Hunt the data

Open a news article from this week. Find every data point in it — in the text, in a chart, in a caption.

For each one, record:

  • the value
  • qualitative or quantitative
  • where it came from
  • whether the article says how it was collected
The interesting column

That last one. Most articles do not tell you. Keep the list — we come back to it in Topic 4 when we look at what makes data reliable.
