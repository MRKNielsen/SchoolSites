Why this lesson is early

Every chart you draw this term inherits the quality of the data underneath it. A beautiful visualisation of bad data is worse than no visualisation, because people believe it.

Key knowledge

Five factors affecting quality

FactorThe questionFails when
AccuracyIs the value correct?A maximum of 85 °C in Mildura
BiasDoes the collection method favour an outcome?Only surveying students who already use the canteen
IntegrityIs the data whole and internally consistent?Duplicate rows; three date formats in one column
RelevanceDoes it bear on the question?Humidity, when you were asked about rainfall
ReliabilityCan the source be depended on, repeatedly?A sensor that drifts; an anonymous blog

The pair people confuse

Accurate is not the same as reliable

Accurate

Close to the true value. A property of this reading.

Reliable

Dependable over time. A property of the source.

Why the distinction matters

A thermometer reading 2 °C too high every single day is reliable but not accurate — and because it is reliable, you can correct it.

A thermometer that is right on average but wildly wrong on random days is accurate on average and useless, because you cannot tell which days to trust.

Key knowledge

Bias is built in before you collect anything

Bias is not someone lying. It is a collection method that makes one answer more likely than the truth warrants.

  • Surveying only at lunchtime — you miss everyone in class
  • An online form — you miss everyone without reliable internet
  • Weather stations only at airports — flat, open, often warmer than the suburbs around them
Discuss

Five of our six stations are at airports. What would that do to a claim about “Victoria’s temperature”? Is the dataset wrong, or is the claim?

Worked example

I do A row built to carry every fault

A constructed row

99999 | Unknown Station | Greater Melborne | VIC | 14/03/2025 | 11.2 | 82.4 °C | -3.1 | 180

No single row in the real extract is this bad. This one is assembled from six faults that are genuinely in there, so we can meet them all at once.

  1. 1 82.4 °C — the unit is inside the value. The column is now text. Integrity.
  2. 2 82.4 is not a possible Victorian maximum. Accuracy.
  3. 3 Rainfall of -3.1 cannot exist. Accuracy.
  4. 4 Humidity of 180 per cent cannot exist. Accuracy.
  5. 5 “Melborne” is misspelled; it will not match the region table. Integrity.
  6. ✓ Station 99999 matches no station at all. Integrity — a broken relationship.
Six faults in one row

And none of them stop the row loading. That is the whole problem with data quality: nothing announces itself.

Practice

You must decide, and say why

FaultOptionsWhat the study design wants
Missing valueImpute, exclude, or flagA decision you can justify
Impossible valueCorrect, exclude, or flagNever silently keep it
DuplicateRemoveSay how you identified it
Inconsistent formatNormalise to oneState which one and why

There is rarely one right answer. There is always a wrong one: changing the data without recording that you did.
