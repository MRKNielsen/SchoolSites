What Unit 1 asks of you

You will not write a specification this year — you will be given one and marked on whether you read it correctly. That is a skill, and it is practised, not guessed.

Key knowledge

What a brief has to tell you

ElementAnswersExample from a data brief
Functional requirementWhat must it do?Show mean maximum temperature per region
Non-functional requirementWhat must it be like?Readable by a Year 7 student
ConstraintWhat limits me?Spreadsheet software only; two lessons
ScopeWhere does it stop?2025 data only; no forecasting

Key knowledge

Five kinds of constraint

KindExamples
EconomicCost, and time — time is the one that bites at school
TechnicalProcessing speed, capacity, equipment available, compatibility, security
SocialThe level of expertise your users actually have
LegalIntellectual property, ownership of data, privacy of data
UsabilityAccessibility, usefulness, ease of use

Naming the category is worth marks. “We only have two weeks” is an economic constraint, not a vague complaint about time.

Worked example

I do Pulling a brief apart

The brief

“Produce a one-page infographic showing how rainfall differed between Victorian regions in 2025. It will be displayed in the Year 7 corridor. You have the class dataset and three lessons. Do not include temperature.”

  1. 1 “Showing how rainfall differed between regions” — functional.
  2. 2 “One-page infographic” — functional, it fixes the output format.
  3. 3 “Year 7 corridor” — non-functional. It must be readable by 12-year-olds, at a distance.
  4. 4 “Three lessons” — economic constraint. “The class dataset” — technical constraint.
  5. ✓ “Do not include temperature” — scope, stated as an exclusion.
The sentence doing most work

“Year 7 corridor” looks like background. It is actually the requirement that decides your font size, your colour choices and how much text you can use.

Scope

The test for scope

Scope sentences usually contain only, not, or a boundary. They tell you where to stop, which is what stops a project growing until it fails.

Key skill

Two lists, always

In scope

2025 observations; six stations; rainfall and temperature; one printed page

Out of scope

Years before 2025; forecasting; stations outside Victoria; an interactive version

The second list is the one that saves you. It is what you point at in week 7 when somebody asks for “just one more thing”.
