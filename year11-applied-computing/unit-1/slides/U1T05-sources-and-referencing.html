The habit to build now

Record where data came from at the moment you download it. Reconstructing it three weeks later is miserable, and usually impossible.

Key knowledge

Primary and secondary sources

PrimarySecondary
WhatYou collected it yourselfSomeone else collected it
ExampleA survey you ran; a sensor you placedBureau of Meteorology records; ABS Census
StrengthFits your question exactlyScale and expertise you could not match
WeaknessSmall, and your own bias is in itCollected for someone else's purpose

Neither is better. They answer different questions, and a strong analysis often uses both.

Key skill

Four questions to ask of any source

  1. 1 Who collected it, and what do they gain from the answer?
  2. 2 When? Data has a shelf life, and some of it spoils fast.
  3. 3 How? Sample size, method, and who got left out.
  4. ✓ Can I get to the original? A number quoted in an article is not a source. The thing it quotes is.
The fourth is the one people skip

Chasing a statistic back to where it started is the single most useful research habit in this subject. It is also how you find out that a surprising number of them do not survive the trip.

Key knowledge

Why referencing is not a formatting exercise

Data, writing, images and code are intellectual property. Someone made them, and using them without acknowledgement takes credit you have not earned.

Referencing does three jobs at once: it credits the maker, it lets a reader check your work, and it protects you.

Also worth knowing

Data about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities carries obligations beyond ordinary copyright. Indigenous Data Sovereignty holds that communities should govern data about their own people and Country. If your project touches it, that is a question to raise before you collect anything.

Key knowledge

An APA reference for a dataset

Author. (Year). Title of dataset [Data set]. Publisher. URL

Worked

Australian Bureau of Statistics. (2022). Census of Population and Housing: General community profile [Data set]. ABS. https://abs.gov.au/census

Four pieces: who, when, what, where. If you have those four recorded, you can format them later. If you have not, no amount of formatting will save you.

Worked example

I do How do you cite a synthetic dataset?

  1. 1 Who made it? This course did — so the school is the author.
  2. 2 When? 2026.
  3. 3 What? The title, and the fact that it is synthetic. Leaving that out would mislead.
  4. 4 Where? The unit folder on the class site.
  5. ✓ Northcote High School. (2026). Victorian climate observations 2025: a synthetic teaching dataset [Data set]. Applied Computing Unit 1.
The honest bit

“Synthetic” belongs in the title, not a footnote. A reader who missed it would think these were real Bureau records — which is exactly the misuse the reference exists to prevent.
