The idea underneath everything

A relational database splits data into tables that relate to each other, so each fact is stored once. Everything else follows from that one decision.

“Melbourne Airport” and “Greater Melbourne” are stored 365 times each. That is redundancy, and it causes three separate problems.

Key knowledge

Why repeating yourself is expensive

  1. 1 Space. The least important one, and the one people name first.
  2. 2 Update anomalies. Correct a station name in 364 rows and miss one — now you have two stations.
  3. 3 Inconsistency. Our raw file has MELBOURNE AIRPORT, Melb Airport and melbourne airport. All one place.
  4. ✓ Store each fact once, in one place, and all three disappear.
The rule

A fact about a station belongs in the station table. A fact about a day at a station belongs in the observation table.

Worked example

I do From one sheet to three tables

  1. 1 What things are described here? Regions, stations, and daily observations. Three things, three tables.
  2. 2 What is true of a region no matter the day? Its name and state. That is the region table.
  3. 3 What is true of a station no matter the day? Name, position, elevation, and which region it is in.
  4. 4 What changes every day? Temperature, rainfall, humidity. That is observation.
  5. ✓ Three tables, each fact stored once: 4 regions, 6 stations, 2190 observations.
The question that does the work

“What is this a fact about?” Ask it of every column and the tables fall out almost by themselves.

The test

If the value would be identical on every row of a 365-day year, it does not belong in the daily table.
