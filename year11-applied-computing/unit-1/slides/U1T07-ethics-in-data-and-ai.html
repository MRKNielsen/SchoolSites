Legal and ethical are different tests

The Privacy Act tells you what you must do. Ethics asks what you should do. Plenty of things pass the first test and fail the second.

Key knowledge

Five ethical issues in managing data

IssueLooks like
Lack of transparencyPeople do not know what is collected, or what it is used for
Inaccurate or incomplete dataA decision made about someone from a record that is wrong
Ownership and controlWho owns data you generated by using a service?
Misuse of personal dataCollected for one reason, used for another

Issue 1

Nobody reads the terms, and everybody knows it

Consent given without understanding is not meaningful consent, even when it is legally valid.

A 7000-word policy that nobody reads satisfies the law and fails the person.

Discuss

If a company knows its users do not read the policy, is relying on that consent honest? Does it change if they could have written it shorter?

Issue 2

When a wrong record becomes a decision

In our weather data, a wrong value means a wrong chart. When the data is about people, a wrong value means a wrong decision about a person — a refused loan, a flagged account, a missed diagnosis.

The technical fault is identical. The consequence is not.

The obligation that follows

If data about people drives decisions about them, they need a way to see it and correct it. That is why the quality work from Topic 4 is an ethical matter and not only a technical one.

Issues 3, 4 and 5

Who owns the trail you leave?

Using a service generates data: what you searched, how long you paused, where you were. You produced it. The company holds it.

Increasingly it also becomes training data — shared or repurposed into a model, where it cannot be pulled back out.

What makes AI repurposing different

Deletion stops working. You can delete a row from a database. You cannot delete a row from a model that has already learned from it.

Worked example

I do The smart watch case

The scenario

A school gives every student a fitness watch. Step counts go to the PE department. A year later the data is shared with a researcher studying teenage activity.

  1. 1 Legal? Possibly, if the consent form mentioned research. APP 6 is the test.
  2. 2 Transparent? Did a 14-year-old understand “research purposes” a year ago?
  3. 3 Ownership? The student produced every step. Who decided it could be shared?
  4. 4 Could they opt out? If the watch was compulsory, consent was not freely given.
  5. ✓ Legal and still ethically weak. Those are separate verdicts, and both need stating.
How to write this in an exam

Name the issue, apply it to the scenario, then say what should change. A general paragraph about privacy earns little; a specific fix earns marks.
