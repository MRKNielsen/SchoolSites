The one that catches people

Integer and floating point are both numbers to you. They are different things to a database, and choosing wrong loses data silently.

Starting point

The software has to be told what a value is

You look at 24.6 and see a temperature. A database sees characters until you tell it otherwise.

Declare it as text and you can no longer average it. Declare it as a whole number and the .6 is thrown away without a word of warning.

Key idea

The data type is a promise about what the value is and what may be done to it. Every later thing you do — sorting, averaging, validating, charting — rests on that promise.

Key knowledge · text

Character and string

TypeHoldsFrom our dataset
CharacterA single characterV in a state code
StringA sequence of characters"Melbourne Airport"
Watch for

A string of digits is still text. "86038" sorts before "9", because text sorts character by character. That is why station IDs look out of order until you tell the software they are numbers — or decide, correctly, that they are labels and should stay text.

Key knowledge · numeric

Integer, floating point, date/time

TypeHoldsFrom our dataset
IntegerWhole numbers, no fractionhumidity_9am — 68
Floating pointNumbers with a decimal partmax_temp_c — 24.6
Date/timeA point in time the software can order and subtractobs_date — 2025-03-14

Date/time is the one students most often store as text. It still looks right — and then “how many days between these two” becomes impossible.

Key knowledge · Boolean

Boolean: exactly two values

True or false. Nothing else.

Our dataset has no Boolean column, but it could: is_rain_day would be true whenever rainfall_mm is above zero.

Discuss

Would storing is_rain_day be useful, or is it redundant when you already store the rainfall? What does it cost you, and what does it save?

Worked example

I do Choosing a type for every column

  1. 1 station_name — words, varying length. String.
  2. 2 obs_date — I will want to sort and subtract these. Date/time, not text.
  3. 3 max_temp_c — has a decimal part. Floating point.
  4. 4 humidity_9am — whole per cent, never fractional. Integer.
  5. ✓ station_id — digits, but I will never average it. Integer as an identifier, and I must remember not to do arithmetic on it.
The question I asked each time

Will I ever do arithmetic on this? Will I ever need to order it? Those two answers pick the type almost every time.

Key knowledge

Characteristics of data and information

CharacteristicThe question it asks
SizeHow much is there? Will it open in a spreadsheet?
StructureIs it organised into fields and records, or a wall of text?
RelevanceDoes it bear on the question actually being asked?
AccessibilityCan the people who need it get to it, and read it?
ClarityCan it be understood without someone explaining it?
ContextDo you know where it came from and what it measures?

Context is the one that bites

A column of numbers with no record of where it came from or what it measures is not information. It is a liability.
