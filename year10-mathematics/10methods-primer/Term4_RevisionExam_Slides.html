  • answer one full exam-style question with a diagram

Formula wall — say each one aloud

Right-angled triangles
\(\sin\theta = \dfrac{O}{H}\)\(\cos\theta = \dfrac{A}{H}\)\(\tan\theta = \dfrac{O}{A}\)θ = sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹
Any triangle (10A)
\(\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B}\)c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos CArea = \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)ab sin C
Decision checkRight angle? → SOH CAH TOA. Opposite pair? → sine rule. SAS/SSS? → cosine rule. Bearings? → draw the north arrow FIRST.

Exam-style worked example (5 marks)

Worked example — bearingsMai walks 300 m from A on a bearing of 060°T to B, then 200 m on a bearing of 150°T to C.
a) Show the angle at B is 90°.   b) Find the distance AC.   c) Find the bearing of C from A (nearest degree).
1a) Direction B→A is 060 + 180 = 240°T; direction B→C is 150°T. Angle at B = 240 − 150 = 90° ✔ (1 mark: shown, not just stated)
2b) Pythagoras: AC = \(\sqrt{300^{2} + 200^{2}} = \sqrt{130000}\) ≈ 360.6 m (1 mark rule, 1 mark answer + units)
3c) Angle at A: tan α = \(\dfrac{200}{300}\) → α ≈ 33.7°
4Bearing: 060 + 33.7 ≈ 094°T (1 mark angle, 1 mark 3-digit bearing)
Where the marks live: diagram with north lines · the rule you chose · substitution · answer with units · 3-digit bearing.

Your turn (3 marks)

Your turnFrom the top of a 40 m tower, the angle of depression to a car is 25°. How far is the car from the base of the tower, to one decimal place?
Hint: alternate angles put 25° at the car. tan 25° = 40 ÷ d.
✔ d = 40 ÷ tan 25° ≈ 85.8 m. Marks: diagram (1), rule + substitution (1), answer + units (1).

Formula & discriminant
Δ = b² − 4acx = \(\dfrac{- b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}\)Δ>0: two · Δ=0: one · Δ<0: none
Sketch = 4 labels minimumx-intercepts · y-intercept · axis · turning point. Every label is usually a mark.

Exam-style worked example (4 marks)

Worked exampleSketch y = x² − 4x − 5, labelling all intercepts and the turning point.
1Factorise: y = (x − 5)(x + 1) → x-intercepts 5 and −1
2y-intercept: x = 0 → −5
3Axis: midpoint of −1 and 5 → x = 2
4TP: y = 4 − 8 − 5 = −9 → (2, −9) minimum. Draw + label all four features.
Where the marks live: one for each labelled feature. An unlabelled beautiful curve scores almost nothing!

Formula wall — say each one aloud

Exponentials
y = aˣ: through (0, 1), asymptote y = 0A = A₀(1 ± \(\dfrac{r}{100}\))ⁿA = P(1 + \(\dfrac{r}{100}\))ⁿ
Logarithms
logₐ x = y ⟺ aʸ = xlog x + log y = log(xy)aˣ = b → x = \(\dfrac{\log b}{\log a}\)
Translate percentages+5% → ×1.05 · −12% → ×0.88 · “when does it double?” → set the bracket power equal to 2 and use logs.

Exam-style worked example (5 marks)

Worked example — compound interest$12 000 is invested at 5% p.a. compound interest.
a) Write the rule.   b) Find the amount after 4 years (nearest cent).   c) How long until the investment doubles? (1 d.p.)
1a) Rule: A = 12000(1.05)ⁿ (1 mark)
2b) Substitute n = 4: A = 12000 × 1.05⁴ ≈ $14 586.08 (1 mark working, 1 mark rounding)
3c) Double: 12000(1.05)ⁿ = 24000 → (1.05)ⁿ = 2
4Logs: n = \(\dfrac{\log 2}{\log 1.05}\) ≈ 14.2 years (1 mark log step, 1 mark answer in words)

Your turn (3 marks)

Your turna) Evaluate log₂ 32 and log₁₀ 0.001.   b) Solve 2ˣ = 100 to two decimal places.
Hint: 2⁵ = 32; 0.001 = 10⁻³; x = log 100 ÷ log 2.
✔ a) log₂ 32 = 5; log₁₀ 0.001 = −3   b) x = 2 ÷ 0.30103 ≈ 6.64 (check: 2⁶ = 64, 2⁷ = 128 ✔)

Formula wall — say each one aloud

Rules
Pr(A ∪ B) = Pr(A) + Pr(B) − Pr(A ∩ B)Pr(A | B) = \(\dfrac{Pr(A ∩ B)}{Pr(B)}\)independent: Pr(A ∩ B) = Pr(A)Pr(B)
Tools
Venn: “both” number firsttree: × along, + betweenⁿCᵣ selections · falling product arrangements

Exam-style worked example (5 marks)

Worked exampleIn a class of 50, 28 students have a dog (D), 16 have a cat (C) and 8 have both.
a) Show this on a Venn diagram.   b) Find Pr(neither).   c) Find Pr(D | C).   d) Are D and C independent?
1a) Both first: 8 → only D = 20, only C = 8, neither = 50 − 36 = 14 (1 mark)
2b) Pr(neither) = \(\dfrac{14}{50} = \dfrac{7}{25}\) (1 mark)
3c) Given C: new total 16 → Pr(D | C) = \(\dfrac{8}{16} = \dfrac{1}{2}\) (1 mark)
4d) Test: Pr(D) = 0.56 but Pr(D | C) = 0.5 — different → not independent (1 mark test, 1 mark conclusion sentence)

Your turn (3 marks)

Your turnA bag has 4 red and 3 blue marbles. Two are drawn without replacement. Find Pr(different colours).
Hint: paths RB and BR; multiply along, add between.
✔ \(\dfrac{4}{7} \times \dfrac{3}{6} + \dfrac{3}{7} \times \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{12}{42} + \dfrac{12}{42} = \dfrac{24}{42}\) = \(\dfrac{4}{7}\)

ENRICHMENT — CH 11 ACTIVITY 1

Solving equations with numerical methods

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • solve an equation by trapping it in an interval
  • run the bisection method by hand and by spreadsheet
  • connect this to how computers really solve equations

Key idea — trap the answer

Bisection methodSolve x² = 5. At x = 2: 4 (too small). At x = 3: 9 (too big). The answer is trapped between 2 and 3.
Halve the trapTest the midpoint, keep the half that still traps the answer, repeat. Each step halves the possible error.

Worked example — three bisection steps

Worked exampleUse bisection to estimate \(\sqrt{5}\) (solve x² − 5 = 0), starting with [2, 3].
1Midpoint 2.5: 2.5² = 6.25 > 5 → keep [2, 2.5]
2Midpoint 2.25: 5.06 > 5 → keep [2, 2.25]
3Midpoint 2.125: 4.52 < 5 → keep [2.125, 2.25]
4Estimate: \(\sqrt{5}\) ≈ 2.19 ± 0.06 — and the calculator says 2.236… we are closing in!

The Monty Hall problem

The gameThree doors: one car 🚗, two goats 🐐. You pick a door. The host (who knows) opens a DIFFERENT door showing a goat, then asks: stick or switch?
The argumentMost people say 50–50. The tree says otherwise…
✔ Your first pick is the car only \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) of the time — so switching wins \(\dfrac{2}{3}\) of the time!
