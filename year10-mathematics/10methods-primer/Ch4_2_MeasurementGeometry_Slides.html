LESSON 1 — SECTIONS 4E/4G

Review of length and area

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use the perimeter and area formulas fluently
  • break a composite shape into simple parts
  • round only at the end

Formula wall — say each one aloud

Length
C = 2πr = πdperimeter = add ALL edges
Area
rectangle lwtriangle \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)bhcircle πr²trapezium \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)(a + b)h
Composite shapesCut into simple pieces → add (or subtract) the parts. Semicircle = half a circle — divide by 2!

Worked example 1

Worked exampleA pool is a rectangle 8 m × 5 m with a semicircle on one end (diameter 5 m). Find the perimeter and area, to 1 d.p.
1Perimeter: 8 + 5 + 8 + half circumference = 21 + \(\dfrac{\pi \times 5}{2}\) ≈ 21 + 7.85 = 28.9 m (the flat 5 m side of the semicircle is INSIDE — not counted)
2Area of rectangle: 8 × 5 = 40 m²
3Area of semicircle: ½ × π × 2.5² ≈ 9.82 m²
4Total area: ≈ 49.8 m²

Your turn

Your turnA square of side 6 cm has a quarter-circle (radius 6 cm) removed from one corner. Find the remaining area, to 1 d.p.
Hint: 36 − ¼ × π × 36.
✔ 36 − 28.27 ≈ 7.7 cm²

  • Draw and label EVERY shape — cut lines in pencil

LESSON 2 — SECTION 4F

Pythagoras including three-dimensional problems

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • apply c² = a² + b² fluently
  • find diagonals inside 3D objects with two steps
  • give exact and rounded answers

Key idea — two triangles for 3D

Pythagorasc² = a² + b² (right-angled triangles only)
3D strategy1️⃣ base diagonal (triangle on the floor) → 2️⃣ space diagonal (triangle standing up, using the base diagonal).

Worked example 2

Worked exampleA room is 4 m × 3 m × 2.5 m. Find the length of the diagonal from a bottom corner to the opposite top corner, to 2 d.p.
1Base diagonal: \(\sqrt{4^{2} + 3^{2}} = \sqrt{25}\) = 5 m
2Space diagonal: \(\sqrt{5^{2} + 2.5^{2}} = \sqrt{31.25}\)
3Answer: ≈ 5.59 m (exact form \(\sqrt{31.25} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{5}}{2}\) — surd skills from Unit 2!)

Your turn

Your turnA shipping box is 6 m × 2 m × 3 m. Will a 7 m pole fit inside along the space diagonal?
Hint: base diagonal \(\sqrt{40}\), then \(\sqrt{40 + 9}\).
✔ Diagonal = \(\sqrt{49}\) = 7 m exactly — it just fits (in theory!). Answer in words.
📘 Your work: Exercise 4F
  • 2D fluency, then two-step 3D problems
  • Label “Triangle 1” and “Triangle 2” in your working

LESSON 3 — SECTION 4H

Measurement errors and accuracy

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • state the limits of accuracy of a measurement
  • explain why every measurement is an interval
  • find smallest and largest possible perimeter/area

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
limits of accuracy—the smallest and largest true value a measurement could be
to the nearest cm—the real value is within half a cm either side
lower / upper boundbowndthe bottom / top of the interval

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — ± half the unit

Key idea“12 cm to the nearest cm” means the true length is anywhere in 11.5 cm ≤ L < 12.5 cm. Every measurement is really an interval!
ConsequenceCalculations inherit the error: smallest area uses lower bounds, largest area uses upper bounds.

Worked example 3

Worked exampleA square has sides measured as 10 cm to the nearest cm. Find the limits of accuracy for its area.
1Side limits: 9.5 ≤ s < 10.5
2Smallest area: 9.5² = 90.25 cm²
3Largest area: 10.5² = 110.25 cm²
4In words: “The area is between 90.25 and 110.25 cm² — a 20 cm² spread from a 1 cm ruler!”

Your turn

Your turnA length is recorded as 36 mm to the nearest mm. State the limits of accuracy.
Hint: half of 1 mm each way.
✔ 35.5 mm ≤ L < 36.5 mm
📘 Your work: Exercise 4H
  • Limits of accuracy, then perimeter/area bounds
  • Write the interval with ≤ on the left, < on the right
  • Discussion: why do builders measure twice?

LESSON 4 — SECTION 4I

Surface area of prisms and cylinders

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • think in nets: every face counted once
  • use the cylinder surface area formula
  • set working out face by face

Key idea — unfold the solid

PrismsSA = sum of ALL faces. List them: top, bottom, front, back, two sides — tick each one.
CylinderSA = 2πr² + 2πrh
two circles + the unrolled rectangle (label: 2πr wide, h tall).

Worked example 4

Worked exampleFind the surface area of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 7 cm (exact, then 1 d.p.).
1Two circles: 2π(3²) = 18π
2Curved surface: 2π(3)(7) = 42π
3Total: 60π cm² (exact) ≈ 188.5 cm²

Your turn

Your turnFind the surface area of a rectangular prism 4 cm × 3 cm × 5 cm.
Hint: three different faces, each appearing twice: 2(lw + lh + wh).
✔ 2(12 + 20 + 15) = 94 cm²
📘 Your work: Exercise 4I
  • Prisms face-by-face, then cylinders
  • Exact (π form) AND rounded answers when asked
  • Open-top questions: read carefully — one circle missing!

LESSON 5 — SECTION 4J (10A)

Surface area of pyramids and cones

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use slant height (not vertical height!) for surfaces
  • find SA of square pyramids and cones
  • find the slant height with Pythagoras when needed

Key idea — slant for surface

ConeSA = πr² + πrs — s is the slant height (along the surface).
Square pyramidbase + 4 triangles: SA = b² + 4 × \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)bs
Only given h?Pythagoras: s² = r² + h² (cone). Slant ALWAYS ≥ height.

Worked example 5

Worked examplea) Cone: r = 3 m, s = 5 m — find SA (exact + 1 d.p.). b) Square pyramid: base 6 cm, slant 5 cm — find SA.
1a) SA = π(3²) + π(3)(5) = 9π + 15π = 24π ≈ 75.4 m²
2b) Base: 6² = 36
3Four triangles: 4 × ½ × 6 × 5 = 60
4Total: 96 cm²

Your turn

Your turnA cone has r = 6 cm and vertical height h = 8 cm. Find s, then the total SA (exact).
Hint: s = \(\sqrt{36 + 64}\).
✔ s = 10 → SA = 36π + 60π = 96π cm²
📘 Your work: Exercise 4J
  • Given s first, then find-s-first questions
  • Underline in colour: h = vertical, s = slant
  • Exact π answers unless told to round

LESSON 6 — SECTION 4K

Volume of prisms and cylinders

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use V = area of cross-section × length
  • find volumes of triangular prisms and cylinders
  • convert units: cm³, m³, L

Key idea — slice and stack

Prisms and cylindersV = A × h — area of the cross-section (the constant slice) × the length/height.
CylinderV = πr²h — the slice is a circle.
Units bonus1 cm³ = 1 mL · 1000 cm³ = 1 L — volume questions love units.

Worked example 6

Worked examplea) Cylinder r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm — volume (exact + 1 d.p.). b) A triangular prism: triangle base 6 cm, height 4 cm; prism length 9 cm.
1a) V = π(4²)(10) = 160π ≈ 502.7 cm³
2b) Slice area: ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm²
3Volume: 12 × 9 = 108 cm³

Your turn

Your turnA water tank is a cylinder r = 1.5 m, h = 2 m. Find its capacity in litres (1 m³ = 1000 L), nearest litre.
Hint: V = π × 2.25 × 2 m³ first.
✔ V ≈ 14.137 m³ ≈ 14 137 L
📘 Your work: Exercise 4K
  • Identify the cross-section first — shade it on the diagram
  • Capacity conversions: cm³ ↔ mL ↔ L
  • Composite solids: add or subtract volumes

LESSON 7 — SECTION 4L (10A)

Volume of pyramids and cones

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use the one-third rule
  • find volumes of cones and square pyramids
  • use vertical height h (not slant!) for volume

Key idea — exactly one third

The one-third ruleV = \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) × base area × h
A pyramid fills exactly ⅓ of its box; a cone fills ⅓ of its cylinder.
h vs s — opposite of 4J!Volume uses vertical height h. Surface uses slant s. Read the diagram carefully.

Worked example 7

Worked examplea) Cone: r = 3 m, h = 4 m. b) Square pyramid: base 5 cm, h = 6 cm.
1a) V = ⅓ × π(3²) × 4 = 12π ≈ 37.7 m³
2b) Base area: 25 cm²
3Volume: ⅓ × 25 × 6 = 50 cm³

Your turn

Your turnA cone has r = 6 cm and slant s = 10 cm. Find h, then the volume (exact).
Hint: h = \(\sqrt{100 - 36}\) — Pythagoras, then the one-third rule.
✔ h = 8 → V = ⅓ × 36π × 8 = 96π cm³
📘 Your work: Exercise 4L
  • Direct questions, then find-h-first questions
  • h for volume, s for surface — write it at the top of the page

Surface area and volume of spheres

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • solve backwards for r

Key idea — the sphere formulas

Memorise the pairSA = 4πr² V = \(\dfrac{4}{3}\)πr³
Hemisphere traphalf a sphere's surface is 2πr² — but a SOLID hemisphere also has the flat circular face: + πr².

Worked example 8

Worked exampleFor a sphere of radius 6 cm find (exact + 1 d.p.): a) the surface area b) the volume.
1a) SA = 4π(36) = 144π ≈ 452.4 cm²
2b) V = \(\dfrac{4}{3}\)π(216) = 288π ≈ 904.8 cm³

Your turn

Your turnFind the total surface area of a SOLID hemisphere with radius 3 cm (exact).

Geometry review and congruent triangles

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • recall angle facts (parallel lines, triangles)
  • name the four congruence tests
  • justify congruence with a test

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
congruent ≅KON-groo-entexactly the same shape AND size
correspondingko-res-POND-ingmatching — the same position in each triangle
included angle—the angle BETWEEN the two named sides
SSS · SAS · AAS · RHS—the four congruence tests

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the four tests

Congruence testsSSS three sides · SAS two sides + included angle · AAS two angles + a matching side · RHS right angle + hypotenuse + side.
Matching marksthe dashes and arcs on the diagram tell you which parts are equal — read them like labels.

Worked example 10

Worked exampleTriangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE, BC = EF and ∠B = ∠E. Are they congruent? Which test?
1List the pairs: two sides equal, and the angle ∠B/∠E sits BETWEEN those sides.
2Test: SAS (side, included angle, side).
3Conclusion sentence: “△ABC ≅ △DEF (SAS)” — the test in brackets IS the justification.

Your turn

Your turnTwo right-angled triangles have equal hypotenuses and one other pair of equal sides. Congruent? Test?
Hint: right angle + hypotenuse + side…
✔ Yes — RHS. Write: “△… ≅ △… (RHS)”.
📘 Your work: Exercise 2A + 2B
  • Angle-fact warm-up, then congruence test identification
  • Every answer needs the test named in brackets
  • Order matters: △ABC ≅ △DEF matches A↔D, B↔E, C↔F

Similar figures

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • find and use a scale factor
  • know the area scale factor is k²

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
similar ~SIM-i-larsame shape, same angles — but a different size
scale factor kskayl FAK-torthe multiplier from one figure to the other
enlargement / reductionen-LARJ-mentk > 1 grows the shape; k < 1 shrinks it

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Worked example 11

Worked exampleRectangle A is 4 cm × 6 cm. Rectangle B is 6 cm × 9 cm. a) Show they are similar and give k. b) Compare the areas.
1Compare matching sides: 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 and 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 — same ratio → similar, k = 1.5.
2Areas: A: 24 cm²; B: 54 cm²
3Ratio of areas: 54 ÷ 24 = 2.25 = 1.5² — areas scale by k²!

Your turn

Your turnA photo 10 cm × 15 cm is enlarged with k = 3. Find the new size and how many times bigger the area is.
Hint: areas scale by k² = 9.
✔ 30 cm × 45 cm; the area is 9 times bigger.

LESSON 12 — SECTION 2E

Proving and applying similar triangles

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • prove triangles similar with the AA test
  • set up the ratio of matching sides
  • find missing lengths (river-width style problems)

Key idea — AA is enough

AA testTwo pairs of equal angles → the triangles are similar. (The third pair is automatic — angle sum!)
Then ratios\(\dfrac{\text{big} \text{side}}{\text{small} \text{side}}\) is the same for every matching pair. Set two ratios equal and solve.

Worked example 12

Worked exampleTwo triangles are similar (AA, marked θ). The small one has base 8 and height 5; the large has base 12 and height x. Find x.
1Similarity statement: the triangles are similar (AA).
2Scale factor from bases: k = 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5
3Apply to the height: x = 5 × 1.5 = 7.5
4Check: \(\dfrac{12}{8} = \dfrac{7.5}{5}\) ✔ same ratio

Your turn

Your turnA 1.6 m student casts a 2.4 m shadow. A tree beside her casts a 15 m shadow. How tall is the tree?
Hint: similar triangles (same sun angle): \(\dfrac{h}{1.6} = \dfrac{15}{2.4}\).
✔ h = 1.6 × 15 ÷ 2.4 = 10 m. Answer in words!
📘 Your work: Exercise 2E
  • Prove similar (AA) first, then find the length
  • Write the similarity statement BEFORE the ratio line
  • Real problems: shadows, ramps, river widths

  • use: angle at the centre = 2 × angle at the circumference
  • use: angle in a semicircle = 90°

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
chordkorda line joining two points on the circle
arcarka piece of the circle edge
subtendsub-TENDthe angle an arc “opens up” at a point
theoremTHEER-ema proven geometric fact

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the two star theorems

Theorem 1Angle at the centre = 2 × angle at the circumference (same arc).
Theorem 2The angle in a semicircle (on a diameter) is 90° — a special case: centre angle 180° → circumference angle 90°.

Worked example 13

Worked exampleIn the diagram, the angle at the centre O is 100°. Find θ, the angle at point P on the circle (same arc).
1Same arc subtends both angles.
2Theorem 1: centre = 2 × circumference → 100° = 2θ
3Answer: θ = 50°, with reason: “angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.”

Your turn

Your turnAB is a diameter and C is on the circle. ∠CAB = 35°. Find ∠ABC.
Hint: ∠ACB = 90° (semicircle), then angle sum.
✔ ∠ABC = 180 − 90 − 35 = 55° — reasons: “angle in a semicircle” + “angle sum of a triangle”.
