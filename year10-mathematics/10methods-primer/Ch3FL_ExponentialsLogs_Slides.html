LESSON 1 — SECTION 3F

Exponential relations and their graphs

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • recognise an exponential rule y = aˣ
  • sketch y = 2ˣ and label its features
  • identify the asymptote

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
exponentialex-po-NEN-shula rule where x is in the power: y = 2ˣ
basebayssthe number being powered — the 2 in y = 2ˣ
asymptoteAS-im-tohta line the graph gets closer and closer to but never touches
steepsteeprising very quickly

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the shape of y = 2ˣ

Key featuresy-intercept (0, 1) — because 2⁰ = 1.
Passes through (1, 2) — the base!
Asymptote: the x-axis (y = 0).
Left sideFor negative x the graph gets tiny (2⁻³ = ⅛) but never reaches 0 — that is what “asymptote” means.
Say it: “The graph approaches y = 0 but never touches it.”

Worked example 1

Worked exampleSketch y = 3ˣ. Label the y-intercept, one more point, and the asymptote.
1y-intercept: 3⁰ = 1 → (0, 1). Every y = aˣ passes through (0, 1).
2One more point: x = 1 → y = 3 → (1, 3).
3Asymptote: y = 0 — draw the curve hugging the x-axis on the left.
4Compare: 3ˣ climbs faster than 2ˣ for x > 0 (bigger base = steeper).

Your turn

Your turnSketch y = 2ˣ and y = 2⁻ˣ on the same axes. What transformation links them?
Hint: swap x for −x… which axis is the mirror?
✔ y = 2⁻ˣ is y = 2ˣ reflected in the y-axis. Both share (0, 1) and asymptote y = 0. 2⁻ˣ shows decay.
📘 Your work: Exercise 3F
  • Plot from a table first, then sketch directly
  • Always draw and label the asymptote — it is a feature, like a TP
  • Frame: “The graph approaches ___ but never touches it.”

LESSON 2 — SECTION 3F (TECHNOLOGY)

Translating exponential graphs

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • predict how +k and −k move the graph AND the asymptote
  • find x-intercepts when the graph is shifted down
  • check predictions with Desmos

Key idea — the asymptote moves too

Key ideay = 2ˣ + k slides the whole graph (and its asymptote) up or down by k.
New feature!Shift down and the graph now crosses the x-axis → an x-intercept appears.

Worked example 2

Worked exampleFor y = 2ˣ − 4, find the asymptote, y-intercept and x-intercept.
1Asymptote: y = 0 moves down 4 → y = −4.
2y-intercept: x = 0 → y = 1 − 4 = −3.
3x-intercept: 2ˣ − 4 = 0 → 2ˣ = 4 → x = 2.
4Check on the graph: curve crosses at (2, 0) ✔

Predict → check → correct

Desmos routine1️⃣ Predict asymptote + intercepts on your whiteboard → 2️⃣ type the rule → 3️⃣ if wrong, name the part you missed.
Speed round (60 s each)a) y = 2ˣ + 3   b) y = 3ˣ − 9   c) y = 2ˣ − 1
✔ a) asymptote y = 3, y-int 4, no x-int · b) asymptote y = −9, y-int −8, x-int x = 2 · c) asymptote y = −1, y-int 0 (through the origin!)

  • Finish 3F including translated graphs

LESSON 3 — SECTION 3G

Exponential growth and decay

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • write a growth or decay rule from a percentage rate
  • calculate future amounts
  • recognise growth (rate added) vs decay (rate subtracted)

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
initial amount A₀in-ISH-althe starting value — at time zero
growth rate r%grohththe percentage increase each time period
decayde-KAYdecreasing by a percentage each period — losing value
per annum (p.a.)per AN-umeach year

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the growth/decay rule

A = A₀ (1 + \(\dfrac{r}{100}\))ⁿ   growth A = A₀ (1 − \(\dfrac{r}{100}\))ⁿ   decay
Translate the percentagegrow 3% → multiply by 1.03 each year · lose 12% → multiply by 0.88 each year.
“Growing 3% each year means × 1.03, n times.”

Worked example 3

Worked exampleA town of 2000 people grows by 3% per year. Write the rule and find the population after 5 years.
1Rule: growth → A = 2000(1.03)ⁿ
2Substitute n = 5: A = 2000 × 1.03⁵
3Calculate: A = 2000 × 1.15927… ≈ 2318.5
4Answer in words: “After 5 years the population is about 2319 people.”

Your turn

Your turnA $30 000 car loses 12% of its value each year. Write the rule and find its value after 3 years (nearest dollar).
Hint: decay → multiply by 0.88 three times.
✔ V = 30000(0.88)ⁿ → V = 30000 × 0.88³ ≈ $20 444. “The car is worth about $20 444 after 3 years.”
📘 Your work: Exercise 3G
  • Write the rule BEFORE calculating — the rule earns marks
  • Growth or decay? Underline “increases/decreases” in the question
  • Answers in words with units ($, people, grams)

Ask for help in any language you like — sentence frame: “Can you explain … again, please?”

LESSON 4 — SECTION 3H

Compound interest

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use A = P(1 + r/100)ⁿ
  • find the interest earned (A − P)
  • compare compound and simple interest

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
principal PPRIN-si-palthe money you start with (invest or borrow)
interestIN-trestextra money earned (or charged)
compound interestKOM-powndinterest calculated on the principal AND past interest
simple interest—interest on the principal only — grows in a straight line

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — compound beats simple

FormulaA = P(1 + \(\dfrac{r}{100}\))ⁿ
Why it curvesEach year's interest joins the principal, so next year earns interest on MORE money. Same rule as 3G growth!
Interest earnedInterest = A − P (the question often asks for this, not A).

Worked example 4

Worked example$5000 is invested at 4% p.a. compound interest for 6 years. Find the final amount and the interest earned (nearest cent).
1Identify: P = 5000, r = 4, n = 6.
2Rule: A = 5000(1.04)⁶
3Calculate: A = 5000 × 1.26532 ≈ $6326.60
4Interest earned: 6326.60 − 5000 = $1326.60

Your turn

Your turn$8000 is invested at 6% p.a. compounding yearly for 4 years. Find the final amount (nearest cent).
Hint: A = 8000(1.06)⁴
✔ A = 8000 × 1.26248 ≈ $10 099.82
📘 Your work: Exercise 3H
  • Write P, r, n before the formula every time
  • “Interest earned” questions: remember to subtract P
  • Extension: compounding monthly → divide r by 12, multiply n by 12

LESSON 5 — SECTION 3I

Introducing logarithms

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • say what a logarithm asks
  • swap between index form and log form
  • evaluate simple logs without a calculator

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
logarithm (log)LOG-a-rith-umthe question “what power?” — log₂ 8 asks “2 to what power gives 8?”
log form—log₂ 8 = 3
index formIN-dex2³ = 8 — the same fact, written with a power
basebayssthe small number: log₂ has base 2

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — log and index are the same fact

logₐ x = y  ⟺  aʸ = x
Read it aloudlog₂ 8 = 3 → “2 to the power 3 gives 8”. The log is the power.
Anchorslogₐ a = 1 (a¹ = a)  ·  logₐ 1 = 0 (a⁰ = 1)
“log base ___ of ___ asks: ___ to what power gives ___?”

Worked example 5

Worked exampleEvaluate without a calculator: a) log₁₀ 1000   b) log₂ 16   c) log₅ \(\dfrac{1}{25}\)
1a) 10 to what power gives 1000? 10³ = 1000 → log₁₀ 1000 = 3
2b) 2 to what power gives 16? 2⁴ = 16 → log₂ 16 = 4
3c) 5 to what power gives 1/25? 5⁻² = 1/25 → log₅ (1/25) = −2 (negative indices: Chapter 3B!)

Your turn

Your turnEvaluate: a) log₃ 81   b) log₁₀ 0.01
Hint: 3⁴ = ? and 0.01 = 10⁻²
✔ a) log₃ 81 = 4   b) log₁₀ 0.01 = −2
📘 Your work: Exercise 3I
  • Swap between forms first, then evaluate
  • Say every log aloud as a “what power?” question
  • Calculator: the log button is base 10

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
logarithmic scale—a scale where each step multiplies by 10 (not adds 10)
magnitudeMAG-ni-tyoodsize — earthquake magnitude uses the Richter scale
pHpee-aychacidity scale — LOWER pH = more acidic, each step ×10
decibel (dB)DES-i-belloudness scale — also logarithmic

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — each step is ×10

Compare with a subtractionTwo values k steps apart on a log scale differ by a factor of 10ᵏ.
Magnitude 6 vs magnitude 4 → 10² = 100 times bigger amplitude.

Worked example 6

Worked exampleEarthquake A is magnitude 6.0; earthquake B is magnitude 4.0. How many times larger is A's amplitude?
1Difference in magnitude: 6 − 4 = 2 steps.
2Each step × 10: factor = 10² = 100.
3Answer in words: “A's amplitude is 100 times larger than B's.”

Your turn

Your turnLemon juice has pH 3; milk has pH 6. How many times more acidic is lemon juice?
Hint: 3 steps apart, and LOWER pH = MORE acidic.
✔ 10³ = 1000 times more acidic.

LESSON 7 — SECTION 3K (10A)

Laws of logarithms

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use the product, quotient and power laws
  • simplify log expressions to a single log
  • evaluate by combining logs

Key idea — three laws

logₐ x + logₐ y = logₐ(xy) logₐ x − logₐ y = logₐ \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) logₐ xⁿ = n logₐ x
They mirror the index lawsadd logs ↔ multiply numbers · subtract logs ↔ divide numbers · coefficient ↔ power. Same family as 3A!

Worked example 7

Worked exampleSimplify and evaluate: a) log₁₀ 4 + log₁₀ 25   b) log₂ 40 − log₂ 5
1a) Product law: log₁₀ 4 + log₁₀ 25 = log₁₀(4 × 25) = log₁₀ 100
2Evaluate: 10² = 100 → answer 2
3b) Quotient law: log₂ 40 − log₂ 5 = log₂(40 ÷ 5) = log₂ 8
4Evaluate: 2³ = 8 → answer 3

Your turn

Your turnSimplify and evaluate: 3 log₁₀ 2 + log₁₀ 12.5
Hint: power law first — 3 log₁₀ 2 = log₁₀ 2³ = log₁₀ 8.
✔ log₁₀ 8 + log₁₀ 12.5 = log₁₀ 100 = 2
📘 Your work: Exercise 3K
  • One law at a time — write which law you used on each line
  • Aim for a single log, then evaluate if possible
  • Common error: log(x + y) is NOT log x + log y

LESSON 8 — SECTION 3L (10A)

Solving exponential equations using logarithms

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • solve aˣ = b exactly and as a decimal
  • use the calculator log button (base 10)
  • solve real growth problems for the time n

Key idea — undo the power with a log

aˣ = b  →  x = logₐ b = \(\dfrac{\log _{1}_{0} b}{\log _{1}_{0} a}\)
CalculatorYour log button is base 10: type log(20) ÷ log(2) for log₂ 20.
Why logs?When x is in the power and b is not a nice power of a, logs are the only exact way out.

Worked example 8

Worked exampleSolve 2ˣ = 20, exact and to two decimal places.
1x is in the power and 20 is not a power of 2 → use logs.
2Exact: x = log₂ 20 = \(\dfrac{\log 20}{\log 2}\)
3Calculator: x = 1.30103 ÷ 0.30103 ≈ 4.32
4Check: 2⁴ = 16 and 2⁵ = 32, so x between 4 and 5 ✔

Worked example 9 — in context

Worked exampleA town of 2000 grows 3% per year: A = 2000(1.03)ⁿ. When will the population reach 4000 (i.e. double)?
1Equation: 2000(1.03)ⁿ = 4000 → (1.03)ⁿ = 2
2Logs: n = \(\dfrac{\log 2}{\log 1.03}\)
3Calculate: n ≈ 0.30103 ÷ 0.01284 ≈ 23.4
4Answer in words: “The population doubles during the 24th year (after about 23.4 years).”

Your turn

Your turnSolve 3ˣ = 50 to two decimal places.
Hint: x = log 50 ÷ log 3.
✔ x = 1.69897 ÷ 0.47712 ≈ 3.56 (check: 3³ = 27, 3⁴ = 81 ✔)
📘 Your work: Exercise 3L
  • Exact form first, then decimal — both may earn marks
  • Sanity check every answer between neighbouring powers
  • Context questions: answer in words with time units

Ask for help in any language you like — sentence frame: “Can you explain … again, please?”
