LESSON 1 — SECTION 7A

Exploring parabolas

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • name the key features of a parabola
  • read the turning point, axis of symmetry and intercepts from a graph
  • describe simple transformations of y = x²

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
parabolapa-RAB-o-lathe U-shaped graph of a quadratic
turning point (TP) / vertex—the highest or lowest point of the parabola
maximum / minimum—TP is a minimum if the graph opens up ∪, a maximum if it opens down ∩
axis of symmetryAK-sis of SIM-e-treethe vertical mirror line through the turning point
y-interceptWY IN-ter-septwhere the graph crosses the y-axis (x = 0)
x-interceptEKS IN-ter-septwhere the graph crosses the x-axis (y = 0)

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the features of y = x²

Key ideaEvery parabola has: a turning point, an axis of symmetry through it, a y-intercept, and 0, 1 or 2 x-intercepts.
SymmetryThe two halves are mirror images — points at equal distance from the axis have equal y-values.

Key idea — three simple changes

narrower (dilation)

moved up (translation)

flipped ∩ (reflection)

Say it: “y = −x² opens down, so the turning point is a maximum.”

Worked example 1

Worked exampleFor y = x² − 4, state: the turning point, the axis of symmetry, the y-intercept and the x-intercepts.
1Turning point: lowest point → (0, −4), a minimum.
2Axis of symmetry: the vertical line x = 0.
3y-intercept: at x = 0, y = −4.
4x-intercepts: solve x² − 4 = 0 → x = −2 and x = 2.

Your turn

Your turnFor y = −x² + 9, state the turning point (max or min?), the axis of symmetry and all intercepts.
Hint: the graph opens down (a = −1). Solve 9 − x² = 0 for the x-intercepts.
✔ TP (0, 9), a maximum · axis x = 0 · y-intercept 9 · x-intercepts x = −3 and x = 3

  • Sentence frame: “The turning point is a ___ because a is ___.”

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
vertex form—y = a(x − h)² + k — the turning point is (h, k)
translationtrans-LAY-shuna slide: left/right (h) or up/down (k)
dilationdy-LAY-shuna stretch: |a| > 1 narrower, |a| < 1 wider
reflectionre-FLEK-shuna flip: a < 0 flips the parabola upside-down ∩

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — vertex form

y = a(x − h)² + k  →  turning point (h, k)
Careful — the sign of hy = (x − 2)² + 1 → TP (2, 1).   y = (x + 3)² − 4 means (x − (−3))² − 4 → TP (−3, −4).
Sketch routine1️⃣ TP from (h, k) · 2️⃣ shape from a (∪ or ∩, wide or narrow) · 3️⃣ y-intercept: put x = 0 · 4️⃣ draw and label.

Worked example 2

Worked exampleSketch y = (x − 2)² + 1, labelling the turning point and y-intercept.
1Turning point: h = 2, k = 1 → TP (2, 1), minimum (a = 1 > 0).
2Shape: a = 1 → same width as y = x², opens up ∪.
3y-intercept: x = 0 → y = (−2)² + 1 = 5.
4Draw: smooth ∪ through (0, 5) with TP (2, 1); label everything.

Your turn

Your turnSketch y = −(x + 1)² + 4. Give the TP (max or min?), y-intercept and x-intercepts.
Hint: TP (−1, 4), maximum. For x-intercepts solve (x + 1)² = 4.
✔ TP (−1, 4) maximum · y-intercept 3 · x-intercepts x = −3 and x = 1

LESSON 4 — SECTION 7C

Sketching parabolas using factorisation

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • factorise a quadratic to find the x-intercepts
  • use symmetry to find the turning point
  • sketch with all features labelled

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
factoriseFAK-to-rizewrite as a product of brackets: x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2)
null factor lawnul FAK-tor lawif two things multiply to 0, one of them must be 0
rootsrootsanother word for the x-intercepts
midpointMID-poynthalfway between — the axis of symmetry is midway between the roots

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the factorised sketch routine

Four steps 1️⃣ y-intercept: put x = 0.
2️⃣ x-intercepts: factorise, then null factor law.
3️⃣ Axis of symmetry: x = midpoint of the two x-intercepts.
4️⃣ Turning point: substitute the axis x-value into the rule.
“The axis of symmetry is exactly halfway between the roots.”

Worked example 3

Worked exampleSketch y = x² − 2x − 8, labelling all features.
1y-intercept: x = 0 → y = −8.
2Factorise: y = (x − 4)(x + 2) → x-intercepts x = 4 and x = −2.
3Axis: midpoint of −2 and 4 → x = 1.
4Turning point: y = 1² − 2(1) − 8 = −9 → TP (1, −9), minimum.

Your turn

Your turnSketch y = x² − 6x + 8 with all features labelled.
Hint: factorise to (x − 2)(x − 4). Midpoint of 2 and 4 is 3.
✔ x-intercepts 2 and 4 · axis x = 3 · TP (3, −1) minimum · y-intercept 8
📘 Your work: Exercise 7C
  • Factorise → intercepts → axis → TP → sketch, every time in that order
  • Show the null factor law line in your working
  • Say it: “(x − 4)(x + 2) = 0, so x = 4 or x = −2.”

LESSON 5 — SECTION 7C (CONSOLIDATION)

Harder factorising cases

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • sketch when there is a common factor
  • sketch non-monic quadratics
  • find and fix common sketching errors

Worked example 4 — common factor

Worked exampleSketch y = 2x² − 8x.
1Factorise: y = 2x(x − 4) — take out the common factor 2x.
2x-intercepts: 2x = 0 or x − 4 = 0 → x = 0 and x = 4. (the y-intercept is also 0 here!)
3Axis: midpoint → x = 2.
4TP: y = 2(4) − 16 = −8 → (2, −8).

Error hunt

Find the error — talk with a partnerA student sketching y = (x − 3)(x + 1) wrote: “x-intercepts at x = −3 and x = 1.”
✔ Signs flipped! (x − 3) = 0 gives x = +3; (x + 1) = 0 gives x = −1. The bracket sign and the root sign are always opposite.
Check frame: “If the bracket is (x − 3), the intercept is x = 3, because 3 − 3 = 0.”

Sketching by completing the square

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • complete the square to reach vertex form
  • read the TP directly
  • sketch when the quadratic does not factorise nicely

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
perfect squarePER-fekt skwairan expression like (x + 2)² — a bracket squared
complete the square—rewrite x² + bx + c in the form (x + b/2)² + …
halvehahvdivide by 2 — “halve the coefficient of x”
exact / surd form—leave answers with √ — do not round

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the method

x² + bx = ( x + \(\dfrac{b}{2}\) )² − (\(\dfrac{b}{2}\))²
In wordsHalve the x-coefficient, write the bracket squared, then subtract the square of that half.
Why do it?It turns any quadratic into vertex form → the TP appears instantly.

Worked example 5

Worked exampleComplete the square for y = x² + 4x + 1, state the TP, and sketch.
1Halve 4 → 2: y = (x + 2)² − 2² + 1
2Simplify: y = (x + 2)² − 3
3TP: (−2, −3), minimum. Axis x = −2.
4y-intercept: x = 0 → y = 1. Sketch and label.

Your turn

Your turnComplete the square for y = x² − 6x + 7 and state the turning point.
Hint: halve −6 → −3. y = (x − 3)² − 9 + 7.
✔ y = (x − 3)² − 2 → TP (3, −2), minimum
📘 Your work: Exercise 7D
  • Complete the square first, then sketch
  • Watch the sign: halving −6 gives −3, and (x − 3)², not (x + 3)²

LESSON 7 — SECTION 7D (CONSOLIDATION)

x-intercepts in exact (surd) form

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • give x-intercepts in exact surd form
  • sketch with exact labels

Key idea — solving from vertex form

Key ideaFrom y = (x + 2)² − 3, the x-intercepts solve (x + 2)² = 3, so:

x + 2 = ±\(\sqrt{3}\)  →  x = −2 ± \(\sqrt{3}\)
Remember ±A square has two square roots — forgetting the negative root loses one intercept. (Surds: Chapter 4!)

Worked example 6

Worked exampleFind the exact x-intercepts of y = (x + 2)² − 3.
1Set y = 0: (x + 2)² − 3 = 0
2Isolate the square: (x + 2)² = 3
3± square root: x + 2 = ±\(\sqrt{3}\)
4Solve: x = −2 + \(\sqrt{3}\) ≈ −0.27 and x = −2 − \(\sqrt{3}\) ≈ −3.73 — give the exact form in your answer.

Your turn

Your turnFind the exact x-intercepts of y = (x − 1)² − 5.
Hint: (x − 1)² = 5 → x − 1 = ±\(\sqrt{5}\)
✔ x = 1 + \(\sqrt{5}\) and x = 1 − \(\sqrt{5}\) (exact form — no rounding)

LESSON 8 — SECTION 7E

Sketching using the quadratic formula and the discriminant

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • use Δ = b² − 4ac to predict how many x-intercepts
  • use the quadratic formula for x-intercepts
  • find the TP from x = −b/(2a)

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
quadratic formula—x = (−b ± \(\sqrt{\Delta}\)) / 2a — works for ANY quadratic
discriminant Δdis-KRIM-in-ant, DEL-taΔ = b² − 4ac — the part under the square root
irrationalir-RASH-on-ala number that is not a fraction — most surds are irrational

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — Δ counts the x-intercepts

Δ > 0 → two x-intercepts

Δ = 0 → one (just touches)

Δ < 0 → none

FormulasΔ = b² − 4ac x = \(\dfrac{- b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}\) axis: x = \(\dfrac{- b}{2a}\)

Worked example 7

Worked exampleSketch y = 2x² + 4x − 3 using the discriminant and quadratic formula. Round intercepts to 2 d.p.
1a = 2, b = 4, c = −3. Δ = 4² − 4(2)(−3) = 16 + 24 = 40 > 0 → two x-intercepts.
2Formula: x = \(\dfrac{- 4 \pm \sqrt{40}}{4}\) → x ≈ 0.58 and x ≈ −2.58.
3Axis: x = −4 ÷ (2×2) = −1.
4TP: y = 2(1) − 4 − 3 = −5 → (−1, −5). y-intercept −3. Sketch and label.

Your turn

Your turnUse Δ to decide how many x-intercepts y = x² + 2x + 5 has.
Hint: Δ = b² − 4ac with a = 1, b = 2, c = 5.
✔ Δ = 4 − 20 = −16 < 0 → no x-intercepts — see the graph: the whole parabola sits above the x-axis.
📘 Your work: Exercise 7E
  • Δ first — say how many intercepts BEFORE using the formula

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
intersectin-ter-SEKTcross — where two graphs meet
point of intersection—the (x, y) where they meet — it satisfies BOTH rules
tangentTAN-jenta line touching the curve at exactly one point
simultaneouslysy-mul-TAY-nee-us-leeboth equations true at the same time (Chapter 1!)

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — substitute, then Δ

MethodSet the rules equal (substitute the line into the parabola) → rearrange to ax² + bx + c = 0 → solve.
Δ counts the crossingsΔ > 0 → line cuts the parabola twice · Δ = 0 → tangent (one touch) · Δ < 0 → line misses.

Worked example 9

Worked exampleFind where y = x² intersects y = x + 2.
1Set equal: x² = x + 2
2Rearrange: x² − x − 2 = 0
3Factorise: (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0 → x = 2 or x = −1
4y-values from the line: (2, 4) and (−1, 1). Two points of intersection.

Your turn

Your turnShow that y = 2x is a tangent to y = x² + 1, and find the point of contact.
Hint: x² + 1 = 2x → x² − 2x + 1 = 0. What is Δ?
✔ Δ = 4 − 4 = 0 → exactly one solution: (x − 1)² = 0, x = 1 → tangent at (1, 2).

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning

average rate of change—the gradient of the straight line (chord) between two points
chordkorda straight line joining two points on a curve
perperfor each — “metres per second” = metres for each second

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — gradient of the chord

Key ideaaverage rate = \(\dfrac{\text{change} in y}{\text{change} in x}\)
On a curvethe average rate between two points = the gradient of the chord (dashed line) joining them.

Worked example 10

Worked exampleFor y = x², find the average rate of change from x = 1 to x = 3.
1y-values: at x = 1, y = 1; at x = 3, y = 9.
2Change in y: 9 − 1 = 8. Change in x: 3 − 1 = 2.
3Average rate: 8 ÷ 2 = 4 — y increases by 4 for each 1 that x increases (on average).

Your turn

Your turnFor y = x² − 2x, find the average rate of change from x = 0 to x = 2. What does your answer mean?
Hint: y(0) = 0 and y(2) = 0.
✔ (0 − 0) ÷ 2 = 0. The curve went down then up — the overall (average) change is zero.

Key idea — shrink the interval

Key ideaMake the second point slide toward the first: the chords (dashed) tilt toward the tangent (solid green).
So…instantaneous rate at a point = gradient of the tangent there = the value the average rates approach.

4The rates approach 2 → instantaneous rate at x = 1 is 2.

Modelling task — the netball shot

Task (pairs)A netball leaves a shooter's hands at height 2 m and follows h = −x² + 4x + 2 (h = height in m, x = horizontal distance in m).
a) How high is the ball at its peak, and how far along?
b) The ring is 3.05 m high, 4 m away. Does the model say the shot passes the ring height there?
c) What does the model ignore? (air, spin, the ring itself…)
✔ a) peak 6 m at x = 2 · b) at x = 4, h = 2 m < 3.05 m — the model says the ball is below ring height there · c) real shots have air resistance, spin, and the ball is not a point!
