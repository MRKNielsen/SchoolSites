LESSON 1 — SECTION 6A

Trigonometric ratios

🎯 Learning intentions — today I will…

  • name the three sides of a right-angled triangle
  • write the ratios sin θ, cos θ and tan θ
  • use a ratio to find an unknown side

🖩 Check now: calculator is in DEGREE (DEG) mode.

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
hypotenusehy-POT-en-yoosthe longest side — always opposite the right angle
oppositeOP-po-sitthe side across from the angle θ
adjacentad-JAY-sentthe side next to the angle θ (not the hypotenuse)

sin θsine thetaa ratio: opposite ÷ hypotenuse
cos θcoz theta / cosinea ratio: adjacent ÷ hypotenuse

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea 1 — naming the sides

RememberThe names opposite and adjacent depend on where the angle θ is.

The hypotenuse never changes — it is always opposite the right angle.
Say it: “The opposite side is across from θ. The adjacent side is next to θ.”

Key idea 2 — SOH CAH TOA

sin θ = OppositeHypotenuse cos θ = AdjacentHypotenuse tan θ = OppositeAdjacent
Memory helpSOH · CAH · TOA — Sine = Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent over Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite over Adjacent.

Worked example 1 — finding a side

Worked example A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm. The angle θ = 30°. Find the length x of the side opposite θ.
1Label the sides. x is the opposite; 10 is the hypotenuse.
2Choose the ratio. Opposite and Hypotenuse → SOH → use sin.
3Write the equation:   sin 30° = x10
4Solve:   x = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm
Sentence frame: “x is opposite the angle and 10 is the hypotenuse, so I use sine.”

Your turn

Your turnA ladder (hypotenuse) is 8 m long and makes an angle of 40° with the ground. Find the distance x from the wall to the bottom of the ladder (the side adjacent to 40°). Round to two decimal places.
Hint: Adjacent and Hypotenuse → CAH → cos 40° = x ÷ 8
✔ x = 8 × cos 40° ≈ 6.13 m

Key idea — when x is on the bottom

Key idea If  cos θ = adjacentx  then we swap:  x = adjacentcos θ

Worked exampleFind x, correct to two decimal places: the side opposite 35° is 4 m, and x is the side adjacent to 35°.

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — undoing a ratio

Key idea If sin θ = k  then  θ = sin⁻¹(k)
If cos θ = k  then  θ = cos⁻¹(k)
If tan θ = k  then  θ = tan⁻¹(k)
CalculatorPress SHIFT or 2nd then sin to get sin⁻¹.
Say it: “theta equals inverse sine of k”.

Worked example 3

Worked exampleIn a right-angled triangle the side opposite θ is 3 and the side adjacent is 4. Find θ correct to the nearest degree.
1Label: opposite = 3, adjacent = 4 → TOA → tan.
2Write: tan θ = 34
3Undo tan: θ = tan⁻¹34 = tan⁻¹(0.75)
4Calculate: θ ≈ 36.87° ≈ 37° (nearest degree)

Your turn

Your turnFind θ to the nearest degree if cos θ = 59.
Hint: θ = cos⁻¹(5 ÷ 9)
✔ θ = cos⁻¹(0.5556…) ≈ 56°

Key idea — elevation and depression

Key ideaBoth angles are measured from the horizontal — never from the vertical.
Useful factOn the same diagram, the angle of elevation = the angle of depression (alternate angles in parallel lines).

Worked example 4

Worked exampleFrom the top of a 25 m cliff, a boat is seen at an angle of depression of 12°. How far is the boat from the base of the cliff, to one decimal place?
1Draw a right-angled triangle. Height = 25 (opposite the 12° angle at the boat — alternate angles).
2Choose the ratio: opposite = 25, adjacent = d → tan.
3Write: tan 12° = 25d  →  d = 25tan 12°
4Calculate: d ≈ 117.6 m
5Answer in words: “The boat is about 117.6 m from the base of the cliff.”

Your turn

Your turnHa stands 20 m from a tree. The angle of elevation to the top is 38°. Find the height of the tree, to one decimal place.
Hint: height is opposite, 20 m is adjacent → h = 20 × tan 38°
✔ h = 20 tan 38° ≈ 15.6 m. In words: “The tree is about 15.6 m tall.”

Problem-solving routine

Every word problem — 4 steps 1️⃣ Read — circle numbers, underline the question.
2️⃣ Draw a right-angled triangle and label it.
3️⃣ Solve with SOH CAH TOA.
4️⃣ Answer in words, with units.
“First I draw a triangle. The angle of elevation / depression is at ___. The unknown side is ___.”

Key idea — reading bearings

Key ideaStart at north. Turn clockwise. Write three digits: 045°T, 090°T, 225°T.
Check yourselfE = 090°T · S = 180°T · W = 270°T · NW = 315°T
“The bearing of B from A is measured at A, starting from north.”

Worked example 5

Worked exampleA ship sails 10 km on a bearing of 120°T. How far east and how far south has it travelled? (1 d.p.)
1Draw: north line at the start point; turn 120° clockwise; draw the 10 km path.
2Triangle: the angle between the path and the east direction is 120° − 90° = 30°.
3East: east = 10 cos 30° ≈ 8.7 km
4South: south = 10 sin 30° = 5.0 km
5Answer in words: “The ship is about 8.7 km east and 5.0 km south of its start.”

Your turn

Your turnA hiker walks 5 km on a bearing of 060°T. How far north and how far east did she walk? (2 d.p.)
Hint: angle from north = 60° → north = 5 cos 60°, east = 5 sin 60°
✔ north = 5 cos 60° = 2.50 km; east = 5 sin 60° ≈ 4.33 km

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning

SASside-angle-sideyou know 2 sides + the included angle → find the 3rd side
SSSside-side-sideyou know 3 sides → find an angle
obtuseob-TYOOSan angle bigger than 90° — its cosine is negative

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — the cosine rule

c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C cos C = a² + b² − c²2ab
Which rule?Opposite pair known → sine rule.   SAS or SSS → cosine rule.
CarefulIf cos C is negative, C is obtuse — that is OK! Your calculator handles it.

Worked example 8

Worked exampleTwo sides of a triangle are 7 m and 9 m with an included angle of 62°. Find the third side c, to two decimal places.
1Recognise: two sides + included angle = SAS → cosine rule.
2Write: c² = 7² + 9² − 2 × 7 × 9 × cos 62°
3Calculate the pieces: c² = 49 + 81 − 126 cos 62° ≈ 130 − 59.16 = 70.84
4Square root: c = √70.84 ≈ 8.42 m

Your turn

Your turnA triangle has sides 5, 7 and 10. Find the largest angle, to one decimal place.
Hint: the largest angle is opposite the longest side (10). cos θ = 5² + 7² − 10²2 × 5 × 7
✔ cos θ = −26 ÷ 70 ≈ −0.3714 → θ ≈ 111.8° (obtuse, as expected — cos was negative)

🗣 New words first

Word / symbolSay itMeaning
unit circleYOO-nita circle with radius 1, centre (0, 0)
quadrantKWOD-rantone quarter of the plane — numbered 1 to 4, anticlockwise
signsyne (like “sign”)positive (+) or negative (−) — careful: sounds like “sine”!
acutea-KYOOTan angle less than 90°

✏ Copy new words into your maths glossary. Write the word in your home language too.

Key idea — P(cos θ, sin θ)

Key ideaFor any angle θ, the point P on the unit circle is
P = (cos θ, sin θ).

So cos θ is the x-coordinate and sin θ is the y-coordinate.
Signs by quadrantQ1: All positive · Q2: Sin positive · Q3: Tan positive · Q4: Cos positive

Worked example 10

Worked exampleState whether each is positive or negative, then write cos 150° using an acute angle.
1cos 150°: 150° is in Quadrant 2 → only sin is positive → cos 150° is negative.
2sin 220°: 220° is in Quadrant 3 → only tan is positive → sin 220° is negative.
3tan 300°: 300° is in Quadrant 4 → only cos is positive → tan 300° is negative.
4Acute angle: 150° is 30° from 180°, so cos 150° = −cos 30°.

Your turn

Your turn(a) Write sin 160° in terms of an acute angle. (b) Is tan 200° positive or negative?
Hint: 160° is in Q2 (sin positive); 200° is in Q3 (tan positive).
✔ (a) sin 160° = sin 20°   (b) tan 200° is positive

Read the graphsin θ is the y-coordinate of P on the unit circle. Max = 1 at 90°, min = −1 at 270°, zero at 0°, 180°, 360°.

Key idea — the cosine graph

Comparecos θ starts at 1. Zero at 90° and 270°. Min = −1 at 180°. Same shape as sine, shifted 90°.
