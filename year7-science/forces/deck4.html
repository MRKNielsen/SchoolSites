What does a rocket actually push against?

Part B · Theory

Newton’s First Law — the law of inertia

First Law

An object at rest stays at rest, and a moving object keeps moving at the same speed in the same direction — unless an unbalanced force acts on it.

Inertia

Inertia is how much an object resists a change to its motion. More mass = more inertia. A full shopping trolley is harder to start, stop and steer than an empty one.

So why do things on Earth stop?

A rolling ball stops because friction and air resistance slow it down. Take those away and it would roll on forever.

Booklet pp.32–33 · §4.1 · Worked Example 4.1

Part B · Theory

I do At rest stays at rest

Worked Example 4.1

A coin sits on a playing card on top of a glass. The card is flicked quickly sideways. Why does the coin drop into the glass instead of flying off with the card?

  1. 1 Before the flick: the coin is at rest. Its weight pulls down and the card pushes up — balanced.
  2. 2 The flick: the big force acts on the card, not the coin. Only a tiny bit of friction acts on the coin, for a very short time.
  3. ✓ The coin’s inertia keeps it where it is. With the card gone nothing holds it up, so its weight is unbalanced and it falls into the glass.

Booklet pp.32–33 · §4.1 · Worked Example 4.2

Part B · Theory

I do Moving keeps moving

Worked Example 4.2

A car travelling at 50 km/h crashes into a wall and stops suddenly. What happens to a passenger who isn’t wearing a seatbelt? How does a seatbelt help?

  1. 1 The crash: the wall puts a huge unbalanced force on the car, so the car stops.
  2. 2 Inertia: nothing has pushed on the passenger yet, so they keep moving forwards at 50 km/h — until they hit the dashboard or windscreen.
  3. ✓ A seatbelt supplies the force that slows the passenger down with the car, spread across the strong bones of the chest and hips.

Part C · Practice

You do Explain it with the First Law

You are standing on a train. It suddenly starts moving forwards. Which way do you lurch? Use the word inertia.

Your inertia keeps you at rest while the floor moves forwards under you.

You lurch backwards until your feet (or a handrail) give you an unbalanced force forwards.

Why is it harder to stop a wheelbarrow full of soil than an empty one, even at the same speed?

The full wheelbarrow has more mass, so it has more inertia.

It resists a change to its motion more, so you need a bigger force to stop it.

A curling stone slides a long way on smooth ice, but stops quickly on concrete. Explain.

On ice there is very little friction, so there is almost no unbalanced force to slow it down — it keeps going.

Concrete gives much more friction: a bigger unbalanced force that stops it quickly.

Part B · Theory

Newton’s Third Law — forces come in pairs

Third Law

For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force. You can’t push on something without it pushing back on you.

How to find the reaction force

Write the action as “A pushes B”, then swap the names: “B pushes A”. The reaction is the same size, in the opposite direction, on the other object, and the same type of force.

Booklet pp.33–34 · §4.2 · Worked Example 4.3

Part B · Theory

I do Why don’t the two forces cancel?

Different objects

Forces only cancel when they act on the same object. An action–reaction pair always acts on two different objects, so they can never cancel.

When two skaters push apart, each skater has one unbalanced force on them — so both skaters move, in opposite directions.

Worked Example 4.3

A student on roller skates pushes against a wall with a force of 40 N. Describe the reaction force. What happens to the student?

  1. 1 Action: the student pushes on the wall, 40 N, towards the wall.
  2. 2 Reaction: the wall pushes on the student, 40 N, away from the wall.
  3. ✓ That force acts on the student and the skates have very little friction, so it is unbalanced: the student rolls backwards, away from the wall.

Booklet pp.35–37 · Part C · like Tasks 4.1 and 4.9–4.14

Part C · Practice

You do Find the reaction force

A kayaker’s paddle pushes the water backwards with 60 N. Describe the reaction force: what it is, its size and its direction.

Swap the names: the water pushes the paddle (and the kayak).

60 N, forwards

A gymnast jumps down hard onto a springboard. What is the reaction force, and what does it do to the gymnast?

Action: the gymnast pushes down on the springboard.

Reaction: the springboard pushes the gymnast up with the same size force — launching her into the air.

When a cannon fires a cannonball forwards, the cannon rolls backwards. Explain why.

The cannon pushes the cannonball forwards.

The cannonball pushes the cannon backwards with the same size force, so the cannon recoils.

First Law or Third Law?  (a) a book slides across the dashboard when a car turns  (b) a jet engine pushes gas backwards and the plane moves forwards  (c) a firework shoots upwards

(a) First — the book’s inertia keeps it going straight while the car turns

(b) Third   (c) Third — the burning gas is pushed down, so it pushes the firework up

Part D · Above level

We do Going further

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: use both laws together, and judge someone’s reasoning.

Kai stands still on the floor. He says: “My weight and the floor pushing up on me are a Third Law pair.” Is Kai right? Name the true reaction to each force.

No. Both forces act on Kai (the same object). They are a balanced pair, which is why he stays still.

Reaction to his weight (Earth pulls Kai down): Kai pulls the Earth up.

Reaction to the floor pushing Kai up: Kai pushes the floor down.

A truck and a small car crash head-on. Which one feels the bigger force? Which one has its motion changed more? Explain.

Third Law: the forces on the truck and the car are the same size.

The car has much less mass, so less inertia — its motion changes far more than the truck’s.

Lesson 4 · Recap

What we covered

  • First Law: without an unbalanced force, an object at rest stays at rest and a moving object keeps moving the same way
  • Inertia is resistance to a change in motion — more mass, more inertia
  • Things on Earth stop because of friction and air resistance
  • Third Law: every action force has an equal and opposite reaction
  • Action and reaction act on different objects, so they never cancel
Exit question

A rower’s oars push the water backwards. Name the reaction force, and say which law explains why the boat moves.

AnswerThe water pushes the oars (and so the boat) forwards, with the same size force — Newton’s Third Law.
