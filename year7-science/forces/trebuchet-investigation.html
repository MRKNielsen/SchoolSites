The Trebuchet Investigation

How does the mass of the counterweight change the throw? Groups launch a desktop trebuchet with five different counterweights, measure how far each launch goes, and use a wireless sensor strapped to the arm to see how fast it swings.

Why this investigation changed
The earlier version tried to measure gravity with g = 2h ÷ t². That formula only works for something dropped or launched exactly sideways, and a trebuchet throws upwards — so students got about 1.4 m/s² however carefully they worked. Now students change one thing, the counterweight, and measure what it does to the range and to the speed of the arm, which links straight back to energy and levers. Measuring g survives only in Part D, as a drop test of the sensor — where the formula does work, because the sensor starts from rest.
