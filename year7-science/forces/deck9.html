Part B · Theory

Simple machines

A simple machine changes the size or the direction of a force, to make a job easier. There are six.

No free energy

A simple machine never gives you more energy than you put in. If it lets you push with less force, you must push through a longer distance.

Is Sam right?

Sam says: “A ramp gives you free energy, because you push with less force.”

AnswerNo. With less force you must push the load a longer distance, so you put in at least as much energy as you get out. A ramp trades distance for force — it never creates energy.

Part B · Theory

I do The parts of a lever

The law of the lever

A lever balances when  effort × effort arm = load × load arm.

Worked Example 9.1

A 300 N child sits 2 m from the pivot of a seesaw. Where must a 400 N child sit on the other side to balance it?

  1. 1 Law of the lever: \(300 \times 2 = 400 \times d\)
  2. 2 \(600 = 400 \times d\),  so \(d = 600 \div 400\)
  3. ✓ \(d = 1.5\ \text{m}\) — the heavier child sits closer to the pivot.

Booklet p.97 · §9.3 · Worked Example 9.2

Part B · Theory

I do Mechanical advantage (MA)

Mechanical advantage

\(\text{MA} = \dfrac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}} = \dfrac{\text{effort arm}}{\text{load arm}}\)

  • MA bigger than 1: the lever multiplies your force.
  • MA smaller than 1: you lose force, but gain speed and distance.
Worked Example 9.2

A lever has an effort arm of 2.0 m and a load arm of 0.5 m. Find its MA, and the load a 100 N effort can lift.

  1. 1 MA: \(2.0 \div 0.5 = 4\)
  2. ✓ Load = MA × effort = \(4 \times 100 = 400\ \text{N}\)

We do Find the mechanical advantage

We do 9.2

A lever has an effort arm of 1.8 m and a load arm of 0.6 m.

(a) Find its mechanical advantage.

(b) What load can a 150 N effort lift?

Write your working in the We do 9.2 box in your booklet.

  1. 1 (a) \(\text{MA} = \text{effort arm} \div \text{load arm} = 1.8 \div 0.6 = 3\)
  2. 2 (b) \(\text{load} = \text{MA} \times \text{effort} = 3 \times 150\)
  3. ✓ Load \(= 450\ \text{N}\)

Booklet p.98 · §9.4

Part B · Theory

The three classes of lever

Levers are sorted by which part is in the middle.

Memory aid: F–L–E, 1–2–3

Class 1: Fulcrum in the middle
Class 2: Load in the middle
Class 3: Effort in the middle

Class 2 levers always multiply force (MA > 1). Class 3 levers always multiply speed (MA < 1).

Booklet p.99 · §9.5 · Worked Example 9.3

Part B · Theory

I do The trebuchet is a Class 1 lever

The pivot is between the counterweight (the effort, on the short arm) and the projectile (the load, on the long arm). So its MA is less than 1: it gives away force to gain speed.

Worked Example 9.3

A trebuchet has a short arm of 0.4 m and a long arm of 2.0 m. Find its MA, and how many times faster the end of the long arm moves than the counterweight.

  1. 1 MA = effort arm ÷ load arm \(= 0.4 \div 2.0 = 0.2\) — less than 1
  2. ✓ Speed: long arm ÷ short arm \(= 2.0 \div 0.4 = 5\), so the arm tip moves 5 times as fast.

Part D · Above level

We do Going further

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: use the law of the lever to design and explain.

Your forearm is a Class 3 lever with the elbow as the fulcrum. Your biceps pulls 5 cm from the elbow. You hold a 14 N ball 35 cm from the elbow. Find (a) the MA (b) the force the biceps must provide.

(a) MA \(= 5 \div 35 \approx 0.14\) — less than 1

(b) Law of the lever: \(\text{effort} \times 5 = 14 \times 35 = 490\), so effort \(= 490 \div 5\)

98 N — seven times the weight of the ball, but your hand moves seven times as fast.

You need to lift a 900 N log with a 2.0 m bar, but you can push with at most 300 N. Where should the fulcrum go?

MA needed \(= 900 \div 300 = 3\), so the effort arm must be 3 × the load arm.

Split the 2.0 m bar into 3 + 1 = 4 equal parts: load arm \(= 2.0 \div 4 = 0.5\ \text{m}\), effort arm \(= 1.5\ \text{m}\)

Put the fulcrum 0.5 m from the log.

Lesson 9 · Recap

What we covered

  • A simple machine changes the size or direction of a force — there are six
  • A lever turns on a fulcrum; the effort is your push and the load is what it moves
  • Balanced when effort × effort arm = load × load arm
  • MA = load ÷ effort = effort arm ÷ load arm. MA > 1 multiplies force; MA < 1 multiplies speed
  • Class 1, 2, 3 = fulcrum, load, effort in the middle. The trebuchet is Class 1, with MA < 1
Exit question

A lever has an effort arm of 1.5 m and a load arm of 0.5 m. What is its MA? Does it multiply force or speed?

AnswerMA \(= 1.5 \div 0.5 = 3\). It is bigger than 1, so it multiplies force.
