Booklet p.29 · §4.1

Concept · The lever

Every lever has three parts

Definition

A lever is a rigid bar that turns around a fixed point — the fulcrum. The effort is the force you put in; the load is the force you get out. The distance from the fulcrum to each force is its arm.

Booklet p.29 · §4.1

Concept · Mechanical advantage

Multiply force — or multiply speed

Mechanical advantage

\(\text{MA} = \text{load force} \div \text{effort force} = \text{effort-arm length} \div \text{load-arm length}\)

\(\text{MA} > 1\) means the lever multiplies your force. \(\text{MA} < 1\) means you gain speed or distance instead — exactly what a trebuchet does.

Booklet p.30 · Worked Example 4.1

Work through with the class

I do Worked example — mechanical advantage

How much load can a small effort lift?

A lever has an effort arm of 2.0 m and a load arm of 0.5 m. Find the MA, then the load a 100 N effort can lift.

  1. 1 MA \(= \text{effort arm} \div \text{load arm} = 2.0 \div 0.5\).
  2. 2 \(\text{MA} = 4\) — the lever multiplies force four times.
  3. ✓ Load \(= \text{MA} \times \text{effort} = 4 \times 100 = 400\ \text{N}\).

Booklet p.30 · Q4.1

Your turn

You do Now you try — balance the seesaw

Where must the child sit?

A 600 N person sits 2 m from the pivot of a seesaw. How far from the pivot must a 400 N child sit to balance it?

  1. 1 To balance: \(\text{force} \times \text{distance}\) must be equal on both sides.
  2. 2 Set them equal: \(600 \times 2 = 400 \times d\) → \(1200 = 400 d\).
  3. ✓ \(d = 1200 \div 400 = 3\ \text{m}\) from the pivot.

Booklet p.31 · §4.2

Concept · Class 1 lever

The trebuchet is a Class 1 lever

Key idea

The pivot sits between the counterweight (effort) and the projectile (load) — a Class 1 lever. The short arm holds the heavy counterweight; the long arm flings the projectile fast and far.

Booklet p.31 · Q4.2, Q4.3

Your turn

You do Trebuchet mechanical advantage

A trade-off, not a free lunch

A trebuchet has a long arm of 3.0 m (projectile) and a short arm of 0.5 m (counterweight). Find the MA. If the counterweight exerts 500 N, what force acts on the projectile?

  1. 1 MA \(= \text{effort arm} \div \text{load arm} = 0.5 \div 3.0 \approx 0.17\).
  2. 2 Force on projectile \(= \text{MA} \times \text{effort} = 0.17 \times 500 \approx 83\ \text{N}\).
  3. ✓ \(\text{MA} < 1\), so the projectile feels less force — but the long arm tip moves much faster. Force is traded for speed.

Reveal the steps — why a smaller force still launches the ball fast

Booklet p.32 · Q4.4 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension · Concept

Why a longer arm launches faster

The idea

Both arms sweep the same angle in the same time, but the tip of the longer arm travels a longer arc (\(\text{arc} = r \times \theta\)). Longer arc in the same time means a higher tip speed — so the projectile launches faster, even though the counterweight falls the same distance.

Year 8 extension · connecting forward

We do From arm length to launch speed

Use \(\text{arc} = r \times \theta\), then \(\text{speed} = \text{arc} \div \text{time}\)

A trebuchet's long arm is 3.0 m. In a launch it sweeps through 2.0 radians in 0.15 s. Find the speed of the arm tip — and compare it to a 1.0 m arm.

  1. 1 Arc length of the tip: \(\text{arc} = r \times \theta = 3.0 \times 2.0 = 6.0\ \text{m}\).
  2. 2 Tip speed: \(v = \text{arc} \div \text{time} = 6.0 \div 0.15 = 40\ \text{m/s}\).
  3. ✓ A 1.0 m arm gives \(\text{arc} = 2.0\ \text{m} \;\Rightarrow\; v = 13.3\ \text{m/s}\) — three times slower. Longer arm, faster launch.

The arm length you choose sets the launch speed — and the range

Section 4 · Recap

What we covered

  • A lever turns about a fulcrum, with an effort in and a load out
  • \(\text{MA} = \text{load} \div \text{effort} = \text{effort arm} \div \text{load arm}\)
  • \(\text{MA} > 1\) multiplies force; \(\text{MA} < 1\) multiplies speed
  • Levers come in three classes set by what's in the middle (fulcrum, load or effort)
  • The trebuchet is a Class 1 lever with a long arm — trading force for a fast launch
Exit question

A lever has a 1.5 m effort arm and a 0.5 m load arm. What is its MA — and does it multiply force or speed?
