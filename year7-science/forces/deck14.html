Part B · Theory

Launching at an angle

The launch speed is shared between two directions:

  • up — decides how long the ball stays in the air
  • along — decides how far it goes each second
The pattern

45° goes furthest. Complementary angles (30° and 60°, 15° and 75°) land in the same place.

Booklet p.151 · §14.2 · Worked Example 14.1

Part B · Theory

I do The maximum range

Range on flat ground (no air)

\[R = \frac{v^2 \times \sin(2\theta)}{g} \qquad R_{\max} = \frac{v^2}{g} \text{ at } 45^\circ\]

\(v\) = launch speed (m/s), \(\theta\) = launch angle, \(g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\)

Worked Example 14.1

A ball is launched at 20 m/s. Find its maximum range on flat ground.

  1. 1 Maximum range is at 45°, so use \(R_{\max} = v^2 \div g\)
  2. 2 \(R_{\max} = (20 \times 20) \div 9.8 = 400 \div 9.8\)
  3. ✓ \(R_{\max} \approx 40.8\ \text{m}\)

I do Two angles, one landing spot

Worked Example 14.2

A ball launched at 25° lands 6.6 m away. Which other launch angle, at the same speed, lands in the same place? Which of the two keeps the ball in the air longer?

  1. 1 Same range: the angles must add to 90°, so \(90 - 25 = 65^\circ\)
  2. 2 The 65° launch goes much higher.
  3. ✓ 65° lands in the same place and stays in the air longer.

Booklet pp.151–152 · §14.2 · Worked Example 14.3

Part B · Theory

I do Using the range equation

Worked Example 14.3

A ball is launched at 12 m/s at 30°. Find its range. Use \(\sin 60^\circ = 0.87\).

  1. 1 Double the angle: \(2 \times 30^\circ = 60^\circ\), so \(\sin(2\theta) = 0.87\)
  2. 2 \(R = (12 \times 12 \times 0.87) \div 9.8 = 125.3 \div 9.8\)
  3. ✓ \(R \approx 12.8\ \text{m}\)

Booklet p.153 · §14.3

Part B · Theory

Why real launches are set lower than 45°

Launch height

A stone released high above the ground has extra time to fall, so a flatter launch, with more sideways speed, goes further.

Air resistance

The air slows the stone the whole way. A steep, high path spends longer in the air, so it loses more.

Speed

People (and some machines) launch faster at a flatter angle — like the long jumper.

Discuss

Our trebuchet releases its projectile well above the floor. Should we set it to release above or below 45°?

Extension 14.11

A trebuchet releases a stone at 18 m/s.

(a) Find the maximum range on flat ground, ignoring air resistance.

(b) The stone actually lands 26 m away. Give two reasons why the real range is shorter.

  1. 1 (a) \(R_{\max} = (18 \times 18) \div 9.8 = 324 \div 9.8 \approx 33\ \text{m}\)
  2. 2 (b) Air resistance slows the stone the whole way.
  3. ✓ The release angle may not be 45°, and the sling may not release at the exact moment or full speed.

Lesson 14 · Recap

What we covered

  • The launch angle shares the speed between up (time in the air) and along
  • On flat ground with no air, 45° gives the maximum range: \(R_{\max} = v^2 \div g\)
  • Complementary angles (adding to 90°) give the same range
  • \(R = v^2 \times \sin(2\theta) \div g\)
  • Launch height, air resistance and speed make real launches best below 45°
Exit question

Doubling the launch speed from 10 m/s to 20 m/s multiplies the maximum range by how much?

Answer\(100 \div 9.8 \approx 10.2\ \text{m}\) and \(400 \div 9.8 \approx 40.8\ \text{m}\) — four times, because the speed is squared.
