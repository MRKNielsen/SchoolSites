Part B · Theory

I do Turning effects

Turning effect (moment)

\(\text{turning effect} = \text{force} \times \text{distance from the pivot}\)

in newton metres (N m)

A lever balances when the total turning effect one way equals the total the other way. With two or more forces on one side, add their turning effects.

Worked Example 11.1

On one side of a seesaw, a 300 N child sits 2.0 m from the pivot and a 200 N child sits 1.0 m from the pivot. Where must a 400 N child sit on the other side to balance it?

  1. 1 Left side: \(300 \times 2.0 + 200 \times 1.0 = 600 + 200 = 800\ \text{N m}\)
  2. 2 Right side: \(400 \times d = 800\)
  3. ✓ \(d = 800 \div 400 = 2.0\ \text{m}\)

Arcs and tip speed

Every point on a turning arm turns through the same angle in the same time. A point far from the pivot goes round a bigger circle, so it covers a longer arc — and moves faster.

Arc length and speed

\(\text{arc} = \dfrac{\text{angle}}{360} \times 2 \times \pi \times r\)   (use \(\pi \approx 3.14\))

\(\text{average speed} = \text{arc} \div \text{time}\)

\(\dfrac{\text{tip speed}}{\text{short-end speed}} = \dfrac{\text{long arm}}{\text{short arm}}\)

Booklet pp.122–123 · Worked Examples 11.2–11.3

Part B · Theory

I do How fast does the tip move?

Worked Example 11.2

A trebuchet’s long arm is 2.0 m. During a launch it sweeps through 90° in 0.25 s. Find the arc travelled by the tip, and its average speed.

  1. 1 Arc: \(\dfrac{90}{360} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 2.0 = 0.25 \times 12.56 = 3.14\ \text{m}\)
  2. ✓ Speed: \(3.14 \div 0.25 \approx 12.6\ \text{m/s}\)
Worked Example 11.3

The same trebuchet has a short arm of 0.4 m. How fast does the counterweight end move?

  1. 1 Ratio: \(2.0 \div 0.4 = 5\), so the tip moves 5 times as fast
  2. ✓ Speed: \(12.6 \div 5 \approx 2.5\ \text{m/s}\)

Part B · Theory

I do Energy sets the limit

The arms share out the speed, but the energy all comes from the falling counterweight. The stone can never get more energy than the counterweight loses — and some is always wasted.

From counterweight to launch speed

\(\text{GPE lost} = m \times g \times h\)  (counterweight)

\(\text{KE of stone} = \text{efficiency} \times \text{GPE lost}\)

\(v = \sqrt{2 \times \text{KE} \div m}\)  (stone)

Worked Example 11.4

A 20 kg counterweight falls 0.8 m. The trebuchet is 25% efficient and the stone has a mass of 0.2 kg. Find the launch speed. Use g = 9.8 N/kg.

  1. 1 GPE lost: \(20 \times 9.8 \times 0.8 = 156.8\ \text{J}\)
  2. 2 KE of stone: \(0.25 \times 156.8 = 39.2\ \text{J}\)
  3. 3 \(v^2 = 2 \times 39.2 \div 0.2 = 392\)
  4. ✓ \(v = \sqrt{392} \approx 19.8\ \text{m/s}\)

Part D · Challenge

We do Check it, combine it

The hardest questions in this extension: check a claim, then combine turning effects with weight.

Mia says her model trebuchet, with a 1 kg counterweight that falls 0.8 m, launched a 0.04 kg ball at 22 m/s. Is that possible?

Energy available: \(1 \times 9.8 \times 0.8 = 7.84\ \text{J}\)

Energy needed: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.04 \times 22^2 = 9.68\ \text{J}\)

Impossible — the ball would need more energy than the counterweight gave up, even at 100% efficiency.

A trebuchet arm is held level. A 20 kg counterweight hangs 0.4 m from the pivot. The arm’s own 80 N weight acts 0.5 m from the pivot on the long side. A trigger rope pulls down 2.0 m from the pivot on the long side. Find the force in the rope.

Short side: \(20 \times 9.8 \times 0.4 = 78.4\ \text{N m}\)

Long side: \(80 \times 0.5 + F \times 2.0 = 78.4\)

\(F \times 2.0 = 38.4\), so \(F = 19.2\ \text{N}\)

19.2 N

Part D · Challenge

We do Arcs and where the energy goes

Combine arcs with speed, and track every joule.

An arm turns through 120° in 0.5 s. Its long arm is 3.0 m and its short arm 0.5 m. Find the tip speed and the counterweight-end speed. Check their ratio.

Tip: \(\tfrac{120}{360} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 3.0 = 6.28\ \text{m}\), so \(6.28 \div 0.5 \approx 12.6\ \text{m/s}\)

Short end: \(\tfrac{120}{360} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 0.5 \approx 1.05\ \text{m}\), so \(1.05 \div 0.5 \approx 2.1\ \text{m/s}\)

\(12.6 \div 2.1 = 6\), the same as \(3.0 \div 0.5\)

A counterweight gives up 300 J. The stone gets 75 J and the moving arm and counterweight keep 150 J. The rest becomes thermal energy and sound. How much is that, and what is the efficiency?

Thermal and sound: \(300 - 75 - 150 = 75\ \text{J}\)

Efficiency: \(75 \div 300 \times 100\% = 25\%\)

75 J; 25% efficient — on a Sankey diagram the stone’s arrow is a quarter of the width.

Lesson 11 · Recap

What we covered

  • Turning effect = force × distance from the pivot (N m); add them when there are several on one side
  • Every point on a turning arm turns through the same angle in the same time, so the long end moves faster
  • Arc = angle ÷ 360 × 2πr; tip speed ÷ short-end speed = long arm ÷ short arm
  • All the stone’s energy comes from the falling counterweight — a longer arm shares out speed, it can’t create energy
  • Launch speed: GPE lost → × efficiency → KE → \(v = \sqrt{2\,\text{KE} \div m}\)
Exit question

A trebuchet has a short arm of 0.5 m and a long arm of 3.0 m. The counterweight end moves at 2 m/s. How fast does the tip move?

Answer\(3.0 \div 0.5 = 6\), so the tip moves \(6 \times 2 = 12\ \text{m/s}\).
