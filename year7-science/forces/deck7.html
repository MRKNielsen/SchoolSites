Why must each hill on the track be a little lower than the one before?

Forms of energy

Energy is what makes things happen — moving, heating, lighting up, making sound. It is measured in joules (J).

Form of energyWhat it isExample
Kineticthe energy of anything that is movinga rolling ball, a moving car
Gravitational potentialenergy stored by lifting something up higha book on a high shelf
Elastic potentialenergy stored by stretching or squashinga stretched rubber band
Chemicalenergy stored in food, fuel and batteriesa sandwich, petrol, a battery
Thermalthe energy of hot things — hotter means morea cup of hot tea
Sound, light, electricalenergy carried by sound waves, light and electric currenta speaker, a torch, a power cable

I do Energy changes form — energy chains

When energy changes from one form to another, that’s an energy transformation. An energy chain shows each step with an arrow. Here is a trebuchet:

Worked Example 7.1

A ball is held above the floor, dropped, and bounces back up. Write the energy chain from when it is let go to the top of its bounce.

  1. 1 Falling: gravitational potential → kinetic (it speeds up as it falls)
  2. 2 Hitting the floor: kinetic → elastic potential (the ball squashes)
  3. 3 Bouncing up: elastic potential → kinetic → gravitational potential
  4. ✓ At every step a little energy is wasted as thermal energy and sound — you can hear the bounce.

Booklet p.77 · §7.3

Part B · Theory

Energy is never lost — but some is wasted

Conservation of energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only change form, or move from one object to another. The total at the end always equals the total at the start.

Useful and wasted

Useful energy does the job we want. Wasted energy is usually thermal energy and sound, which spread out and are hard to use again.

Efficiency

\(\text{Efficiency} = \dfrac{\text{useful energy out}}{\text{total energy in}} \times 100\%\)

Booklet pp.77–78 · §7.3 · Worked Example 7.2

Part B · Theory

I do Calculating efficiency

Worked Example 7.2

An old-style light bulb is supplied with 100 J of electrical energy. It gives out 5 J of light. How much energy is wasted, and what is its efficiency?

  1. 1 Wasted: \(100 - 5 = 95\ \text{J}\) — as thermal energy (the bulb gets hot)
  2. ✓ Efficiency: \(5 \div 100 \times 100\% = 5\%\)
We do

Check the motor in the Sankey diagram: 100 J in, 70 J useful. What is its efficiency?

Answer\(70 \div 100 \times 100\% = 70\%\). That’s 14 times more efficient than the old light bulb.

No real machine is 100% efficient — it would have to waste nothing at all.

Part B · Theory

I do Calculating GPE and KE

Two energy formulas

\(\text{GPE} = m \times g \times h\)     \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times m \times v^2\)

\(m\) = mass (kg) · \(g = 9.8\ \text{N/kg}\) · \(h\) = height (m) · \(v\) = speed (m/s) · energy in joules (J)

Worked Example 7.3

A 2 kg ball is held at a height of 5 m. Find its gravitational potential energy.

  1. 1 \(\text{GPE} = mgh = 2 \times 9.8 \times 5\)
  2. ✓ \(\text{GPE} = 98\ \text{J}\)
Worked Example 7.4

A 4 kg dog runs at 3 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.

  1. 1 Square the speed first: \(v^2 = 3 \times 3 = 9\)
  2. ✓ \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 9 = 18\ \text{J}\)

Part D · Above level

We do Going further

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: use conservation of energy and combine formulas.

A 0.5 kg ball is dropped from 4 m. Just before it lands it has 17.64 J of KE. (a) Find its GPE at the start. (b) Find the efficiency of the GPE → KE change. (c) Was any energy destroyed?

(a) \(\text{GPE} = 0.5 \times 9.8 \times 4 = 19.6\ \text{J}\)

(b) \(17.64 \div 19.6 \times 100\% = 90\%\)

(c) No — the missing 1.96 J became thermal energy and sound as the ball pushed through the air.

A 0.2 kg ball is dropped from 5 m. No energy is wasted. Find its landing speed. Hint: find \(v^2\) first.

GPE at the top: \(0.2 \times 9.8 \times 5 = 9.8\ \text{J}\), so KE at the bottom = 9.8 J

\(9.8 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.2 \times v^2 \;\Rightarrow\; v^2 = 9.8 \div 0.1 = 98\)

\(v = \sqrt{98} \approx 9.9\ \text{m/s}\)

Lesson 7 · Recap

What we covered

  • Energy is measured in joules (J) and comes in many forms
  • An energy chain shows each transformation
  • Energy is conserved — never created or destroyed — but some is always wasted
  • \(\text{Efficiency} = \text{useful out} \div \text{total in} \times 100\%\)
  • \(\text{GPE} = mgh\)   and   \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\)
Exit question

A 1 kg ball is lifted 2 m. How much GPE does it gain? If it is then dropped, what does that energy become?

Answer\(\text{GPE} = 1 \times 9.8 \times 2 = 19.6\ \text{J}\). As it falls, the GPE becomes kinetic energy (with a little wasted as thermal energy and sound).
