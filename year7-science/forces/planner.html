Year 7 Science · Unit planning document

Unit: Forces & Motion

18 lessons over 6 weeks (3 a week), 3 of them optional · Victorian Curriculum F–10 v2.0 · Year 7 core with Year 8 extension

Core activities link to our own unit pages unless marked Stile. The week grouping matches the unit index, so the planner and the site never disagree. Every lesson has its own slide deck and its own booklet section, always in four parts: A Reading · B Theory and examples · C Practice · D Above level. Lessons 1–3 are rebuilt this way; Lessons 4–18 open the earlier combined decks until theirs are rebuilt. Lessons 11, 14 and 17 are optional — two are Year 8 extension and one is a buffer for the trebuchet launches; skip all three and the 15 core lessons run in 5 weeks. This unit is built on its practicals — each experiment is in the student booklet and the trebuchet investigation is the summative task, so they are listed as core; no further practicals are added, and the optional column is simulations, Stile and booklet work only. Above level: Newton’s laws by name and the equations of motion sit at Levels 9–10 (VC2S10U17); at Levels 7–8 the forces content description is VC2S8U14. Lessons 2 and 5 use them as tools for the experiments, not as Year 7 standards.

VC2S8U14 VC2S8I01 VC2S8I02 VC2S8I03 VC2S8I04 VC2S8I05
  • Lesson 1 · What is a force? — Understand that a force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons; read a spring scale; tell mass from weight and calculate weight; distinguish contact from non-contact forces.
  • I can read a spring scale, calculate weight with \(W = mg\), and classify forces as contact or non-contact.
  • Lesson 2 · Force diagrams and net force — Name the common forces; draw force arrows to scale; draw free body diagrams; explain balanced and unbalanced forces using net force.
  • I can draw and label a free body diagram, calculate net force, and predict the motion.
  • Lesson 3 · Experiment 1 — Identify the variables in a fair test; measure friction; use repeat trials and means.
  • I can name my variables, calculate a mean, and say whether my prediction was supported.
  • Lesson 1 · What is a Force? — A: reading The Forces You Cannot See (C1–C5); B: push or pull, newtons and spring scales, mass and weight, contact vs non-contact (Worked Examples 1.1–1.3); C: Tasks 1.1–1.9; D: Extensions 1.10–1.12 (Lesson Booklet pp. 2–8)
  • Lesson 2 · Force Diagrams and Net Force — A: reading Winning a Tug-of-War; B: naming forces, arrows to scale, free body diagrams, net force (Worked Examples 2.1–2.3); C: Tasks 2.1–2.11; D: Extensions 2.12–2.15 (pp. 9–16)
  • Lesson 3 · Experiment 1: Investigating Friction — A: reading Grip; B: fair testing (Worked Example 3.1), prediction, method; C: results, means, analysis (Tasks 3.1–3.6); D: evaluating the method (Extensions 3.7–3.9) (pp. 17–21)
  • Support: every lesson opens with a short reading and comprehension questions before any calculation; Part B has worked examples to follow step by step, and Part C practises each skill with questions like the examples.
  • Extend: Part D of each lesson — weight on the Moon and rearranging \(W = mg\) (Lesson 1); judging a misconception, the skydiver in three stages, net force with \(F = ma\) (Lesson 2); evaluating the method (Lesson 3).
  • PhET Forces and Motion: Basics — Net Force and Friction screens, run from the board
  • Stile: Year 7 Forces unit — pick the lessons that match
  • Finish Part C of Lesson 2 (net force) as homework
  • Formative: Part C of each lesson, checked in class; Part D for students working above level.
  • Exit questions — your weight on Earth and the Moon (Lesson 1); is a book on a table balanced? (Lesson 2); name today’s variables (Lesson 3).

  • Lessons 16–18 · The investigation — Collect 10 launches and calculate \(g = 2h \div t^2\) for each; find the mean, percentage error and uncertainty; evaluate sources of error; write an evidence-based conclusion.
  • I can tell accuracy (percentage error) from precision (uncertainty), and say which way each source of error pushes my value of \(g\).
  • Lesson 16 · earlier deck slide 9 (method) — ten launches per group, filmed side-on in slow motion; Trebuchet lab tech sheet for the lab requisition
