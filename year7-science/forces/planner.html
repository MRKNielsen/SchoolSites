Unit: Forces & Motion

18 lessons over 6 weeks (3 a week), 2 of them optional · Victorian Curriculum F–10 v2.0 · Year 7 core with Year 8 extension

Core activities link to our own unit pages unless marked Stile. The week grouping matches the unit index, so the planner and the site never disagree. Every lesson has its own slide deck and its own booklet section, always in four parts: A Reading · B Theory and examples · C Practice · D Above level. Lessons 1–14 are built this way; Lessons 15–18 run from the Trebuchet Investigation booklet. Each rebuilt deck opens with a five-question multichoice review — three on the lesson before and two from older lessons (Lesson 1 checks primary-school ideas instead) — and its “Your turn” and Part D slides use questions like the booklet’s, with worked answers hidden until revealed, so the booklet’s own answers stay off the public site. Lessons 11 and 14 are optional Year 8 extensions; skip both and the unit runs in 16 lessons. This unit is built on its practicals — each experiment is in the student booklet and the trebuchet investigation, assessed by an open-booklet test in Lesson 18, is the summative task, so they are listed as core; no further practicals are added, and the optional column is simulations, Stile and booklet work only. Above level: Newton’s laws by name and the equations of motion sit at Levels 9–10 (VC2S10U17); at Levels 7–8 the forces content description is VC2S8U14. Lessons 4–6 and 12–13 use them as tools for the experiments, not as Year 7 standards. Conservation of energy as a law is likewise Levels 9–10 (VC2S10U15) and sits in Part D of Lessons 7–8.

Week1
  • Lesson 1 — What is a force?
  • Lesson 2 — Force diagrams and net force
  • Lesson 3 — Experiment 1: everyday forces

VC2S8U14 VC2S8I02 VC2S8I03 VC2S8I04 VC2S8I05
  • Lesson 1 · What is a force? — Understand that a force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons; read a spring scale; tell mass from weight and calculate weight; distinguish contact from non-contact forces.
  • I can read a spring scale, calculate weight with \(W = mg\), and classify forces as contact or non-contact.
  • Lesson 2 · Force diagrams and net force — Name the common forces; draw force arrows to scale; draw free body diagrams; explain balanced and unbalanced forces using net force.
  • I can draw and label a free body diagram, calculate net force, and predict the motion.
  • Lesson 3 · Experiment 1 — Observe pushing, gravitational, electrostatic, magnetic and buoyancy forces at five workstations; describe the direction each force acts in; write inferences and generalisations from observations.
  • I can say what each force did and which way it acted, tell contact from non-contact forces, and write an inference and a generalisation.
  • Lesson 1 · What is a Force? — A: reading The Forces You Cannot See (C1–C8); B: push or pull, newtons and spring scales, mass and weight, contact vs non-contact (Worked Examples 1.1–1.3); C: Tasks 1.1–1.12; D: Extensions 1.13–1.19 (Lesson Booklet pp. 3–16)
  • Lesson 2 · Force Diagrams and Net Force — A: reading Winning a Tug-of-War; B: naming forces, arrows to scale, free body diagrams, net force (Worked Examples 2.1–2.3); C: Tasks 2.1–2.16; D: Extensions 2.17–2.23 (pp. 17–30)
  • Lesson 3 · Experiment 1: Everyday Forces — vocabulary, aim, how the workstations run and safety; then five workstations, one page each: A pushing forces and blowball, B gravitational forces, C electrostatic forces, D magnetic forces, E buoyancy forces (§3.1–3.5) (pp. 31–36)
  • Support: every lesson except the Experiment 1 workstations opens with a short reading and comprehension questions before any calculation; Part B has worked examples to follow step by step, and Part C practises each skill with questions like the examples.
  • Extend: Part D of each lesson — weight on the Moon and rearranging \(W = mg\) (Lesson 1); judging a misconception, the skydiver in three stages, net force with \(F = ma\) (Lesson 2).
  • PhET Forces and Motion: Basics — Net Force and Friction screens, run from the board
  • Stile: Year 7 Forces unit — pick the lessons that match
  • Finish Part C of Lesson 2 (net force) as homework
  • Lab: Everyday forces workstations, 2–3 sets of each — Everyday forces risk assessment (RiskAssess, opens ready to clone)
  • Formative: the opening quiz — what students already know (Lesson 1), then a review of the lesson before; Part C of each lesson, checked in class; Part D for students working above level.
  • Exit questions — your weight on Earth and the Moon (Lesson 1); is a book on a table balanced? (Lesson 2); a non-contact force you saw today, and which way it acted (Lesson 3).

  • Lesson 4 · First and Third Laws — Explain the First Law using inertia and unbalanced force; explain why moving things on Earth slow down; identify action–reaction pairs and explain why they don’t cancel.
  • I can explain a situation using inertia and unbalanced force, and describe the reaction to a force — its size, its direction and the object it acts on.
  • Lesson 5 · Second Law — Describe acceleration; explain how force and mass change acceleration; use \(F = ma\) to find force, mass or acceleration, using the net force.
  • I can rearrange and use \(F = ma\) with correct units, finding the net force first.
  • Lesson 6 · Experiment 2 — Identify the variables in a fair test; measure how adding mass to a block changes friction; use repeat trials and means; draw a line graph.
  • I can name my variables, calculate a mean, and say whether my prediction was supported.
  • Lesson 4 · Newton’s First and Third Laws — A: reading Why Rockets Work in Empty Space (C1–C8); B: the First Law and inertia, the Third Law and force pairs (Worked Examples 4.1–4.3); C: Tasks 4.1–4.14; D: Extensions 4.15–4.21 (Lesson Booklet pp. 37–50)
  • Lesson 5 · Newton’s Second Law: \(F = ma\) — A: reading Pushing a Broken-Down Car (C1–C8); B: acceleration, force, mass and acceleration, the formula triangle, using the net force (Worked Examples 5.1–5.4); C: Tasks 5.1–5.14; D: Extensions 5.15–5.21 (pp. 51–64)
  • Lesson 6 · Experiment 2: Friction and Mass — A: reading Grip (C1–C4); B: fair testing (Worked Example 6.1), prediction, the set-up, method; C: results, means (Worked Example 6.2), errors and improvements, line graph (Tasks 6.1–6.7); D: interpreting the graph, a sandpaper prediction, \(F = ma\), a fair test of surface type (Extensions 6.8–6.11) (pp. 65–73)
  • Support: worked examples are revealed one step at a time before students try the lookalike questions; the formula triangle and the four steps for every \(F = ma\) question (Lesson 5); calculating a mean is worked through before the practical (Lesson 6).
  • Extend: Part D of each lesson — force-pair misconceptions, the horse and cart, the astronaut and the toolbox (Lesson 4); the rocket, the lift and the golf ball with \(F = ma\) (Lesson 5); interpreting the graph, \(F = ma\) for the sliding block and a fair test of surface type (Lesson 6).
  • PhET Forces and Motion: Basics — Motion screen (Lesson 4) and Acceleration screen (Lesson 5)
  • Stile: Year 7 Forces unit — balanced and unbalanced forces
  • Finish Part C of Lesson 5 (\(F = ma\)) as homework
  • Lab: Friction and mass (Lesson 6) — Friction and mass risk assessment (RiskAssess, opens ready to clone)
  • Formative: the review quiz that opens each lesson; Part C of each lesson, checked in class; Part D for students working above level.
  • Exit questions — the reaction force when oars push water backwards (Lesson 4); a 2 kg ball pushed with a net force of 10 N: its acceleration and the law used (Lesson 5); today’s independent and dependent variables (Lesson 6).

  • Lesson 7 · Energy — Name the forms of energy; write energy chains; explain conservation of energy and efficiency; calculate \(\text{GPE} = mgh\) and \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\).
  • I can calculate GPE, KE and efficiency with correct units, and say where energy is wasted.
  • Lesson 8 · Experiment 3 — Plan a fair test of drop height against bounce height; use repeat trials and means; calculate bounce efficiency.
  • I can name my variables, calculate the efficiency of a bounce, and explain where the wasted energy goes.
  • Lesson 9 · Levers — Name the six simple machines; identify the fulcrum, effort and load; calculate mechanical advantage; classify levers as Class 1, 2 or 3; explain the trebuchet as a Class 1 lever.
  • I can calculate MA from forces or arm lengths and give an example of each lever class.
  • Lesson 7 · Energy and Transformation — A: reading Why the First Hill is Always the Tallest (C1–C7); B: forms of energy, energy chains, conservation and efficiency, Sankey diagrams, GPE and KE (Worked Examples 7.1–7.4); C: Tasks 7.1–7.13; D: Extensions 7.14–7.20 (Lesson Booklet pp. 74–86)
  • Lesson 8 · Experiment 3: Bounce Efficiency — A: reading The Rules of the Bounce (C1–C4); B: measuring a bounce, bounce efficiency (Worked Example 8.1), fair test, prediction, method; C: results, means, efficiency, line graph (Tasks 8.1–8.7); D: gradient, efficiency in joules, carpet and a temperature fair test (Extensions 8.8–8.11) (pp. 87–94)
  • Lesson 9 · Simple Machines and Levers — A: reading Lifting Stones for a Pharaoh (C1–C8); B: the six simple machines, the law of the lever, mechanical advantage, the three classes, the trebuchet as a Class 1 lever (Worked Examples 9.1–9.3); C: Tasks 9.1–9.13; D: Extensions 9.14–9.20 (pp. 95–108)
  • Support: each formula is introduced with one worked substitution before students try their own; Experiment 3 measures efficiency with heights only, no joules; the F–L–E, 1–2–3 memory aid for the lever classes.
  • Extend: Part D of each lesson — conservation of energy and landing speed from KE (Lesson 7); bounce efficiency in joules and a temperature fair test (Lesson 8); the forearm as a lever and placing a fulcrum (Lesson 9). The optional Lesson 11 takes levers further: turning effects, tip speed, and energy and launch speed.

  • PhET Balancing Act — balance the seesaw before Worked Example 9.1
  • Stile: Year 8 Energy unit — energy forms and transformations
  • Formative: the review quiz that opens each lesson; Part C of each lesson, checked in class; Part D for students working above level.
  • Exit questions — GPE of a 1 kg ball lifted 2 m (Lesson 7); the efficiency of a ball dropped from 50 cm that bounces to 30 cm (Lesson 8); the MA of a 1.5 m / 0.5 m lever, and whether it multiplies force or speed (Lesson 9).

  • I can measure an effort arm, calculate the MA two ways, and explain why a heavier effort balances closer to the fulcrum.
