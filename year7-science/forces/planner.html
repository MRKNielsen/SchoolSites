Year 7 Science · Unit planning document

Unit: Forces & Motion

6 lessons and 5 experiments over 5 weeks, building to the trebuchet investigation · Victorian Curriculum F–10 v2.0 · Year 7 core with Year 8 extension in every section

Core activities link to our own unit pages. The week grouping matches the unit index, so the planner and the site never disagree. This unit is built on its practicals — each lesson's experiment is in the student booklet, and the trebuchet investigation is the culminating task. They are listed as core because the booklet and the assessment depend on them; no further practicals are added, and the optional column is on-screen simulations and booklet work only. Year 8 extension sits in the purple boxes of every booklet section.

Week1
  • What is a force?
  • Pushes and pulls, measured in newtons
  • Contact and non-contact forces
  • Free body diagrams; balanced and unbalanced forces
  • Experiment 1 — friction
  • Lesson 1 — Understand that a force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons; distinguish contact from non-contact forces; represent forces using free body diagrams; explain balanced and unbalanced forces using net force.
  • I can classify forces as contact or non-contact, draw and label a free body diagram, and calculate net force to predict motion.
  • Lesson 1 · What is a Force? — I do: net force on a pushed box; You do: skydiver free body diagram (Task 1.7c), car net force (Extension 1.8)
  • Student Booklet §1 (pp. 2–10): reading passage, beginner skills §1.1–§1.7, Tasks 1.1–1.7
  • Experiment 1 — Investigating friction (booklet pp. 11–12): how surface type affects the friction force
  • Support: the beginner-skills block (§1.1–§1.7) builds from push-or-pull up to force arrows drawn to scale, with Worked Examples 1.1–1.2 and a spring-scale reading guide. Reading comprehension C1–C3 before any calculation.
  • Extend: Extension 1.8 (car net force and which law it shows); Experiment 1 Q1.3 uses \(F = ma\) to find the block's deceleration.
  • PhET Forces and Motion: Basics — Net Force and Friction screens, run from the board
  • Practice Set — Net Force (booklet p. 9) as homework
  • Formative: Booklet §1 Tasks 1.1–1.7; Experiment 1 Q1.1–Q1.3.
  • Exit question — a book sits still on a table: are the forces balanced or unbalanced, and how do you know?
Week2
  • Newton's laws of motion
  • Inertia
  • \(F = ma\)
  • Action and reaction
  • Experiment 2 — trolley and pulley
  • Lesson 2 — Explain Newton's First Law using inertia and net force; use \(F = ma\) to calculate force, mass or acceleration; identify action–reaction pairs; apply the laws to real situations and the trebuchet.
  • I can rearrange and use \(F = ma\) with correct units, name both forces in an action–reaction pair, and say which law explains a situation.
  • Lesson 2 · Newton's Laws of Motion — I do: Worked Example 2.1; You do: Q2.1–Q2.2 (inertia), Task 2.1 (\(F = ma\) table), Q2.4 (reaction force)
  • Student Booklet §2 (pp. 13–18)
  • Experiment 2 — Newton's Second Law (booklet pp. 19–21): trolley, pulley and hanging masses
  • Support: Worked Example 2.1 is revealed step by step before Task 2.1; Practice Set — \(F = ma\) (p. 17) gives graded repetition of the rearrangement.
  • Extend: Q2.3 rocket (Second and Third Laws together); Experiment 2 Q2.6 — plot \(a\) against \(F\) and interpret the gradient; the deck's trebuchet-launch problem uses all three laws at once.
  • PhET Forces and Motion: Basics — Acceleration screen
  • Reading — Why Rockets Work in Empty Space (§2) as a discussion starter
  • Formative: Booklet §2 Q2.1–Q2.4 and Task 2.1; Experiment 2 Q2.5–Q2.6.
  • Exit question — a 2 kg ball is pushed with a net force of 10 N: find its acceleration and name the law used.
Week3
  • Energy & transformation
  • Energy stores, conservation, efficiency
  • Simple machines & levers
  • Mechanical advantage; the trebuchet as a lever
  • Experiments 3 and 4
  • Lesson 3 — Name the main energy stores; calculate \(\text{GPE} = mgh\) and \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\); explain conservation and efficiency; trace an energy chain through the trebuchet.
  • I can calculate GPE and KE with correct units, calculate efficiency and say where energy is lost.
  • Lesson 4 — Identify the fulcrum, effort and load; calculate mechanical advantage; classify levers as Class 1, 2 or 3; explain the trebuchet as a Class 1 lever.
  • I can calculate MA from forces or arm lengths and explain why a long arm gives a faster launch.
  • Lesson 3 · Energy & Transformation — I do: Worked Example 3.1; You do: Q3.1–Q3.2, then the energy chain and Sankey diagram (Q3.4–Q3.5)
  • Lesson 4 · Simple Machines & Levers — I do: Worked Example 4.1; You do: seesaw (Q4.1), trebuchet MA (Q4.2–Q4.3)
  • Student Booklet §3 (pp. 22–26) and §4 (pp. 29–34)
  • Experiment 3 — Energy efficiency (pp. 27–28): bounce height against drop height
  • Experiment 4 — Lever mechanical advantage (p. 35): metre stick, fulcrum and masses
  • Support: Practice Sets — Energy Calculations (p. 25) and Levers and Mechanical Advantage (p. 33); each formula is introduced with one worked substitution before students attempt their own.
  • Extend: Q3.3 efficiency, Q3.5 Sankey, Q3.7 steel vs rubber ball; Q4.4 arc length and tip speed. The deck's KE→launch-speed and arm-length→tip-speed problems connect forward to Lesson 5.
  • PhET Energy Skate Park: Basics — GPE↔KE with the bar chart on
  • PhET Balancing Act — balance the seesaw before Q4.1
  • Formative: Booklet §3–§4; Experiment 3 Q3.6–Q3.7; Experiment 4 Q4.5–Q4.6.
  • Exit questions — GPE of a 1 kg ball lifted 2 m; MA of a 1.5 m / 0.5 m lever and whether it multiplies force or speed.
Week4
  • Projectile motion
  • Horizontal and vertical motion, measuring \(g\)
  • Experiment 5 — ball roll-off
  • Investigation planning
  • Lesson 5 — Explain what makes an object a projectile; describe the horizontal and vertical motion separately; use \(h = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2\), \(g = 2h \div t^2\) and \(R = v \times t\).
  • I can calculate time of flight from a drop height, calculate \(g\) from measured height and time, and find launch speed from range and time.
  • Lesson 6 · planning — Write an aim and a testable hypothesis; identify independent, dependent and controlled variables; complete a risk assessment.
  • Lesson 5 · Projectile Motion — I do: Worked Example 5.1 and finding \(g\) from data; You do: Task 5.1
  • Student Booklet §5 (pp. 36–40)
  • Experiment 5 — Ball roll-off: measuring \(g\) (pp. 41–42): slow-motion video of a ball rolling off a table
  • Lesson 6 · The Trebuchet Investigation, planning slides — free body diagrams (Task 6.1), aim, hypothesis, variables, risk assessment (booklet §6.1–§6.2, pp. 43–46)
  • Support: Practice Set — Projectile Motion (p. 39); the Year 7 hypothesis is a complete-the-sentence scaffold (§6.2).
  • Extend: §5.2 Optimising Range — Q5.1 maximum range at 45°, Q5.2 why a trebuchet isn't set to exactly 45°; Q6.2 counterweight energy to launch speed; the Year 8 hypothesis is written in full with a numerical prediction.
  • PhET Projectile Motion — compare a dropped and a horizontally launched ball
  • Practice Set — Projectile Motion as homework
  • Formative: Booklet §5 Task 5.1; Experiment 5 Q5.3–Q5.4.
  • Planning (§6.2) checked by the teacher before any group launches.
Week5
  • The trebuchet investigation
  • Launch, measure, analyse, conclude
  • Unit assessment
  • Lesson 6 · investigation — Collect 10 launches and calculate \(g = 2h \div t^2\) for each; find the mean, percentage error and uncertainty; evaluate sources of error; write an evidence-based conclusion.
  • I can tell accuracy (percentage error) from precision (uncertainty), and say which way each source of error pushes my value of \(g\).
  • Lesson 6 · The Trebuchet Investigation — method, results, analysis, sources of error and conclusion slides
  • Student Booklet §6.3–§6.5 (pp. 47–50)
  • Trebuchet lab tech sheet — the lab requisition for the launches
  • Support: sentence starters in §6.4 and a set structure for the conclusion in §6.5; Year 7 identify two sources of error, each with an improvement.
  • Extend: Year 8 identify four sources of error and the direction each pushes \(g\), report \(g\) as mean ± uncertainty, and link the result to \(F = ma\).
  • Pool every group's mean \(g\) on the board and discuss the spread as a class
  • Revisit any Practice Set before the booklet is handed in
  • Summative: the completed booklet and trebuchet investigation, marked against the rubric at the back of the booklet (p. 51) — Year 7 /137, Year 8 /173.
  • Formative: the error-and-uncertainty discussion before write-up.
