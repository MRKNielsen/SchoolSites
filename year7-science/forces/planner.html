Year 7 Science · Unit planning document

Unit: Forces & Motion

15 core periods over 6 weeks (3 periods a week), plus 3 optional periods · Victorian Curriculum F–10 v2.0 · Year 7 core with Year 8 extension

Core activities link to our own unit pages unless marked Stile. The week grouping matches the unit index, so the planner and the site never disagree. Three periods are optional — two hold the Year 8 extension slides (those items are worth 0 of the 137 Year 7 marks) and one is a buffer for the trebuchet launches; skip all three and the 15 core periods run in 5 weeks. This unit is built on its practicals — each experiment is in the student booklet and the trebuchet investigation is the summative task, so they are listed as core; no further practicals are added, and the optional column is simulations, Stile and booklet work only. Above level: Newton’s laws by name and the equations of motion sit at Levels 9–10 (VC2S10U17); at Levels 7–8 the forces content description is VC2S8U14. Lessons 2 and 5 use them as tools for the experiments, not as Year 7 standards.

VC2S8U14
Balanced and unbalanced forces acting on objects, including gravitational force, represented with force diagrams; changes in motion relate to mass and force
VC2S8U13
Simple machines, including the lever, change the direction and size of forces
VC2S8U15
Energy exists in different forms, including gravitational and elastic; energy transfers and transformations, and their efficiency
VC2S8H01
Scientific knowledge, including models and theories, can change in light of new evidence
VC2S8I01–I08
Science inquiry — questioning and predicting (I01); planning and conducting (I02–I03); processing, modelling and analysing (I04–I05); evaluating (I06–I07); communicating (I08)
VC2S10U17
Levels 9–10, above level — Newton’s laws of motion and the equations of motion

Week1
  • What is a force?
  • Pushes and pulls, measured in newtons
  • Contact and non-contact forces
  • Free body diagrams; net force

VC2S8U14 VC2S8I01 VC2S8I03 VC2S8I04 VC2S8I05
  • Lesson 1 (periods 1–2) — Understand that a force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons; distinguish contact from non-contact forces; represent forces with free body diagrams; explain balanced and unbalanced forces using net force.
  • I can classify forces as contact or non-contact, draw and label a free body diagram, and calculate net force to predict motion.
  • Experiment 1 (period 3) — Predict, then test, how the type of surface affects friction; repeat trials and calculate a mean.
  • I can record three trials for each surface, calculate the mean, and say whether my prediction was supported.
  • P1 · Lesson 1 · What is a Force? slides 1–4 — push or pull, newtons, contact vs non-contact; booklet beginner skills §1.1–§1.7 (pp. 2–6)
  • P2 · Lesson 1 slides 5–10 — free body diagrams; I do: net force on a pushed box; You do: skydiver (Task 1.7c), car net force (Extension 1.8); Tasks 1.6–1.8 (pp. 7–8)
  • P3 · Experiment 1 — Investigating friction (booklet pp. 11–12): prediction, three trials per surface, means; Q1.1–Q1.3
  • Student Booklet §1 (pp. 2–12)
  • Support: the beginner-skills block (§1.1–§1.7) builds from push-or-pull up to force arrows drawn to scale, with Worked Examples 1.1–1.2 and a spring-scale reading guide. Reading comprehension C1–C3 comes before any calculation.
  • Extend: Extension 1.8 (car net force and the law it shows); Experiment 1 Q1.3 uses \(F = ma\) to find the block’s deceleration.
  • PhET Forces and Motion: Basics — Net Force and Friction screens, run from the board
  • Stile: Year 7 Forces unit — pick the lessons that match
  • Practice Set — Net Force (booklet p. 9) as homework
  • Formative: Booklet §1 Tasks 1.1–1.8; Experiment 1 Q1.1–Q1.3.
  • Exit question — a book sits still on a table: are the forces balanced or unbalanced, and how do you know?

  • Experiment 2 (period 6) — Identify the independent, dependent and controlled variables; measure how acceleration changes with force.

  • Extend: Q2.3 rocket (Second and Third Laws together); Experiment 2 Q2.6 — plot acceleration against force and interpret the gradient; the trebuchet-launch problem uses all three laws at once.

VC2S8U15 VC2S8U13 VC2S8I03 VC2S8I04 VC2S8I05

  • Lesson 6 · investigation (periods 16–18) — Collect 10 launches and calculate \(g = 2h \div t^2\) for each; find the mean, percentage error and uncertainty; evaluate sources of error; write an evidence-based conclusion.
  • I can tell accuracy (percentage error) from precision (uncertainty), and say which way each source of error pushes my value of \(g\).
  • P16 · Lesson 6 slide 9 (method) — ten launches per group, filmed side-on in slow motion; Trebuchet lab tech sheet for the lab requisition

  • Extend: Year 8 identify four sources of error and the direction each pushes \(g\), report \(g\) as mean ± uncertainty, and link the result to \(F = ma\).

  • Pool every group’s mean \(g\) on the board and discuss the spread as a class
