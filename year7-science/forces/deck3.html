Starter

Why is it so hard to walk on ice?

AnswerTo walk, your shoe pushes back on the ground and friction pushes you forwards. Ice is so smooth that there is hardly any friction, so your foot just slips backwards.

Check after reading

In our experiment, why must we pull the block at a steady speed when we read the spring scale?

AnswerAt a steady speed the motion isn’t changing, so the forces are balanced: your pull is exactly the same size as the friction. That means the spring scale reading is the friction force.

I do Making it a fair test

Fair test

Change one thing, measure one thing, and keep everything else the same.

VariableWhat it means
Independentthe one thing you change on purpose
Dependentthe thing you measure
Controlledeverything you keep the same
Worked Example 3.1

A student rolls a toy car down a ramp set at different heights, and measures how far it travels across the floor. Identify the variables.

  1. 1 Independent (changed): the height of the ramp.
  2. 2 Dependent (measured): the distance the car travels.
  3. ✓ Controlled (kept the same): the same car, the same floor, letting go without a push.

A student wants to know if the temperature of water changes how fast sugar dissolves. She times how long a spoonful of sugar takes to dissolve in water at 20 °C, 40 °C and 60 °C. Name the independent variable, the dependent variable and two controlled variables.

Independent: the temperature of the water

In our friction experiment, what will we change and what will we measure?

We change the type of surface (independent).

We measure the friction force with the spring scale (dependent).

Booklet pp.24–25 · Part B · Prediction, equipment and method

Part B · Planning the experiment

Investigating friction

Predict first

Which surface will make the most friction? Write your prediction and a reason in your booklet before you start.

Equipment

Wooden block (or heavy book) · spring scale · 3 or more surfaces (bench, sandpaper, fabric, carpet) · ruler

Method
  1. Put the block on the first surface.
  2. Hook on the spring scale. Pull slowly and steadily.
  3. Read the scale while the block moves at a steady speed. Record it in newtons.
  4. Do 3 trials on this surface.
  5. Repeat for each surface.
Safety

Pull gently and keep the scale in line with the block — an overstretched scale can snap back. Keep sandpaper away from eyes and skin.

Booklet p.26 · Part C · Results and analysis

Part C · Results and analysis

I do Calculating a mean

Every trial is a little different. The mean (average) gives one value we can trust more than any single trial.

How to find a mean

Add all the trials, then divide by how many trials there are.

Example

Three trials on the bench gave 1.2 N, 1.4 N and 1.3 N. Find the mean.

  1. 1 Add the trials: \(1.2 + 1.4 + 1.3 = 3.9\)
  2. 2 Divide by the number of trials: \(3.9 \div 3\)
  3. ✓ Mean friction on the bench = 1.3 N

Part C · Practice

You do Try these, then check your working

A group measured 3.4 N, 3.1 N and 3.7 N on rubber matting. Calculate the mean.

Add: \(3.4 + 3.1 + 3.7 = 10.2\)

Divide: \(10.2 \div 3 = 3.4\)

3.4 N

On cardboard a group measured 1.5 N, 1.7 N and 2.6 N. Which value looks wrong? Find the mean of the other two.

2.6 N is much bigger than the others, so it looks wrong.

Mean of the other two: \((1.5 + 1.7) \div 2 = 3.2 \div 2 = 1.6\)

1.6 N

Means: bench 1.3 N, sandpaper 3.2 N, carpet 2.4 N, fabric 1.9 N. Which surface had the most friction, and which the least?

Put them in order: 3.2 > 2.4 > 1.9 > 1.3

Most: sandpaper. Least: the bench.

Use the idea of bumps to explain why the sandpaper made the most friction.

Sandpaper is the roughest surface, so it has the biggest bumps.

Bigger bumps catch on the bumps of the block more, so they resist sliding more — more friction.

Part D · Above level

We do How good was the method?

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: judge a method, and use a formula.

One group pulled the block quickly and in jerks. Their readings on the bench were 1.0 N, 2.1 N and 1.5 N. Why are their readings so spread out? Suggest one improvement.

When the pull is jerky the block keeps speeding up and slowing down, so the pull is not balanced with friction and the scale jumps around.

Improvement: pull slowly at a steady speed and read the scale while the speed is steady — or film the scale and read it from the video.

A 0.5 kg block is sliding across a bench. Friction of 2.0 N is the only sideways force on it. Use \(a = F \div m\) to find how quickly it slows down.

\(a = F \div m = 2.0 \div 0.5 = 4\ \text{m/s}^2\)

It slows down by 4 m/s every second.

Lesson 3 · Recap

What we covered

  • A fair test changes one variable, measures one, and keeps the rest the same
  • At a steady speed, the spring scale reading equals the friction force
  • Rougher surfaces have bigger bumps, so they make more friction
  • Repeat each trial and take the mean — leave out a result that clearly doesn’t fit
Exit question

Name the independent and dependent variables in today’s experiment.

AnswerIndependent: the type of surface. Dependent: the friction force (the spring scale reading).
