Booklet p.23 · §3.1

Concept · Types of energy

Energy is stored in different ways

Key equations

\(\text{GPE} = m g h\) \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2\) \(\text{Efficiency} = \text{useful} \div \text{total} \times 100\%\)

Mass \(m\) in kg, \(g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\), height \(h\) in m, speed \(v\) in m/s. Energy is measured in joules (J).

Booklet p.23 · Worked Example 3.1

Work through with the class

I do Worked example — gravitational PE

How much energy is stored by lifting a ball?

A 2 kg ball is held at a height of 5 m. Find its gravitational potential energy. (\(g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\))

  1. 1 Write the formula: \(\text{GPE} = m g h\).
  2. 2 Substitute: \(\text{GPE} = 2 \times 9.8 \times 5\).
  3. ✓ The ball stores \(\text{GPE} = 98\ \text{J}\).

Booklet p.23 · Q3.1, Q3.2

Your turn

You do Now you try — GPE then KE

Calculate, then compare

(a) Find the GPE of a 3 kg ball held at 4 m.
(b) It is dropped; just before landing its speed is 8.85 m/s. Find its KE and compare it to the GPE.

  1. 1 (a) \(\text{GPE} = m g h = 3 \times 9.8 \times 4 = 117.6\ \text{J}\)
  2. 2 (b) \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 8.85^2 \approx 117.5\ \text{J}\)
  3. ✓ \(\text{KE} \approx \text{GPE}\) — almost all the GPE became KE, so energy was conserved (transformed, not lost).

Let students attempt, then reveal — note that \(\text{KE} \approx \text{GPE}\)

Booklet p.23 · §3.1

Concept · Conservation

Energy is transformed, not lost

Law of conservation of energy

Energy is never created or destroyed — it just changes form. As the ball falls, gravitational PE transforms into kinetic energy, but the total energy stays the same.

Booklet p.23 · §3.1

Concept · Efficiency

No real machine is 100% efficient

Key idea

Some input energy always becomes wasted energy — usually heat and sound from friction. Efficiency tells us what fraction of the input ends up as the useful output we wanted.

Booklet p.24 · Q3.3 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension

We do Extension — calculate the efficiency

Where did the energy go?

A ball starts with 98 J of GPE but lands with only 92 J of KE. Calculate the efficiency of the GPE → KE transformation and suggest where the “missing” energy went.

  1. 1 Efficiency \(= \text{useful} \div \text{total} \times 100\%\).
  2. 2 Substitute: \(= 92 \div 98 \times 100\%\).
  3. 3 \(\approx 93.9\%\ \text{efficient}\).
  4. ✓ The missing \(6\ \text{J}\) became heat and sound (air resistance and the impact).

Reveal step-by-step — efficiency is always less than 100%

Year 8 extension · connecting forward

We do From energy to launch speed

Rearrange \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\)

A trebuchet transfers 4.5 J of kinetic energy to a 0.04 kg projectile. Find its launch speed \(v\).

  1. 1 Start from \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2\) and rearrange: \(v = \sqrt{2 \times \text{KE} \div m}\).
  2. 2 Substitute: \(v = \sqrt{2 \times 4.5 \div 0.04} = \sqrt{225}\).
  3. ✓ Launch speed \(v = 15\ \text{m/s}\) — this feeds straight into the projectile investigation.

The energy in the launch sets the speed — and the speed sets the range

Section 3 · Recap

What we covered

  • Energy is stored as kinetic, gravitational PE and elastic PE
  • \(\text{GPE} = mgh\) and \(\text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\), both measured in joules
  • Energy is conserved — it transforms form, but the total stays the same
  • Real transformations waste energy as heat and sound — efficiency is always under 100%
  • The trebuchet's energy chain: GPE → KE → KE, with thermal & sound lost along the way
Exit question
