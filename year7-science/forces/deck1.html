Part B · Theory

A force is a push or a pull

Forces are measured in newtons (N), named after Sir Isaac Newton. A force can:

  • Start or stop a moving object
  • Change the speed of motion
  • Change the direction of motion
  • Change the shape of an object
Key idea

Forces always act in a direction. We show this with arrows — the longer the arrow, the bigger the force.

Check for understanding

If you push a shopping trolley and it speeds up, which two of the four effects are happening?

Part B · Theory

I do Measuring force — the newton

Force is measured in newtons (N) with a spring scale (newton meter). About 1 N holds up a small apple; about 10 N holds up a 1 kg bag of sugar.

Reading a spring scale

Find the maximum, work out what one small division is worth, read at eye level, and always write the unit.

Worked Example 1.1

This scale reads 0–10 N with 20 small divisions. The pointer is on the 7th division. What is the reading?

  1. 1 One division: \(10 \div 20 = 0.5\ \text{N}\).
  2. ✓ Reading: \(7 \times 0.5 = 3.5\ \text{N}\).

Booklet pp.4–5 · §1.4 · Worked Example 1.2

Part B · Theory

I do Mass and weight are not the same thing

  • Mass is the amount of matter, in kilograms (kg). It is the same everywhere.
  • Weight is the force of gravity on that mass, in newtons (N). It changes with gravity.
The link

\(W = mg\)   with \(g = 9.8\ \text{N/kg}\) on Earth and \(g = 1.6\ \text{N/kg}\) on the Moon.

Worked Example 1.2

A student has a mass of 50 kg. Find their weight on Earth and on the Moon.

  1. 1 Earth: \(W = mg = 50 \times 9.8 = 490\ \text{N}\).
  2. 2 Moon: \(W = mg = 50 \times 1.6 = 80\ \text{N}\).
  3. ✓ Mass stays 50 kg in both places — only the weight changes.

Part D · Above level

We do Jumping on the Moon

Same mass, different weight

An astronaut has a mass of 75 kg. Find her weight on Earth and on the Moon. Why can she jump much higher on the Moon?

  1. 1 Earth: \(W = mg = 75 \times 9.8 = 735\ \text{N}\).
  2. 2 Moon: \(W = mg = 75 \times 1.6 = 120\ \text{N}\).
  3. ✓ Her mass is still 75 kg, but gravity pulls on her with far less force — so the same push from her legs lifts her much higher.

Lesson 1 · Recap

What we covered

  • A force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons (N)
  • A force can start, stop, speed up, slow down, turn or squash an object
  • Read a spring scale by working out what one division is worth
  • Mass (kg) is not weight (N): \(W = mg\)
  • Contact forces need touching; non-contact forces act across a gap
Exit question

Your mass is 40 kg. What is your weight on Earth — and would it be different on the Moon?
