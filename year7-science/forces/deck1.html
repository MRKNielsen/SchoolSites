Booklet pp.2–3 · §1.1–1.2

Concept

A force is a push or a pull

Forces are measured in newtons (N), named after Sir Isaac Newton. A force can:

  • Start or stop a moving object
  • Change the speed of motion
  • Change the direction of motion
  • Change the shape of an object
Key idea

Forces always act in a direction. We show this with arrows — the longer the arrow, the bigger the force.

Check for understanding

If you push a shopping trolley and it speeds up, which two of the four effects are happening?

Booklet p.7 · §1.9

Concept

Free body diagrams

Definition

A free body diagram shows every force on one object as a labelled arrow. Arrow length is proportional to the size of the force — a 20 N arrow is twice as long as a 10 N arrow.

Booklet p.8 · Task 1.7b

Work through with the class

I do Worked example — net force

Find the net force and the motion

A box on a smooth floor is pushed right with 20 N. Friction pushes left with 5 N. What is the net force, and what happens to the box?

  1. 1 Identify the two horizontal forces: push \(= 20\ \text{N}\) right, friction \(= 5\ \text{N}\) left.
  2. 2 The forces act in opposite directions, so subtract them.
  3. 3 \(F_{\text{net}} = 20 - 5 = 15\ \text{N}\), pointing right.
  4. ✓ The forces are unbalanced, so the box accelerates to the right.

Booklet p.8 · Extension 1.8

Your turn

You do Now you try

Predict the motion of the car

A car has a driving force of 4500 N forward. Friction and air resistance total 1200 N backward. Find the net force and describe the car’s motion.

  1. 1 Opposite directions, so subtract: \(4500 - 1200\).
  2. 2 \(F_{\text{net}} = 3300\ \text{N}\) forward.
  3. ✓ Unbalanced force — the car accelerates forward (speeds up).

Section 1 · Recap

What we covered

  • A force is a push or pull, measured in newtons (N)
  • Contact forces need touching; non-contact forces act at a distance
  • Free body diagrams show all forces as arrows, with length proportional to size
  • Balanced forces → no acceleration; unbalanced → acceleration
  • Net force is the overall force after combining all the forces
Exit question

A book sits still on a table. Are the forces on it balanced or unbalanced? How do you know?
