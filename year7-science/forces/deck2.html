Booklet p.14 · §2.1

Concept · First Law

Newton's First Law — inertia

Definition

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by an unbalanced (net) force. Inertia is the tendency to resist a change in motion — more mass means more inertia.

Booklet p.14 · Q2.1, Q2.2

Your turn — explain using inertia

You do Explain it with the First Law

Use the words inertia and unbalanced force

Q2.1 Passengers lurch forward when a bus suddenly brakes. Why?
Q2.2 A hockey puck slides across almost-frictionless ice — what eventually stops it, and why?

  1. 1 Q2.1 The bus decelerates, but the passengers' inertia keeps them moving forward at the original speed.
  2. ✓ Q2.1 They keep going until an unbalanced force (seatbelt / seat) acts to slow them too.
  3. 2 Q2.2 With almost no friction there is no net force, so the puck glides at near-constant velocity.
  4. ✓ Q2.2 The small remaining friction (an unbalanced force) gradually brings it to rest.

Booklet p.15 · §2.2

Concept · Second Law

Newton's Second Law — \(F = ma\)

Key equations

\(F = m \times a\) \(a = F \div m\) \(m = F \div a\)

Acceleration is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to mass. Force in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), acceleration in \(\text{m/s}^2\).

Booklet p.15 · Worked Example 2.1

Work through with the class

I do Worked example — find the acceleration

A trolley is pushed — how fast does it speed up?

A 5 kg trolley is pushed with a net force of 20 N. What is its acceleration?

  1. 1 Write the formula and rearrange for \(a\): \(F = m \times a \;\Rightarrow\; a = F \div m\).
  2. 2 Identify the values: \(F = 20\ \text{N}\), \(m = 5\ \text{kg}\).
  3. 3 Substitute: \(a = 20 \div 5\).
  4. ✓ The trolley accelerates at \(a = 4\ \text{m/s}^2\).

Booklet p.15 · Task 2.1

Your turn

You do Now you try — fill the table

Rearrange \(F = ma\) for the missing value

(a) Soccer ball: \(F = 45\ \text{N},\ m = 0.45\ \text{kg}\). Find \(a\).
(b) Car braking: \(m = 1200\ \text{kg},\ a = 3.5\ \text{m/s}^2\). Find \(F\).
(c) Rocket: \(F = 6000\ \text{N},\ a = 30\ \text{m/s}^2\). Find \(m\).

  1. 1 (a) \(a = F \div m = 45 \div 0.45 = 100\ \text{m/s}^2\)
  2. 2 (b) \(F = m \times a = 1200 \times 3.5 = 4200\ \text{N}\)
  3. ✓ (c) \(m = F \div a = 6000 \div 30 = 200\ \text{kg}\)

Let students attempt all three, then reveal

Booklet p.16 · Q2.3 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension

We do Extension — the rocket problem

Two laws at once

A rocket expels gas downward with a force of 500 000 N. The rocket's mass is 20 000 kg. Find its acceleration and state which of Newton's laws are involved.

  1. 1 Use \(a = F \div m = 500\,000 \div 20\,000\).
  2. 2 \(a = 25\ \text{m/s}^2\), directed upward.
  3. 3 Second Law gives the size of the acceleration from the net force and mass.
  4. ✓ Third Law too: the rocket pushes gas down, so the gas pushes the rocket up — that's the thrust.

Reveal step-by-step · note how the Second and Third Laws work together

Booklet p.16 · §2.3

Concept · Third Law

Newton's Third Law — action & reaction

Definition

For every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force. The pair always acts on different objects, so they do not cancel each other out.

Booklet p.16 · Q2.4

Your turn

You do Name the reaction force

Name it, size it, direct it

A swimmer pushes backward against the water with a force of 80 N. Describe the reaction force: name it, state its size, and state its direction.

  1. 1 Name: the force of the water on the swimmer.
  2. 2 Size: equal to the action — \(80\ \text{N}\).
  3. ✓ Direction: opposite — forward, pushing the swimmer ahead.
Check

Why don't these two 80 N forces cancel and leave the swimmer still?

Year 8 extension · connecting forward

We do The laws in the trebuchet launch

Bringing the three laws together

During launch, the trebuchet arm exerts a net force of 8 N on a 0.04 kg projectile. (a) Find the projectile's acceleration. (b) Name the reaction force. (c) Once it leaves the sling, what happens to its motion?

  1. 1 (a) 2nd Law: \(a = F \div m = 8 \div 0.04 = 200\ \text{m/s}^2\).
  2. 2 (b) 3rd Law: the projectile pushes back on the sling/arm with \(8\ \text{N}\), in the opposite direction.
  3. ✓ (c) 1st Law: in flight the only force is gravity — horizontally there's no force, so its horizontal speed stays constant.

All three laws in one launch — the bridge to the projectile & trebuchet investigation

Section 2 · Recap

What we covered

  • First Law: objects keep doing what they're doing unless an unbalanced force acts — that's inertia
  • Second Law: \(F = ma\) — bigger force means bigger acceleration; bigger mass means smaller acceleration
  • Third Law: every action force has an equal and opposite reaction on a different object
  • The three laws work together — as in the rocket and the trebuchet launch
Exit question

A 2 kg ball is pushed with a net force of 10 N. What is its acceleration — and which law did you just use?
