Part B · Theory

Naming forces and drawing arrows

The forces we use
  • Weight (\(W\)) — gravity; always straight down
  • Normal force (\(N\)) — a surface pushing back
  • Friction (\(f\)) — surfaces rubbing; opposite to the motion
  • Air resistance — air pushing back; opposite to the motion
  • Tension (\(T\)) — a rope or string pulling
  • Applied force — a person or machine pushing or pulling
Three rules for every force arrow
  1. Start the arrow on the object.
  2. Point it the way the force acts.
  3. Make the length match the size — a 20 N arrow is twice as long as a 10 N arrow.
Worked Example 2.1

Using a scale of 1 cm = 5 N, how long should the arrow be for a 25 N push?

  1. 1 Length = force ÷ scale: \(25 \div 5\)
  2. ✓ 5 cm long

Part B · Theory

Free body diagrams

A free body diagram shows every force on one object. Draw the object as a simple box, then add a labelled arrow for each force, using the three rules.

Worked Example 2.2

A book with a weight of 10 N rests on a table. Draw its free body diagram.

  1. 1 Find every force: gravity pulls the book down (weight); the table pushes it up (normal force).
  2. 2 Size each force: the book isn’t moving up or down, so the push up equals the pull down — both 10 N.
  3. ✓ A box, a 10 N arrow down labelled W, and an arrow the same length up labelled N — like the left-hand diagram.

Part B · Theory

I do Adding up forces — the net force

Net force

The net force is the single force left over when you add up all the forces.

  • Same direction → add
  • Opposite directions → subtract; the net force points the way of the bigger one
  • Net force zero → balanced → the motion doesn’t change
  • Net force not zero → unbalanced → speeds up, slows down or turns
Worked Example 2.3

A sledge is pulled forwards with 45 N. Friction pushes back with 18 N. Find the net force and describe the motion.

  1. 1 The forces point in opposite directions, so subtract.
  2. 2 \(45 - 18 = 27\ \text{N}\) forwards.
  3. ✓ The net force is not zero, so the forces are unbalanced — the sledge speeds up.

Part C · Practice

You do Try these, then check your working

Using a scale of 1 cm = 4 N, how long should the arrows be for (a) 12 N and (b) 30 N?

(a) \(12 \div 4 = 3\ \text{cm}\)

(b) \(30 \div 4 = 7.5\ \text{cm}\)

3 cm and 7.5 cm

An arrow is 4.5 cm long. The scale is 1 cm = 10 N. What force does it show?

Force = length × scale: \(4.5 \times 10\)

45 N

Two dogs pull a sled forwards, one with 120 N and one with 90 N. Find the net force. Balanced or unbalanced?

Same direction, so add: \(120 + 90 = 210\ \text{N}\)

210 N forwards — unbalanced

A cart is pushed right with 65 N. Friction pushes left with 20 N. Find the net force. What happens to the cart?

Opposite directions, so subtract: \(65 - 20 = 45\ \text{N}\)

45 N to the right — unbalanced, so it speeds up

Part C · Practice

You do Size, direction — and what happens next

A vase weighs 12 N and sits still on a shelf. How big is the normal force from the shelf? Explain.

The vase isn’t moving up or down, so the forces are balanced.

12 N upwards — the same size as its weight

In a tug-of-war, the left team pulls with 750 N and the right team with 680 N. Find the net force. Who is winning?

\(750 - 680 = 70\ \text{N}\)

70 N to the left — the left team is winning

A crate has three forces on it: 25 N right, 15 N right and 50 N left. Find the net force.

Right: \(25 + 15 = 40\ \text{N}\)

Then: \(50 - 40 = 10\ \text{N}\)

10 N to the left

A parachutist weighs 800 N. Just after the parachute opens, air resistance is 1100 N. Find the net force. What happens to her speed?

\(1100 - 800 = 300\ \text{N}\) upwards

The net force acts upwards, against her motion, so she slows down.

Part D · Above level

We do Going further

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: judge someone’s reasoning, and use net force in a formula.

Ben says: “A bowling ball rolling down a smooth lane at a steady speed must have a forward force pushing it.” Is Ben right?

A steady speed in a straight line means the motion is not changing.

So the net force must be zero — nothing has to push it forwards.

Ben is wrong. It keeps rolling because nothing big is slowing it down.

A 1000 kg car has a driving force of 3500 N. Air resistance and friction add up to 1500 N. Find the net force, then use \(a = F \div m\) to find its acceleration.

Net force: \(3500 - 1500 = 2000\ \text{N}\) forwards

\(a = 2000 \div 1000 = 2\ \text{m/s}^2\)

2000 N; 2 m/s² forwards

Lesson 2 · Recap

What we covered

  • Every force has a name: weight, normal force, friction, air resistance, tension, applied force
  • Force arrows start on the object, point the way the force acts, and are drawn to scale
  • A free body diagram shows every force on one object
  • Net force: add forces in the same direction, subtract opposite ones
  • Balanced → no change in motion. Unbalanced → speeds up, slows down or turns
Exit question

A book sits still on a table. Are the forces on it balanced or unbalanced? How do you know?

AnswerBalanced. The book isn’t moving, so its motion isn’t changing: the table pushes up (normal force) exactly as hard as gravity pulls down (weight).
