Part B · Theory

Naming forces and drawing arrows

The forces we use
  • Weight (\(W\)) — gravity, always straight down
  • Normal force (\(N\)) — a surface pushing back
  • Friction (\(f\)) — opposite to the motion
  • Air resistance — opposite to the motion
  • Tension (\(T\)) — along a rope or string
  • Applied force — a push or pull from a person or machine
Three rules for every force arrow
  1. Start the arrow on the object.
  2. Point it the way the force acts.
  3. Length matches size — a 20 N arrow is twice as long as a 10 N arrow.
Worked Example 2.1

Using 1 cm = 5 N, how long is the arrow for a 25 N push?  25 ÷ 5 = 5 cm

Part B · Theory

Free body diagrams

Definition

A free body diagram shows every force on one object as a labelled arrow. Arrow length is proportional to the size of the force — a 20 N arrow is twice as long as a 10 N arrow.

Part B · Work through with the class

I do Worked example — net force

Net force

Same direction: add. Opposite directions: subtract — the net force points the way of the bigger force. Zero net force means balanced; anything else is unbalanced.

Find the net force and the motion

A sledge is pulled forwards with 45 N. Friction pushes back with 18 N. Find the net force and describe the motion.

  1. 1 The forces act in opposite directions, so subtract them.
  2. 2 \(F_{\text{net}} = 45 - 18 = 27\ \text{N}\) forwards.
  3. ✓ The net force is not zero, so the forces are unbalanced and the sledge speeds up.

Part D · Above level

We do Is Sam right?

Judge the reasoning

Sam says: “A hockey puck sliding across ice at a steady speed must have an unbalanced force pushing it forwards.”

  1. 1 Steady speed in a straight line means the motion is not changing.
  2. 2 An unbalanced force would make the puck speed up, slow down or turn — so the net force must be zero.
  3. ✓ Sam is wrong: no forward push is needed. Weight and the normal force balance; on near-frictionless ice the puck keeps gliding.

Booklet p.15 · Part D · Extension 2.14

Part D · Above level

You do Predict the motion of the car

Net force, motion, and how to balance it

A car has a driving force of 4500 N. Friction and air resistance total 1200 N. Find the net force, say which way the car accelerates, and suggest one way the forces could become balanced.

  1. 1 Opposite directions, so subtract: \(4500 - 1200\).
  2. 2 \(F_{\text{net}} = 3300\ \text{N}\) forwards — unbalanced, so the car speeds up.
  3. ✓ Balanced if the driving force drops to 1200 N, or the resistance rises to 4500 N.

Lesson 2 · Recap

What we covered

  • Every force has a name: weight, normal force, friction, air resistance, tension, applied force
  • Force arrows start on the object, point the way the force acts, and are drawn to scale
  • A free body diagram shows every force on one object
  • Net force: add forces in the same direction, subtract opposite ones
  • Balanced → no change in motion; unbalanced → speeds up, slows down or turns
Exit question

A book sits still on a table. Are the forces on it balanced or unbalanced? How do you know?
