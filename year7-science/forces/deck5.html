Starter

Why is it harder to get a full shopping trolley moving than an empty one, even if you push just as hard?

AnswerThe full trolley has more mass. The same push gives a bigger mass a smaller acceleration — it speeds up more slowly.

Part B · Theory

What is acceleration?

Acceleration is how quickly an object’s speed changes. It is measured in metres per second squared (m/s²) — which means “metres per second, every second”.

3 m/s² means…

the speed goes up by 3 m/s every second. Starting from rest:

Time (s)0123
Speed (m/s)0369

Slowing down is an acceleration too. It is sometimes called a deceleration.

Quick check

A bike starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s². How fast is it going after 4 seconds?

AnswerIts speed goes up by 2 m/s every second: \(4 \times 2 = 8\ \text{m/s}\).

Newton’s Second Law

If you…the acceleration…
increase the force (same mass)increases
increase the mass (same force)decreases
double the force (same mass)doubles
double the mass (same force)halves
Second Law

\(F = m \times a\)   →   \(a = \dfrac{F}{m}\)   and   \(m = \dfrac{F}{a}\)

Force in N · mass in kg · acceleration in m/s²

Part B · Theory

I do Using \(F = ma\) — four steps

Every \(F = ma\) question
  1. Write what you know, with units.
  2. Choose the version of the formula you need (use the triangle).
  3. Substitute the numbers.
  4. Calculate — and write the unit.
Worked Example 5.1

A 5 kg trolley is pushed with a net force of 20 N. What is its acceleration?

  1. 1 Know: \(F = 20\ \text{N}\), \(m = 5\ \text{kg}\)
  2. 2 Formula: \(a = F \div m\)
  3. ✓ \(a = 20 \div 5 = 4\ \text{m/s}^2\)

Booklet pp.42–43 · §5.3 · Worked Examples 5.2–5.3

Part B · Theory

I do Finding force and finding mass

Worked Example 5.2

A 1200 kg car accelerates at 2 m/s². What net force is acting on it?

  1. 1 Know: \(m = 1200\ \text{kg}\), \(a = 2\ \text{m/s}^2\)
  2. 2 Formula: \(F = m \times a\)
  3. ✓ \(F = 1200 \times 2 = 2400\ \text{N}\)
Worked Example 5.3

A net force of 60 N gives a box an acceleration of 4 m/s². What is the mass of the box?

  1. 1 Know: \(F = 60\ \text{N}\), \(a = 4\ \text{m/s}^2\)
  2. 2 Formula: \(m = F \div a\)
  3. ✓ \(m = 60 \div 4 = 15\ \text{kg}\)

Booklet p.43 · §5.4 · Worked Example 5.4

Part B · Theory

I do Always use the net force

The F is the net force

The \(F\) in \(F = ma\) is the net force — what’s left after you add up all the forces (Lesson 2).

If the forces are balanced, the net force is zero, so the acceleration is zero: the object stays still or keeps a steady speed. That’s the First Law again!

Worked Example 5.4

A 10 kg trolley is pushed forwards with 50 N. Friction pushes back with 10 N. Find its acceleration.

  1. 1 Net force: opposite directions, so subtract: \(50 - 10 = 40\ \text{N}\) forwards
  2. ✓ \(a = F \div m = 40 \div 10 = 4\ \text{m/s}^2\) forwards

Part C · Practice

You do Net force first

A 20 kg go-kart is pushed with 70 N. Friction pushes back with 10 N. Find (a) the net force (b) the acceleration.

(a) \(70 - 10 = 60\ \text{N}\) forwards

(b) \(a = 60 \div 20 = 3\ \text{m/s}^2\)

60 N; 3 m/s²

A 4 kg trolley is pushed with net forces of 8 N, 16 N and 24 N. Find each acceleration. What is the pattern?

\(8 \div 4 = 2\),  \(16 \div 4 = 4\),  \(24 \div 4 = 6\ \text{m/s}^2\)

Each extra 8 N adds 2 m/s² — double the force, double the acceleration.

A 1000 kg car drives at a steady 50 km/h on a flat road. The engine gives a driving force of 1800 N. (a) What is its acceleration? (b) How big are the resistive forces?

(a) Zero — its speed isn’t changing.

(b) Zero acceleration means zero net force, so the forces are balanced.

Resistive forces = 1800 N backwards

Part D · Above level

We do Going further

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: judge someone’s reasoning, and use more than one formula.

Zoe pushes a 30 kg box with 90 N, but it doesn’t move. She says: “\(F = ma\) means it should accelerate at 3 m/s².” What has she got wrong? How big is the friction?

Zoe used her push, not the net force.

The box doesn’t move, so its acceleration is zero — the net force must be zero.

Friction balances her push: 90 N.

A lift and its passengers have a mass of 600 kg. The cable pulls up with 6600 N. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. Find (a) the weight (b) the net force (c) the acceleration.

(a) \(W = mg = 600 \times 9.8 = 5880\ \text{N}\)

(b) \(6600 - 5880 = 720\ \text{N}\) upwards

(c) \(a = 720 \div 600 = 1.2\ \text{m/s}^2\) upwards

5880 N; 720 N up; 1.2 m/s² up

Lesson 5 · Recap

What we covered

  • Acceleration is how quickly speed changes, in m/s²
  • More force → more acceleration. More mass → less acceleration
  • \(F = ma\),  \(a = F \div m\),  \(m = F \div a\)
  • Always find the net force first
  • Balanced forces → zero acceleration (the First Law again)
Exit question

A 2 kg ball is pushed with a net force of 10 N. Find its acceleration, and name the law you used.

Answer\(a = F \div m = 10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{m/s}^2\) — Newton’s Second Law.
