Starter question

If you drop a ball and throw another one horizontally at the same moment from the same height — which hits the ground first?

Booklet p.36 · §5.1

Concept · Projectiles

Once launched, only gravity acts

Key principle

After launch, a projectile has no horizontal force — so horizontal speed stays constant. Gravity pulls it downward at \(g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\), curving the path into a parabola. The two directions are independent.

Horizontal and vertical — treated separately

Back to the starter

Equations · Horizontal launch

Three equations — one experiment

The connection

Measure \(h\) with a ruler and \(t\) with a slow-motion video → you can calculate \(g\). If you also measure \(R\) (where the ball lands), you get the launch speed \(v\). This is exactly what Experiment 5 does.

Booklet p.37 · Worked Example 5.1

Work through with the class

I do Worked example — time of flight

How long does the ball take to land?

A ball is launched horizontally from a height of \(h = 1.25\ \text{m}\). How long does it take to hit the ground?

  1. 1 Write the formula: \(h = \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2\), rearranged to \(t = \sqrt{2h \div g}\).
  2. 2 Substitute: \(t = \sqrt{2 \times 1.25 \div 9.8} = \sqrt{0.255}\).
  3. ✓ \(t \approx 0.51\ \text{s}\). The height sets the time — not the horizontal speed.

Booklet p.37 · Task 5.1, row 3

Work through with the class

I do Worked example — calculating g from data

Checking the accepted value of gravity

A student measures \(t = 0.64\ \text{s}\) for a ball dropped from \(h = 2.0\ \text{m}\). Calculate \(g\) and find the percentage error from \(9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\).

  1. 1 Use: \(g = 2h \div t^2 = 2 \times 2.0 \div (0.64)^2\).
  2. 2 \(g = 4.0 \div 0.410 \approx 9.76\ \text{m/s}^2\).
  3. ✓ % error \(= |9.76 - 9.8| \div 9.8 \times 100 \approx 0.4\%\) — very close to accepted.

Small errors in t have a big effect — t is squared

Booklet p.38 · §5.2 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension · Angled launch

Angled launch — optimising range

Year 8 key idea

The range equation \(R = \dfrac{v^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}\) peaks at 45° because \(\sin(2 \times 45^\circ) = \sin 90^\circ = 1\). Higher or lower angles give shorter range. But real trebuchets often aren't set to exactly 45° — think about why.

Booklet p.38 · Q5.1 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension · Worked example

We do Maximum range at 45°

How far can it go?

A ball is launched at \(v = 20\ \text{m/s}\) at the optimal angle of 45°. Calculate the maximum range. Use \(g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\).

  1. 1 At 45°: \(R_{\max} = v^2 \div g\) (because \(\sin 90^\circ = 1\)).
  2. 2 Substitute: \(R = 20^2 \div 9.8 = 400 \div 9.8\).
  3. ✓ \(R \approx 40.8\ \text{m}\). Double the speed → four times the range (the \(v^2\) effect).

Reveal steps — then ask Q5.2: why might 45° not be ideal for a trebuchet?

Booklet p.38 · Q5.2 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension · Discussion

Why a real trebuchet isn't set to exactly 45°

  • Air resistance slows the projectile more at high speeds — a lower angle keeps the ball faster for longer, so a slightly flatter launch (below 45°) often wins.
  • Launch height — the trebuchet releases the ball from several metres above the ground, not from ground level. When the launch point is higher than the landing point, the optimal angle is less than 45°.
  • Sling geometry — the sling releases at a fixed point in the arm's rotation, locking the actual launch angle regardless of intent.
  • Practical optimum — competition trebuchets are typically tuned to around 40–42°, not 45°.
Discuss

If you can only change the counterweight position on your trebuchet, how would you try to adjust the launch angle — and which direction would you move it?

Section 5 · Recap

What we covered

  • A projectile after launch has only gravity acting — horizontal speed stays constant
  • Vertical fall: \(h = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2\) → time of flight depends only on height
  • Horizontal range: \(R = v \times t\) — launch speed × time
  • Measuring gravity: \(g = 2h \div t^2\) — rearranged from the fall equation
  • (Yr 8) Angled range: \(R = \dfrac{v^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}\) — maximum at 45°
  • This is exactly the physics of Experiment 5 and the trebuchet investigation
Exit question

A ball rolls off a table 0.8 m high and lands 1.2 m from the base. Find the time of flight and the launch speed. (Answers: \(t \approx 0.40\ \text{s}\), \(v \approx 3.0\ \text{m/s}\))
