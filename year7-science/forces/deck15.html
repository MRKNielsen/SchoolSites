Investigation booklet p.4 · B1 · How a trebuchet throws

Part B · Theory

A trebuchet is a Class 1 lever

The arm turns on a pivot between the counterweight and the sling.

Investigation booklet p.4 · B1 · One launch, step by step

Part B · Theory

One launch, step by step

  1. 1 The trigger is released. Gravity is now an unbalanced force on the counterweight, so it falls.
  2. 2 As the short arm swings down, the long arm swings up and over the top. The whole arm turns about the pivot.
  3. 3 The sling is dragged along the base, then lifted and whipped around behind the arm.
  4. 4 When the sling has swung far enough, its loop slides off the release pin and the projectile flies free.
  5. 5 In flight, the only forces on the projectile are its weight and air resistance.

Investigation booklet p.5 · B1 · Where the energy goes

Part B · Theory

Where the energy goes

Gravitational PE

The counterweight is raised.

Kinetic energy

The arm, counterweight and sling move.

Kinetic energy

The projectile flies. Some energy is wasted as thermal energy and sound.

Stored energy

\[\text{GPE} = m \times g \times h\]

Same drop \(h\), so double the mass → double the energy.

Why the long arm moves fast

The pivot is close to the counterweight. While the counterweight falls a short way, the tip of the long arm sweeps a long arc in the same time. The trebuchet gives away force to gain speed.

Investigation booklet p.5 · B2 · Measuring the swing with a sensor

Part B · Theory

Measuring the swing with a sensor

The PASCO Wireless 3-Axis Acceleration/Altimeter sensor contains a gyroscope — like the chip that tells a phone it is being turned.

It measures how fast it is turning: its spin rate (angular velocity), in radians per second (rad/s).

How big is a radian per second?

One full turn ≈ 6.3 radians.

So 6.3 rad/s ≈ one full turn every second.

Quick check

A sensor reads 12.6 rad/s. About how many full turns a second is that?

Answer\(12.6 \div 6.3 = 2\) — about two full turns every second.

Investigation booklet p.5 · B2 · Every point on the arm turns together

Part B · Theory

Same spin, different speeds

The arm is rigid: every point turns through the same angle in the same time. So the sensor reads the same spin rate wherever it is strapped.

But points further from the pivot travel bigger circles, so they move faster.

Speed of the tip of the arm

\[v = \omega \times L\]

\(v\) = tip speed (m/s) · \(\omega\) (“omega”) = spin rate (rad/s) · \(L\) = long arm, in metres

Investigation booklet p.6 · B2 · Reading the sensor

Part B · Theory

Reading the sensor graph

  • Three axes, \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\): use the one with the big hump — it lines up with the axle.
  • Hump pointing down? The sensor is just turned the other way. Ignore the minus sign.
  • The dip after the peak is the arm swinging back — ignore it.
  • We record the peak spin: the top of the hump.

Investigation booklet p.6 · Worked Example 1

Part B · Worked example

I do Peak spin and tip speed

Worked Example 1

The graph is from a launch with an 800 g counterweight. The long arm is 0.40 m and the short arm is 0.10 m. Find the peak spin, the speed of the tip, and the speed of the counterweight end of the arm.

  1. 1 Peak spin: the top of the hump is about \(9.0\ \text{rad/s}\)
  2. 2 Tip speed: \(v = \omega \times L = 9.0 \times 0.40 = 3.6\ \text{m/s}\)
  3. 3 Counterweight end: \(v = 9.0 \times 0.10 = 0.9\ \text{m/s}\)
  4. ✓ The tip moves \(3.6 \div 0.9 = 4\) times as fast — the same as \(0.40 \div 0.10 = 4\).

Investigation booklet p.6 · Task 1

Part B · Work through together

We do Tip speed from the spin

Task 1

A launch gives a peak spin of 7.5 rad/s. The long arm is 0.50 m and the short arm is 0.10 m.

(a) Calculate the speed of the tip of the long arm.

(b) Calculate the speed of the counterweight end of the arm.

(c) How many times faster does the tip move than the counterweight end?

Write your working in the Task 1 box in your booklet.

  1. a \(v = 7.5 \times 0.50 = 3.75\ \text{m/s}\) (about 3.8 m/s)
  2. b \(v = 7.5 \times 0.10 = 0.75\ \text{m/s}\)
  3. c \(3.75 \div 0.75 = 5\) times as fast — the same as \(0.50 \div 0.10\)

Investigation booklet p.7 · B3 · Equipment and group roles

Part B · Planning a fair test

Equipment and group roles

Each group gets
  • a desktop trebuchet with a counterweight hanger
  • slotted masses for five counterweights
  • one beanbag — the same one every launch
  • a PASCO sensor and strap, and a tablet or laptop with SPARKvue
  • a measuring tape, a metre ruler, masking tape and a counter
  • safety glasses for everyone
Roles — swap after each counterweight
  • Operator: loads, cocks and releases. The only person beside the trebuchet.
  • Safety officer: checks the range is clear, calls “Launching!” and “All clear”.
  • Spotter: stands well to the side and marks where the projectile first lands.
  • Recorder: runs SPARKvue, reads the peak spin, records both results.

Investigation booklet p.8 · Task 4

Part B · Check your answers

Keeping it fair: compare

Add anything you missed in a different colour.

  1. 1 Sling and pin: the projectile is let go at a different moment, so it flies at a different angle. Never bend the pin; hook the loop on the same way.
  2. 2 Pull-back: further back means a bigger drop — more energy, faster swing. Always set the arm against the same trigger.
  3. 3 Sensor position: the swing changes and a loose sensor misreads. Strap it tightly at one marked spot.
  4. 4 0 m line: every range is out by the same amount. Line the base up with the tape every launch.
  5. + Your own, e.g. which way it points: aimed off the tape, the range is misread. Face it straight down the tape.

Investigation booklet p.9 · B3 · Method

Part B · Method

The method for next lesson

Setting up
  1. Trebuchet on the floor. Masking tape along the front of the base: the 0 m line.
  2. Lay the tape measure straight down the range.
  3. Strap the sensor tightly near the pivot. Swing slowly to check it clears the frame.
  4. Connect in SPARKvue: angular velocity against time.
  5. Measure \(L\), \(h\) and the projectile’s mass.
Launching
  1. Load the smallest counterweight.
  2. Cock the arm, load the pouch, hook the loop on the pin. Hands clear.
  3. Range clear? “Launching!”
  4. Start recording, release, Stop.
  5. Mark where it first lands; measure to the nearest cm.
  6. Read the peak spin. Record both.
  7. Three launches, then the next counterweight.

Safety rules
  • Safety glasses on while launching.
  • Never stand in front of the trebuchet or beside the range.
  • Soft beanbag projectiles only.
  • Collect projectiles only after “All clear”.
  • Hands out of the arm’s path while it is cocked.
  • Change the counterweight only with the arm hanging at rest.

Risk assessment: compare

  1. 1 Projectile: could hit someone, especially in the eye or face. Safety glasses; nobody in front of or beside the range; soft beanbags; collect after “All clear”.
  2. 2 Counterweight: masses could fall on a foot, or fingers could be caught. Secure the masses; change them only with the arm at rest; hands and feet clear.
  3. + Your own, e.g. the tape measure on the floor: someone could trip. Tape it flat; walk around the range, never across it.

Lesson 15 · Recap

What we covered

  • A trebuchet is a Class 1 lever: GPE of the counterweight → kinetic energy of the arm → kinetic energy of the projectile
  • The sensor measures spin rate in rad/s — the same everywhere on the arm
  • Tip speed: \(v = \omega \times L\), with \(L\) in metres
  • We change the counterweight mass, and measure the range and the peak spin
Exit question

A peak spin of 8.0 rad/s on an arm with a 0.45 m long arm. How fast is the tip moving?

Answer\(v = 8.0 \times 0.45 = 3.6\ \text{m/s}\)
