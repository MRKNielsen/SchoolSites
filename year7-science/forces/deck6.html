Check after reading

Why does pressing two surfaces together harder make the friction bigger?

AnswerThe tiny bumps on each surface push further into each other, so they catch more and resist sliding more. Adding mass to our block presses it harder on the bench.

Fair test

VariableWhat it means
Independentthe one thing you change on purpose

Worked Example 6.1

A student rolls a toy car down a ramp set at different heights, and measures how far it travels across the floor. Identify the variables.

  1. 1 Independent (changed): the height of the ramp.
  2. 2 Dependent (measured): the distance the car travels.
  3. ✓ Controlled (kept the same): the same car, the same floor, letting go without a push.

Booklet p.66 · Part B · Equipment and Figure E6.1

Part B · Planning the experiment

Friction and mass: the set-up

Equipment

Wooden block with a hook · spring balance (the spring scale from Lesson 1) or force sensor · 200 g masses (five, to make 1 kg)

Why a steady speed?

At a steady speed the forces are balanced, so your pull equals the friction — the spring balance reading is the friction force.

Booklet p.66 · Part B · Prediction and method

Part B · Planning the experiment

Prediction and method

Predict first

What will happen to the friction force as you add more mass to the block? Write your prediction and a reason in your booklet before you start.

Safety

Pull gently and keep the spring balance in line with the block — an overstretched balance can snap back. Stack the masses so they can’t slide off, and keep feet clear of the bench edge.

Method
  1. Place the wooden block on the bench top.
  2. Hook the spring balance onto the block.
  3. Pull slowly so the block keeps moving at a steady speed. Read the force — that is the friction. Record it.
  4. Repeat step 3 twice more, so you have three results.
  5. Add a 200 g mass on top of the block. Measure the friction three times.
  6. Repeat with 400, 600, 800 and 1000 g on the block, three results each.

Booklet p.68 · Part C · Worked Example 6.2

Part C · Results and analysis

I do Calculating a mean

Every trial is a little different. The mean (average) gives one value we can trust more than any single trial.

How to find a mean

Add all the trials, then divide by how many trials there are.

Worked Example 6.2

With no mass added, three results were 0.8 N, 1.0 N and 0.9 N. Find the mean.

  1. 1 Add the results: \(0.8 + 1.0 + 0.9 = 2.7\)
  2. 2 Divide by the number of results: \(2.7 \div 3\)
  3. ✓ Mean friction with no mass added = 0.9 N

With 400 g on the block, a group measured 1.9 N, 2.1 N and 2.3 N. Calculate the mean friction force.

Part D · Above level

We do Going further

These go beyond the Year 7 standard: interpret a graph, predict, and use a formula.

Another group’s heavier block had 1.4 N of friction with no mass added, not 0.9 N. Why does their graph start higher?

A heavier block presses harder on the bench, even with nothing stacked on it.

Its bumps catch more, so there is more friction at 0 g.

A group repeats the experiment on smooth plastic instead of the bench. Predict how their graph compares with yours.

Smooth plastic has smaller bumps, so there is less friction at every mass.

Their line is lower, and it rises less steeply.

A 0.25 kg block carries 0.75 kg of masses, so the total is 1.0 kg. While it slides, friction of 3.0 N is the only sideways force on it. Use \(a = F \div m\) to find how quickly it slows down.

\(a = F \div m = 3.0 \div 1.0 = 3\ \text{m/s}^2\)

It slows down by 3 m/s every second.

Lesson 6 · Recap

What we covered

  • A fair test changes one variable, measures one, and keeps the rest the same
  • At a steady speed, the spring balance reading equals the friction force
  • More mass presses the block harder on the bench, so the bumps catch more: more friction
  • Friction went up steadily as mass was added, so the line graph is a straight line
  • Repeat each test and take the mean — leave out a result that clearly doesn’t fit
Exit question

Name the independent and dependent variables in today’s experiment.

AnswerIndependent: the mass added to the block. Dependent: the friction force (the spring balance reading).
