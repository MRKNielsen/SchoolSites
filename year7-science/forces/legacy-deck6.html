Your investigation

The task
  • Launch a projectile from your trebuchet and film it

Booklet p.45 · Q6.2 (Yr 8)

Year 8 extension · Worked example

We do From counterweight to launch speed

Energy through the whole launch

A 15 kg counterweight drops 0.8 m. Assume 25% of the energy is lost to friction and sound. Find (i) the GPE, (ii) the KE given to the 0.04 kg projectile, and (iii) its launch speed.

  1. 1 (i) \(\text{GPE} = mgh = 15 \times 9.8 \times 0.8 = 117.6\ \text{J}\).
  2. 2 (ii) 25% lost → 75% kept: \(\text{KE} = 0.75 \times 117.6 = 88.2\ \text{J}\).
  3. 3 (iii) \(v = \sqrt{2 \times \text{KE} \div m} = \sqrt{2 \times 88.2 \div 0.04} = \sqrt{4410}\).
  4. ✓ Launch speed \(v \approx 66\ \text{m/s}\) (an idealised maximum — real launches lose more).

Booklet p.45 · §6.2

Planning · Step 1

Aim and hypothesis

Aim

To measure the acceleration due to gravity (\(g\)) by launching a projectile from a trebuchet and analysing its flight.

Hypothesis — Year 7 scaffold
  • “By launching from height \(h = \underline{\qquad}\ \text{m}\), I will measure a time of flight of about \(\underline{\qquad}\ \text{s}\), giving \(g \approx \underline{\qquad}\ \text{m/s}^2\), because…”
Hypothesis — Year 8
  • Write a full hypothesis: a testable prediction, the scientific principle (\(h = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2\)), and an expected numerical outcome.

Booklet pp.45–46 · §6.2 · Variables

Planning · Step 2

Variables

Independent

The trial number (repeat launches) — or vary the launch height \(h\) to test that \(g\) stays consistent.

Dependent

The time of flight \(t\) (from the video) — and the value of \(g\) you calculate from it.

Controlled

Projectile mass, release mechanism, counterweight, camera frame rate, launch & landing surface.

Check

Why must the projectile mass be kept identical for every group and every trial?

Booklet pp.45–46 · §6.2 · Risk assessment

Planning · Step 3

Risk assessment

Projectile strike

Medium. Soft projectiles only, safety glasses, 1 m exclusion zone, never aim at people.

Moving arm

Medium. One operator, others stand behind, fully reset and lock the arm before reloading.

Trip hazards

Low. Tape down the measuring tape, keep the launch lane clear, retrieve only on “all clear”.

Teacher note

Teacher supervises every launch. Full equipment and setup are on the Trebuchet lab tech sheet.

Method

Running the investigation

Goal

10 launches, filmed side-on, to calculate \(g = 2h \div t^2\) and find the mean.

Set up
  1. Level the trebuchet; plumb-line the release point to the floor and mark 0 m.
  2. Measure the launch height \(h\) (fixed for all trials).
Each launch
  1. Launch; film side-on in slow motion (≥ 120 fps).
  2. Read the range \(R\); count video frames for the time \(t\).
  3. Repeat for 10 launches.
Then
  • Calculate \(g = 2h \div t^2\) for each trial.
  • Flag anomalies; find the mean of the valid trials.

Booklet p.47 · §6.3

Results · Calculating g

From measurements to gravity

Record carefully

Run 10 trials in a table: \(R\), frames, \(t\), \(t^2\), \(g\), and an anomaly flag. Because \(t\) is squared, a small timing error has a big effect on \(g\) — so accurate frame counting matters most.

Booklet p.47 · §6.3–6.4

Analysis · Year 8

Percentage error and uncertainty

Percentage error

\(|g - 9.8| \div 9.8 \times 100\%\)

How close your mean is to the accepted value.

Uncertainty

\((g_{\max} - g_{\min}) \div 2\)

The spread of your valid results: \(g = \text{mean} \pm \text{uncertainty}\).

Interpreting your data

Were most values close to 9.8, or scattered? A small percentage error shows accuracy; a small uncertainty shows precision — they're not the same thing.

Booklet pp.47–48 · §6.4 · Sources of error

Analysis · Evaluating

Where the errors come from

Random errors
  • Frame counting for \(t\) — reduce with a higher frame rate
  • Inconsistent release — use a fixed mechanism
  • Reduce their effect by repeating and averaging
Systematic errors
  • Air resistance — makes \(g\) appear low; use a dense projectile
  • Camera not side-on — align it square to the flight
  • These shift every reading the same way
Year 7 vs Year 8

Year 7: identify TWO sources and an improvement each. Year 8: FOUR sources, with the direction each pushes your value of \(g\).

Booklet p.50 · §6.5

Writing up · Conclusion

We do Writing your conclusion

Structure your conclusion around the evidence

Use your data to evaluate the aim — don't just restate it. Build the paragraph from these parts:

  1. 1 State the mean \(g\) you measured, with its uncertainty.
  2. 2 Compare to \(9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\) using your percentage error.
  3. 3 Judge whether this supports your hypothesis, and why.
  4. ✓ Identify the biggest source of error and one improvement you would make.

Year 8: also link your result to Newton's Second Law (\(F = ma\) with gravity)
