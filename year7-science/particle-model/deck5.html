The Dancing Grains

Before you read

In 1827 Robert Brown saw tiny grains in water jiggling about without stopping. Were they alive? Predict, then read to find out.

Read The Dancing Grains in your booklet. Then answer questions C1–C8.

Check after reading

Why does the grain move in a zigzag rather than in a straight line?

AnswerWater particles hit it from every side in random directions. At any moment a few more hit one side than the other, and the side that gets hit more keeps changing — so the grain is shoved this way, then that way.

Part B · Theory

I do Temperature and particle speed

Heating and cooling

Heating gives particles more kinetic energy: they move (or vibrate) faster.

Cooling takes kinetic energy away: they move (or vibrate) more slowly.

Temperature measures how fast the particles are moving on average.

Worked Example 5.1

A metal spoon is left standing in a cup of hot soup. Describe what happens to the particles in the spoon.

  1. 1 The spoon gains energy from the soup, so its particles gain kinetic energy.
  2. 2 They stay in their fixed places — it is still a solid — but they vibrate faster.
  3. ✓ The spoon’s temperature rises.

We do 5.1

A balloon is taken from a warm room out into the cold night air. Describe what happens to the particles of air inside it.

Write your working in the We do 5.1 box in your booklet.

  1. 1 The air inside loses energy to the cold air, so its particles lose kinetic energy.
  2. 2 They are still a gas — far apart, moving in random directions — but they move more slowly.
  3. ✓ The temperature of the air inside the balloon falls.

Booklet p.43 · §5.2 · Worked Example 5.2

Part B · Theory

I do Brownian motion

Worked Example 5.2

Through a microscope, a speck of smoke in air is seen jiggling about. Explain what causes this.

  1. 1 Air is made of particles moving fast in random directions. They collide with the speck from every side.
  2. 2 When more particles hit one side than the other, the speck is pushed a little.
  3. ✓ The direction keeps changing, so the speck jiggles in a zigzag.

Booklet p.44 · We do 5.2

Part B · Work through together

We do Jiggling chalk dust

We do 5.2

A student watches tiny grains of chalk dust floating in water through a microscope. The grains jiggle about. When the water is warmed, they jiggle faster. Explain both observations.

Write your working in the We do 5.2 box in your booklet.

  1. 1 Jiggling: water particles move fast in random directions and collide with each grain unevenly, shoving it one way then another.
  2. 2 Warmed: the water particles gain kinetic energy and move faster.
  3. ✓ Faster particles hit the grains harder and more often, so the grains jiggle faster.

I do Moving particles explain everyday things

Worked Example 5.3

Someone opens a packet of chips at the front of the room. A minute later you can smell them at the back. Explain how.

  1. 1 Particles that carry the smell escape from the chips into the air.
  2. 2 Air particles and smell particles are all moving fast in random directions.
  3. ✓ The smell particles keep getting knocked about and spread out until some reach your nose.

Part D · Above level

We do Going further — speed and temperature

These go beyond the Year 7 standard. Predict, and use numbers.

Scientists often measure temperature in kelvin, where K = °C + 273. Water boils at 100 °C. What is that in kelvin? What is 0 K in °C?

\(100 + 273 = 373\) K

\(0 - 273 = -273\) °C

373 K; and 0 K is −273 °C, the coldest possible temperature.

A car’s tyres are checked on a cold morning, then again after a long drive. The air pressure inside is higher after the drive. Explain using particle speed.

The drive warms the tyres, so the air particles inside gain kinetic energy and move faster.

Faster particles hit the inside of the tyre harder and more often, so the pressure goes up.

Lesson 5 · Recap

What we covered

  • Particles are always moving; heating and cooling change how fast
  • Heating gives particles more kinetic energy; cooling takes it away
  • Temperature measures how fast the particles are moving on average
  • Brownian motion — grains jiggling in a zigzag — is evidence that particles are real and moving
Exit question

A puddle sits in the sun on a cold morning. As the sun warms it, what happens to the water particles?

AnswerThey gain kinetic energy from the sun, so they move faster — the same particles, just moving faster. The puddle’s temperature rises.
