Part B · Theory

The four ideas of the particle model

The particle model
  1. All matter is made of tiny particles.
  2. The particles are always moving.
  3. The particles attract each other.
  4. There is empty space between the particles.
Check

What is in the empty space between particles?

AnswerNothing at all — not even air. Air is made of particles too, so it can’t fill the gaps.

Part B · Theory

I do Describing particles

Worked Example 4.1

Describe the particles in a block of ice. Mention their arrangement, their movement and the attraction between them.

  1. 1 Ice is a solid. Its particles are packed closely in a regular pattern.
  2. 2 They vibrate in fixed places but can’t move past each other.
  3. ✓ The attraction between them is strong, which holds them in place.

Booklet p.36 · §4.3 · Worked Example 4.2

Part B · Theory

I do Writing a particle explanation

Property, then particles

Start with the property: “A solid keeps its shape …”

Then give the particle reason: “… because its particles are held in fixed places by strong attractions.”

Worked Example 4.2

A sealed syringe full of air can be pushed in. Explain this using the particle model.

  1. 1 Property: the air can be squashed into a smaller volume.
  2. ✓ Particle reason: gas particles are far apart, with lots of empty space between them. Pushing the plunger moves them closer together.

I do Why does a liquid flow?

Worked Example 4.3

Honey can be poured from a jar. Explain this using the particle model.

  1. 1 Property: a liquid flows and takes the shape of its container.
  2. ✓ Particle reason: liquid particles are close together but not held in fixed places, so they can slide past each other.

Part D · Above level

We do Going further — stretching the model

These go beyond the Year 7 standard. Use the model to explain something new.

When 1 L of liquid nitrogen boils, it becomes about 700 L of gas. Use the particle model to explain why the volume grows so much, even though the number of particles stays the same.

The particles themselves don’t change. In the liquid they are touching; in the gas they are far apart, with lots of empty space between them.

The same particles spread out with empty space between them, so the gas takes up far more room.

Two gases mix with each other within minutes, but two solids pressed together don’t mix. Explain using particles.

Gas particles move fast in random directions, with space between them, so they spread into each other.

Solid particles only vibrate in fixed places, so they can’t move into each other.

Lesson 4 · Recap

What we covered

  • All matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving, attract each other, and have empty space between them
  • Solid: packed in a pattern, vibrate in place, strong attraction
  • Liquid: close but no pattern, slide past each other
  • Gas: far apart, move fast in random directions, almost no attraction
  • A particle explanation links a property to what the particles are doing
Exit question

Describe the particles in the air inside a balloon, and use them to explain why you can squash the balloon.

AnswerThe air is a gas: its particles are far apart, moving fast in random directions, with almost no attraction. There is lots of empty space between them, so squeezing pushes them closer together.
