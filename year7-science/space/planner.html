Year 7 Science · Unit planning document

Unit: Space

6 core lessons over 3 weeks, plus 1 optional lesson and 3 above-level extension lessons · Victorian Curriculum F–10 v2.0 · Year 7 core with above-level extension

Core activities link to our own unit pages unless marked Stile. The week grouping matches the unit index, so the planner and the site never disagree. Lesson 4 (Reading the Rocks) is optional — the unit is coherent without it, and it is not assessed. Week 4 sits above the Year 7 level — the core three weeks are complete without it. Lesson 7 gathers the unit's Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander astronomy in one place. No practical or equipment-dependent activities are listed — everything here runs from the decks, the booklet and Stile.

Week1
  • Our place in space
  • Scale, and why the sky repeats itself
  • Earth, Moon & Sun
  • Day, night & seasons
  • Lesson 1 — Describe the relative sizes of Earth, Moon and Sun; explain Earth's layered structure; calculate light travel times.
  • I can rank Earth, Moon and Sun by size, name Earth's four layers, and work out how long light takes to travel between them.
  • Lesson 2 — Distinguish rotation from revolution; describe how axial tilt causes seasons; identify solstices and equinoxes.
  • I can explain why Australia has summer in December.
  • Lesson 1 · Earth, Moon & Sun — entry task: rank objects by size; You do: light-travel-time calculations
  • Lesson 2 · Day, Night & Seasons — day/night diagram, booklet p.8; opens with the spaced retrieval warm-up
  • Student Booklet §1 (pp. 3–7) and §2.1–§2.3 (pp. 8–10)
  • Support: the Earth and Sun structure tasks in §1 are label-the-diagram rather than blank recall; the Stile Check-in: Seasons re-teaches at a slower pace.
  • Extend: daylight-hour comparisons between Melbourne and the poles; ask what would change if the tilt were 0° or 45°.
  • Stile: Introduction — Journey to the Sun
  • Stile: 0.2 Day and night
  • Stile: 2.1 Seasons · 2.3 Daylight hours
  • Formative: Booklet §1 and §2.1–§2.3; the Lesson 1 retrieval quiz that opens Lesson 2.
  • Stile check-ins: Seasons, Daylight hours.
  • Exit pass — name Earth's four layers and explain what causes seasons.
Week2
  • The Moon, and what it leaves behind
  • then out into the solar system
  • Moon phases & tides
  • Reading the Rocks (optional)
  • Our Solar System
  • Lesson 3 — Explain what causes the Moon's phases; sequence the eight phases; explain how the Moon and Sun cause tides; distinguish spring from neap tides.
  • I can name the eight phases in order and tell spring tides from neap tides using the positions of Sun, Earth and Moon.
  • Lesson 4 (optional) — Explain how a layered rock records the tides; interpret the Elatina data table; explain tidal braking; describe the evidence that dates the Acraman impact.
  • I can state what one lamina in a rhythmite represents, and name two rock features only an impact produces.
  • Lesson 5 — Order the 8 planets; classify terrestrial and giant planets; describe dwarf planets, asteroids and comets; use the astronomical unit.
  • I can list the planets in order from memory and use AU to compare distances.
  • Lesson 3 · The Moon — Phases & Tides — the interactive phase visualiser (Southern Hemisphere by default), eclipses, spring and neap tides
  • Lesson 4 · Reading the Rocks — optional. Runs from the deck's photographs and the Elatina data table: what a rhythmite records, tidal braking, and the evidence dating the Acraman impact. Booklet p.17.
  • Lesson 5 · Our Solar System — planet-order entry task (booklet p.20), AU distances, the asteroid and Kuiper belts
  • Student Booklet §2.4–§2.5 (pp. 11–13), §4 (pp. 20–21), and §3 (pp. 14–19) only if you run Lesson 4
  • Support: phases are taught from the visualiser before the diagram; planet mnemonics are supplied on the slide. Stile 1.5 Skill builder — Rounding underpins the AU work.
  • Extend (Lesson 4): the recession-rate question — the rocks put the Moon only about 3.7% closer at 635 Ma, so the teaching point is the rate (~2 cm/yr average against 3.8 cm/yr today). They constrain the orbit; they do not on their own prove the giant-impact origin.
  • Lesson 4 itself — the whole lesson is optional; skip it and the sequence still runs
  • Stile: 3.1 The phases of the Moon · 3.2 Eclipses · 3.4 Tides
  • Stile: 1.1 The Solar System · 1.6 Changing models of the Solar System
  • Stile: 1.5 Skill builder — Rounding · 3.5 Moons of Jupiter and Saturn
  • Formative: Booklet §2.4–§2.5 and §4; retrieval quizzes (Lessons 1–2, then 1–3).
  • Stile check-ins: The phases of the Moon, Eclipses, Tides, The Solar System, Changing models of the Solar System.
  • If you run Lesson 4: booklet p.17 is worth collecting — but §3 is not assessed in the written task.
Week3
  • What we send up
  • and tens of thousands of years of observation on Country
  • Space technology & sustainability
  • Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander astronomy
  • Unit assessment
  • Lesson 6 · Part 1 — Outline key milestones in space exploration; describe crewed, robotic and telescope missions; explain Australia's contributions; evaluate the benefits and risks.
  • Lesson 6 · Part 2 — Define space debris and how it accumulates; explain the Kessler syndrome; describe Earth observation; evaluate the sustainability of orbital activity.
  • Lesson 7 — Explain what dark constellations are; describe the Boorong star catalogue and why it is scientifically significant; explain sky knowledge as ecological calendar and as navigation; recognise it as science developed over 65,000 years.
  • I can describe dark constellations using the Emu in the Sky, and give an example of Aboriginal knowledge and Western science working together.
  • Lesson 6 · Space Technology & Sustainability — two parts. Milestone card sort (booklet p.22); debris kinetic-energy calculation (p.24); You do: Europa mission design brief, then a debris-management design for the Australian Space Agency.
  • Lesson 7 · Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander Astronomy — tracing the Emu in the Sky (booklet p.28), the Boorong catalogue, seasonal calendars, celestial navigation, the SKA on Wajarri Country, two-way learning

Week4
  • Above level — extension
  • The search for life
  • Gravity & orbits
  • Stars, galaxies & starlight
  • Lesson 8 — Describe the conditions needed for life; explain the habitable zone; describe promising targets in our solar system; explain how exoplanets are detected.
  • Lesson 9 — State Newton's law of universal gravitation; explain what an orbit is and why orbiting objects don't fall; relate altitude to orbital speed and period.
  • Lesson 10 · Parts 1–2 — Light-years, star types and stellar life cycles, galaxies; then the electromagnetic spectrum, telescopes and spectroscopy.
  • Success criteria for all three sit above the Year 7 standard — treat them as stretch, not as a pass/fail line.
  • Lesson 8 · The Search for Life — conditions-for-life sort (booklet p.33); You do: build a scientific argument for life in Europa's subsurface ocean
  • Lesson 9 · Gravity & Orbits — Newton's cannonball thought experiment (p.39); comparing gravitational forces; geostationary orbit
  • Lesson 10 · Stars, Galaxies & Starlight — light-year scaling table (p.42); ordering the EM spectrum (p.44); choosing the right telescope for the job
